# Product Launch Plan

## Purpose
A coordinated product launch maximizes awareness, trial, and early adoption by orchestrating messaging, channels, and stakeholders around launch day. It builds anticipation beforehand, creates momentum on launch day, and sustains engagement afterward. Uncoordinated launches miss audience attention, dilute messaging, and fail to convert curiosity into adoption. This skill ensures your launch is systematic, measurable, and builds momentum that carries through critical early adoption phase.

## When to Use
- Launching new product, major feature, or significant product tier
- Entering new market segment or geographic region
- Rebranding or repositioning existing product
- Major pricing or packaging change with customer-facing impact
- Coordinating across multiple internal teams (marketing, sales, product, CS) and potentially external partners
- Seeking to maximize awareness and early adoption velocity
- Launching something that benefits from hype and concentrated attention
- **Do NOT use when**: Rolling out internal-only features (no customer launch needed), deploying small bug fixes or minor updates (continuous delivery), launching with no marketing budget or team capacity, or when success doesn't depend on concentrated attention (evergreen product, low-stakes change)

## Instructions

### Step 1: Define Launch Objectives & Success Metrics
Establish what success looks like before planning:
- **Launch objectives** (pick 2-3 primary):
  - Awareness: Reach X% of target audience
  - Trial adoption: Get X new users/accounts in first 30 days
  - Sales impact: Generate X new leads or X revenue in first month
  - Market positioning: Establish perception as "first to market" or "category leader" in segment
  - Competitive response: Differentiate from competitor launch or announcement
  - User engagement: Achieve X% activation rate or X feature adoption among new users

- **Success metrics** (quantifiable, measurable, tracked daily during launch week):
  - Awareness metrics: Website traffic, impressions, reach, share of voice vs. competitors
  - Trial metrics: New user signups, trial starts, free accounts created
  - Sales metrics: New leads, lead quality (MQL conversion rate), revenue influenced
  - Engagement metrics: Activation rate (% doing X action), feature adoption, NPS of new users
  - Social metrics: Mentions, engagement rate, sentiment, follower growth
  - Press/coverage metrics: Articles published, reach, quality of outlets

- **Baseline & targets**: Establish pre-launch baseline for each metric; set realistic 30-day targets
  - Website traffic baseline: 10,000/week; target: 25,000/week during launch week, settle to 15,000/week by week 4
  - New signups baseline: 100/week; target: 500 during launch week, 200/week for month after
  - Sales leads baseline: 20/week; target: 100 in launch week, 50/week for month after

### Step 2: Develop Messaging Framework & Positioning
Create clear, differentiated messaging across audiences:
- **Core positioning statement** (one sentence, most defensible claim):
  - "We're the first [solution type] built specifically for [segment]"
  - "[Product name] lets [audience] [outcome] in [time frame]"
  - Example: "Warp is the only terminal built for teams, turning hours of debugging into minutes"

- **Messaging pillars** (3-4 core benefits/reasons to care):
  1. **Primary pillar**: Strongest differentiator or most urgent customer problem solved
  2. **Secondary pillar**: Secondary benefit or use case
  3. **Tertiary pillar**: Proof of viability (user count, investor backing, industry recognition)
  4. **Emotional pillar**: Brand values, vision, or aspirational message

- **Messaging by audience**:
  | Audience | Primary message | Why they care | Supporting proof | CTA |
  |----------|-----------------|---------------|------------------|-----|
  | Existing customers | Expansion capability, new workflow | Does more of what they love | Roadmap/feature announcement | Upgrade/try feature |
  | Prospect segment A | Problem solved, time saved | Directly addresses their pain | Case study, metric | Free trial |
  | Prospect segment B | Competitive advantage | Beats alternatives | Comparison, speed metric | Request demo |
  | Media/analysts | Market shift, category creation | News angle, authority story | Positioning, unique insight | Interview, coverage |
  | Employees/partners | Company growth, exciting future | Pride, opportunity | Vision, roadmap | Share externally |

- **Proof points** (evidence supporting claims):
  - Customer testimonials or case studies (strongest)
  - Performance metrics (faster, cheaper, more reliable)
  - Independent validation (analyst reports, awards, press mentions)
  - User count or adoption velocity ("used by X companies")
  - Team credentials (founder background, advisor credibility)

- **Objection/messaging guard rails** (common concerns and rebuttals):
  | Objection | Rebuttal | Why true |
  |-----------|----------|----------|
  | "I've never heard of you" | "We're new, which is why we're launching with X differentiation" | Transparency + differentiation |
  | "Why should I switch?" | "Switch cost is X; ROI from Y benefit is Z months" | Quantified value |
  | "Is this mature enough?" | "Built by team from [credible background]; trusted by [credible early customers]" | Proof of traction |

### Step 3: Create Launch Timeline (Pre-Launch, Launch Day, Post-Launch)
Develop detailed timeline with milestones and responsible parties:

**Pre-launch phase** (8-12 weeks before):
- **Weeks 1-2**: Define objectives, messaging, positioning (marketing + product leadership)
- **Weeks 2-3**: Identify launch partners, influencers, press contacts to reach out to (marketing)
- **Weeks 3-4**: Finalize creative assets (landing page, video, graphics, sales deck); prepare email templates (creative/design)
- **Weeks 4-6**: Begin seeding awareness—tease on social, launch waitlist or early access program (marketing)
- **Weeks 6-8**: Send press releases to journalists; brief analysts; reach out to influencers for embargo reviews (PR)
- **Weeks 8-10**: Prepare sales enablement (battle cards, pitch deck, talking points, objection handling) (sales)
- **Weeks 10-12**: Final QA, team training, all creative and messaging finalized, social calendar locked (all teams)
- **Week 12 (launch week - 1)**: Go into quiet period; no new announcements to let media/influencer coverage take stage

**Launch day & launch week**:
- **T-1 (day before)**: Team all-hands; review messaging; final tech checks; customer support briefed
- **T-0 early morning**: Soft-launch to existing customers via email (warm audience, early adopters)
- **T-0 9am ET** (your timezone): Deploy all launch assets simultaneously
  - Website/product live with banner
  - Social posts go out (pre-scheduled across all platforms)
  - Press release distributed to journalists
  - Influencer embargo lifts; early reviews go live
  - Email blast to prospect list goes out
  - Sales team begins cold outreach with launch angle
  - Ads launch on Google, LinkedIn, etc.
  - Webinar or live demo event goes live
- **T-0 through T+3**: Team monitoring
  - Twitter/social engagement; respond to early questions in <1 hour
  - Website performance; monitor for crashes, high traffic
  - Sales inbound; respond to demo requests same-day
  - Support volume; any urgent issues
  - Dashboard showing launches metrics update hourly

**Post-launch phase** (4 weeks after):
- **Days 1-7**: Ride momentum; respond to feedback; ship quick wins if bugs emerge; share user wins on social
- **Week 2**: Analyze early user feedback; publish case studies or success stories from early adopters
- **Week 3**: Host webinar or community event; gather testimonials from early users
- **Week 4**: Publish detailed retrospective; case study; think piece on market implications
- **Month 2-3**: Transition from launch to sustained growth marketing; continue messaging in regular cadence

### Step 4: Build Channel & Messaging Matrix
Define what message goes where and when:

**Channel matrix** (reach, timing, frequency):
| Channel | Audience | Message angle | Format | Timing | Frequency |
|---------|----------|---------------|--------|--------|-----------|
| Email (existing customers) | Current users | Expansion feature benefit | Benefit-focused, CTA | T-0 9am | 1 email |
| Email (prospects) | Qualified leads, waitlist | Problem solved, trial CTA | Feature + benefit, clear CTA | T-0 10am | 2 emails (day 0 + day 2 reminder) |
| Twitter/X | Broad tech/business audience | Announcement, news angle, conversational | Thread or single tweets, link to launch post | T-0 9am + daily for week 1 | 1-2 daily |
| LinkedIn | Professionals, B2B buyers | Positioning + credibility | Professional tone, leader voice, CTA | T-0 10am | 3-5 posts week 1 |
| Website | All visitors | Benefit, differentiation, proof | Hero section, feature descriptions, testimonials, pricing | T-0 9am live | Static until month 2 |
| Product Hunt | Tech-savvy early adopters | Community, social proof, transparency | List with narrative + engagement | Day 1 at 9am PST (best launch window) | Daily engagement week 1 |
| Press/news | Business/tech journalists | News angle, unique insight | Press release, personalized pitches, expert availability | T-1 (embargo) | Outreach T-1, articles publish T-0+ |
| Influencers/partners | Their audiences | Credibility, unique angle (their perspective) | Reviews, endorsements, tutorials | T-0 embargo lifts | Partner-dependent |
| Paid ads | Prospects matching ICP | Problem + solution, benefit-focused CTAs | Google Ads, LinkedIn ads, retargeting | T-0 through month 1 | Daily spend budget X |
| Sales team | Enterprise/mid-market prospects | Customized, competitive, ROI-focused | Personal outreach, one-pagers, demo offers | T-0+ | Prospecting + inbound |
| Webinar/event | Registered audience | Interactive demo, expert insights, Q&A | Live session, recording available after | Week 1 or 2 | 1-2 events |

### Step 5: Prepare Launch Assets & Creative
Create all marketing materials before launch week:

**Minimum asset checklist**:
- Website landing page (hero section, benefit sections, feature showcase, pricing/trial CTA, testimonials, FAQ)
- Email template (subject line tested, body benefits-focused, clear CTA, mobile-responsive)
- Social media graphics (3-5 variations for different platforms; include video preview if applicable)
- Twitter/LinkedIn announcement post (hook in first 5 words; long-form thought or thread)
- 30-second explainer video (shows problem, solution, benefit; can be product screencast with voiceover)
- Press release (headline, quote from founder, product benefits, link to launch)
- Sales one-pager (problem, solution, proof, competitive positioning, trial/demo CTA)
- Product Hunt listing (title, tagline, description, background story, call-out)
- Pitch deck for sales/partnerships (8-12 slides: problem, solution, market opportunity, traction, ask)

**Creative best practices**:
- Visuals: Show before/after, problem/solution contrast, or transformation
- Copy: Lead with benefit, not feature; use specific numbers ("30% faster" not "faster")
- CTAs: Clear, action-oriented, personalized to audience ("Start free trial," "Request demo," "Join 500+ users")
- Video: Hook in first 2 seconds; fast pacing; captions for social viewing without sound
- Product Hunt: Name + tagline that works as one sentence; tagline < 60 characters

### Step 6: Coordinate Stakeholder Alignment & Training
Ensure all teams understand messaging and their role:

**Marketing team**:
- Understands full positioning and messaging matrix
- Has access to all asset templates and can customize for specific channels
- Clear on timing (which assets go live when)
- Responsible for social monitoring and responding to engagement during launch week
- Has list of journalists/influencers to monitor for coverage

**Sales team**:
- Trained on positioning, competitive positioning, objection handling
- Has one-pager and pitch deck; knows talking points
- Clear on inbound process (who handles demo requests, SLA for response)
- Sales leader owns follow-up cadence and team accountability
- Briefed on expected lead volume and quality to set expectations

**Product/CS team**:
- Understands why this matters to customers
- Prepared for increased support volume; escalation process defined
- Knows what early adopter feedback to surface to product team
- CS team equipped with talking points for customer conversations about launch
- Can identify and reach out to strong early adopter candidates for case studies/testimonials

**Executive/founder**:
- Clear on messaging and key positioning points for any media interviews
- Available for webinar/launch event (or recorded message)
- Knows which influencers/press to reach out to personally if needed
- Comfortable being the "face" of launch for social amplification

**Alignment process**:
- Weekly check-in 4 weeks before launch (review timeline, resolve blockers)
- Bi-weekly check-in 2 weeks before (creative review, messaging refinement)
- Daily 1-hour stand-up during launch week (monitor metrics, respond to issues, celebrate wins)

### Step 7: Measure, Learn & Sustain Momentum
Track results and iterate in real-time:

**Launch week metrics dashboard** (updated daily):
- Website traffic (vs. baseline), conversion rate to trial/demo
- Email metrics (open rate, CTR, signup rate)
- Social metrics (impressions, engagement rate, follower growth, reach, share of voice)
- Sales metrics (inbound leads, demo requests, qualified leads)
- Product metrics (new user signups, activation rate, feature adoption)
- Press/coverage (articles published, reach, sentiment)
- Overall: Launch week vs. baseline (+X% traffic, +X% signups, +X leads)

**Post-launch iteration**:
- Day 3: Review initial metrics; identify what's working (amplify) and what isn't (adjust)
- Day 7: Publish early traction post ("X companies signed up in first week"); share wins on social
- Week 2: Gather early user feedback; publish case study or testimonial
- Week 3: Host community event or webinar; extend launch momentum
- Week 4: Transition from launch intensity to sustained growth; document learnings

**Documentation & learnings**:
- What worked (which channels, messages, assets drove most impact)?
- What didn't work (low-performing channels, messaging, assets)?
- Surprises (unexpected response, audience, channel success)?
- For next launch: What would you do differently?

## Quality Gates

1. **Clear objectives**: Launch has 2-3 primary success metrics; every team member can articulate what success looks like
2. **Unified messaging**: Core positioning statement is clear; messaging matrix ensures consistent but tailored message across channels; team can recite positioning
3. **Complete timeline**: Pre-launch, launch day, and post-launch phases defined with specific milestones and owners; nothing is assumed to "just happen"
4. **Channel coverage**: Multi-channel approach covers owned (email, website), earned (press, influencer), shared (social), and paid (ads); channels chosen based on audience location
5. **Asset readiness**: All creative assets completed and approved 1 week before launch; tested for technical issues (links, loading, responsiveness)
6. **Team alignment**: All stakeholders (marketing, sales, product, CS) have attended training; understand messaging, role, and launch-week process
7. **Measurement plan**: Dashboard defined to track launch metrics daily; baseline established; team knows what to watch and how to respond

## Examples

### Good Example: B2B SaaS Feature Launch (AI Assistant)

**Objective**: Launch AI-powered feature to existing customers (revenue expansion), acquire 500 new free trials, establish category leadership in "AI for teams"

**Core positioning**: "First AI assistant built for team productivity, not individual tasks"

**Timeline**:
- Week -4: Messaging finalized; existing customer advisory group briefed for feedback
- Week -2: Landing page, email sequences, and social calendar locked; sales deck completed
- Week -1: Press outreach (embargo lifts at T-0); influencer seeding for reviews
- T-0 9am: Website updates with feature banner; email sent to current 10,000 customers; Product Hunt launch; ads go live
- T-0 10am: Twitter announcement thread; LinkedIn posts from leadership team
- T+1: Early adopter testimonials collected; published on website
- T+4: Case study published; webinar scheduled for week 2
- T+28: Transition to sustained marketing; document learnings

**Channels & messaging**:
| Channel | Message | Timing |
|---------|---------|--------|
| Email (existing) | "Your AI assistant is here—30% of teams activate day 1" | T-0 9am |
| Email (prospects) | "See how leading teams use AI for productivity" | T-0 10am |
| Twitter | "We shipped AI for teams. Made it 10x smarter than single-user tools" | T-0 10am daily |
| Product Hunt | "AI assistant built for team workflows, not isolated tasks" | T-0 |
| Ads | "Try our AI—used by X teams to save hours per week" | T-0 through month 1 |
| Sales | "Land AI feature as expansion into existing customers; competitive positioning vs. [competitor]" | T-0+ |

**Success metrics**:
- 500 new free trials in week 1 (baseline: 100/week)
- 3,000 feature activations among existing customers (baseline: 0)
- 50 qualified sales leads from new trial signups
- 10,000+ impressions on Twitter launch thread
- 5+ press mentions in tier-2 tech publications

**Result**: Exceeded targets: 650 trials, 4,200 activations, 75 leads, 12 press mentions, 15,000 Twitter impressions. Established "AI for teams" category positioning.

### Bad Example: Uncoordinated Feature Release

**Process**: Product team ships feature; no coordinated announcement; each team (marketing, sales) figures out their own approach
- Marketing publishes blog post 3 days after product release (missed awareness window)
- Sales doesn't mention feature for 2 weeks (misses early expansion opportunity)
- Email goes out once; generic subject line ("New feature released"); low open rate
- No messaging alignment; sales talks about "speed," marketing emphasizes "simplicity," customers confused about what actually changed
- No measurement plan; unclear if feature drove any business value
- Support team surprised by questions about feature; no training in advance
- No post-launch momentum; feature languishes at 10% adoption despite being valuable

**Result**: Feature launched; barely noticed; adoption flat; business impact unclear; team questions whether effort was worth it

## Common Mistakes

1. **Over-assumption on awareness**: Assuming existing customers and your audience know about launch and will adopt without you telling them. Most won't hear about it without active outreach. Solution: Over-communicate pre-launch; assume you need to reach each segment 3-5 times through different channels to ensure awareness.

2. **Messaging for yourself, not audience**: Messaging focused on what you're proud of (technical achievement, innovation) rather than what the customer cares about (problem solved, value delivered, time/money saved). Solution: Every message should open with customer benefit ("Saves your team 5 hours/week") before saying what it is or how it works.

3. **Launching without press plan**: Assuming organic social and email will drive awareness; not reaching out to journalists/influencers until launch day. PR lift takes 1-2 weeks to materialize; early planning needed. Solution: Begin press outreach 6 weeks pre-launch; offer exclusives to top outlets; brief analysts; reach out to influencers in your space 4 weeks before.

4. **Sales not enabled**: Sales team learns about launch same time as customers; doesn't have talking points, competitive positioning, or battle cards. They can't confidently position to prospects. Solution: Complete sales enablement 2 weeks before launch; provide one-pagers, pitch deck, objection handling; do practice demos.

5. **Single-channel launch**: Concentrating all effort on one or two channels (e.g., Twitter and blog) when different segments consume information differently. SMBs might check LinkedIn; enterprises rely on email and analysts; agencies discover through YouTube. Solution: Multi-channel approach; tailor message to each channel; coordinate timing so you hit audience where they are.

6. **No post-launch follow-up**: Treating launch day as the finish line; assuming awareness and trial leads to adoption without support or education. Users sign up, then abandon when they don't see immediate value. Solution: Plan post-launch engagement (email onboarding sequences, webinars, early adopter support) as seriously as launch day itself; aim to activate 40% of trial users within first week.

## Anti-Patterns

1. **The False Consensus Effect**: Assuming your team's excitement about launch means audience is equally excited. Team has been working on this for months; audiences are seeing it for the first time and have competing priorities. Your launch is not as important to them as it is to you. Solution: Over-communicate externally; treat audiences as having limited attention span; create FOMO and urgency ("limited time," "early pricing").

2. **The Feature Trap**: Messaging around technical features or capabilities rather than customer outcomes. "Machine learning powered" sounds smart but customers care about "finds issues automatically." Solution: Translate every feature to benefit ("This model does X" → "You save Y hours because of X"); lead with benefit, mention feature as evidence.

3. **The Timing Crunch**: Planning launch timeline without buffer; everything scheduled to complete "just in time." When inevitable delays happen (creative not ready, sales deck revisions, product issues), everything gets rushed and quality suffers. Solution: Plan 12 weeks before launch; complete all assets 2 weeks early; final week is for monitoring and buffer only.

4. **The Team Siloing**: Marketing plans launch independently; sales prepares independently; product handles their own rollout. Teams execute in parallel but don't align on timing or messaging. Launch day is chaos with conflicting messages and missed handoffs. Solution: Weekly alignment meetings 4 weeks before; daily stand-up during launch week; shared dashboard showing real-time metrics.

5. **The Follow-the-Leader Launch**: Copying competitor's launch approach (timing, channels, messaging) assuming it will work for you. Customers are experiencing same messaging from both of you; you blend into the noise. Solution: Study competitors but differentiate; find unique angle (different audience segment, different proof point, different positioning, different channels).

6. **The One-Off Mentality**: Launching, then moving on to next thing; not documenting what worked, what didn't, or learnings for next time. Each launch starts from scratch rather than improving on last. Solution: Publish detailed retrospective; document metrics, learning, insights; create playbook for future launches; improve each time.
