# Habit Tracker Setup

## Purpose

Habits fail because they're isolated from daily life, lack clear triggers and rewards, and progress is invisible. This skill walks you through BJ Fogg's proven behavior model (cue-routine-reward), habit stacking (anchoring new habits to existing ones), explicit implementation intentions ("when X, then Y"), and a tracking system that makes success visible. You'll exit with 1-3 new habits wired into your daily routine and a simple tracker that keeps you honest.

## When to Use

- You want to build a new behavior (exercise, writing, meditation, reading)
- You've tried a habit before and it fizzled after 2-4 weeks
- You need to attach a new habit to existing routines
- You're not sure when or how to trigger the behavior
- You want visible progress, not just "I did it today"
- **Do NOT use when**: The behavior is a one-time event (like "apply to college"), you're trying to quit a habit (use a different skill), or you're under 16 years old (consult an adult first)

## Instructions

### Step 1: Choose Your Habit (Atomic, Not Ambitious)

Common failure: "Get fit" or "Write a novel." Too large.

Your habit should be:
- **Atomic**: Completable in 2-10 minutes max
- **Unmistakable**: No ambiguity about whether you did it ("write 500 words" beats "work on my book")
- **Linked to an existing routine**: You'll anchor it to something you already do

Examples of good habits:
- 5 push-ups after brushing teeth
- Write 100 words of journal after morning coffee
- Meditate for 3 minutes before checking email
- Review tomorrow's calendar for 2 minutes before bed

Start with one habit. Multi-habit launches (e.g., exercise + meditation + journaling at once) have a 95% failure rate.

### Step 2: Identify the Cue (Anchor to Existing Behavior)

Your cue is the trigger—the moment you'll perform the habit. The best cues are **existing behaviors** because you already do them consistently.

**Habit Stacking Formula**: "After I [EXISTING HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]."

Examples:
- After I pour my coffee, I will do 10 push-ups (takes 2 min)
- After I close my laptop at 9pm, I will write 3 things I'm grateful for
- After I finish lunch, I will take a 5-minute walk
- After I get home from work, I will change into workout clothes (not the full workout yet, just the clothes)

The existing habit must be rock-solid. Don't anchor to something you're trying to build (yet). Use well-established behaviors:
- Brushing teeth
- Morning coffee
- Checking email
- Eating lunch
- Bedtime routine
- Commute start/end

**Environmental Setup**: Remove friction. If your habit is "write 100 words," have a notebook and pen on the breakfast table. If it's "meditate," have a cushion in the corner, unrolled, waiting. Friction kills habits.

### Step 3: Define the Routine (Keep It Tiny)

Write the exact sequence of steps. Specificity matters.

**Example: Morning Writing Habit**

Cue: After I pour coffee, I will write.

Routine:
1. Sit at kitchen table (not moving between rooms adds friction)
2. Open the blank Google Doc titled "Morning Pages"
3. Set a 10-minute timer
4. Write until timer sounds (no editing, no pausing)

That's it. Not "write well" or "write productively." Just write. The form doesn't matter yet.

**Why tiny?**: The smallest version of your habit has the highest survival rate. You can always exceed it. "100 words" beats "write a chapter." "5 push-ups" beats "30-minute workout."

### Step 4: Attach a Reward (The Closing Ritual)

This is critical. Rewards wire the habit into your brain by triggering dopamine. Without them, habits don't stick past week 2.

**Reward types:**
- **Immediate sensory**: Cold glass of water, piece of dark chocolate, 30 seconds of your favorite song
- **Progress signal**: Checking off a box, adding a tally mark, saying "one done" aloud
- **Social**: Text a friend, post to a group, high-five someone
- **Emotional**: Write "done" in all caps, feel the pride, pause to notice the win

The reward must happen immediately after, within 10 seconds. Your brain wires the cue → routine → reward sequence tightly.

**Example rewards**:
- After 5 push-ups: drink a glass of cold water
- After writing 100 words: check off the day on a wall calendar (instant visual progress)
- After meditation: write the date and "✓" in a habit tracker
- After evening walk: text a friend "day 5 done"

Avoid rewards that take >30 seconds or undermine the habit (eating cake after exercise doesn't work).

### Step 5: Craft Implementation Intentions

Write out the full habit loop as an "if-then" statement:

**Format**: "After [CUE], I [ROUTINE], then I [REWARD]."

Examples:
- "After I pour morning coffee, I sit and write 100 words, then I check off today on my wall calendar and drink cold water."
- "After I finish lunch, I do 10 push-ups at my desk, then I say 'one done' aloud and take three deep breaths."
- "After I brush my teeth, I meditate for 3 minutes on the couch, then I write today's date in my habit journal."

Write this statement and read it aloud. It should feel specific and doable. If it feels like a stretch, simplify.

### Step 6: Build Your Tracking System

Visible progress sustains motivation. Choose one:

**Option A: Wall Calendar (Best for streaks)**
- Buy a large monthly calendar or print one
- Hang it in a visible location (bathroom, kitchen, home office)
- Each day you complete the habit, mark an X or checkmark
- Goal: don't break the chain (the longer the X streak, the more motivation to continue)

**Option B: Spreadsheet (Best for data)**
- Create a simple grid: date, habit, done (Y/N), notes
- Review weekly to spot patterns
- Add other columns: energy level, mood, obstacles

**Option C: Habit Tracker App**
- Streaks, Habitica, Done, or similar
- Get daily reminders and streak visibility
- Less tactile than wall calendar but portable

**Option D: Notebook Tally**
- Each day, write the date and a tally mark (IIII)
- Simple, no devices
- Look back monthly to see total count

My recommendation: Start with a wall calendar (Option A). Physical, visible, streak-based, no app login required.

### Step 7: Plan for Week 1-4 (The Critical Window)

**Days 1-3: Novelty Phase**
- The habit is exciting and new
- Track daily but don't overthink it
- Expect to execute the routine 2-3 times easily

**Days 4-7: Reality Phase**
- Novelty fades
- You'll "forget" or feel resistance
- This is normal and not a sign of failure
- If you skip a day, do the habit the next day (don't break streak with guilt)

**Days 8-21: Boring Phase**
- Motivation is low; habit isn't automated yet
- Tracking keeps you accountable
- This is when most habits die
- Protect the cue: don't change your morning routine, don't skip the existing habit you're anchoring to

**Days 22+: Integration Phase**
- Habit starts to feel normal
- Reward is less critical
- You can begin to add volume (e.g., 5 push-ups → 10, or 100 words → 200)

Plan in advance what you'll do during the boring phase: a motivational text from a friend, a reminder on your phone, or a specific checkup conversation with a partner.

### Step 8: Review and Adjust (Weekly Check-In)

Every 7 days, ask:
- Did I execute the habit ≥5 days this week?
- Did I miss because I forgot the cue, or because I skipped the existing routine?
- Does the routine still feel manageable, or was it too hard?
- Is the reward working (am I looking forward to it)?

Adjustments:
- **Too hard?** Make routine even tinier (3 push-ups instead of 5)
- **Too boring?** Change the reward or make the tracking more visual
- **Too easy by week 3?** Add volume slowly (e.g., week 3 = 5 reps, week 4 = 8 reps)
- **Forgot the cue?** Make the cue more visible (note on coffee maker, alarm on phone 10 seconds before)

## Quality Gates (5+)

1. **Cue Solid**: Is the cue an existing habit you do ≥90% of days? (If you anchor to something flaky, habit fails.)
2. **Routine Atomic**: Can you complete the routine in under 10 minutes? (Smaller = higher success rate.)
3. **Reward Immediate**: Does the reward happen within 10 seconds of the routine? (Delayed rewards don't wire the habit.)
4. **Environment Prepped**: Have you removed friction? (Notebook on table, not in a drawer.)
5. **Tracking Visible**: Can you see progress daily without opening an app? (Visibility sustains motivation.)
6. **No Multi-Launch**: Are you starting only ONE habit, not three at once? (Multi-habit launches fail 95% of the time.)

**Routine**:
1. Sit at kitchen table
2. Open blank Google Doc
3. Set 10-minute timer
4. Write (no editing)

**Reward**: Drink cold water, check off calendar

**Tracking**: Wall calendar, X per day

### Bad Habit (Vague, No Reward, No Tracking)

**Habit**: "Get fit"

**Cue**: Whenever I feel like it

**Routine**: Work out

**Reward**: None

Problems:
- "Get fit" is not atomic; it's too vague
- "Whenever I feel like it" is not a cue; it's procrastination
- No specific routine; too much room for excuses
- No reward; behavior won't wire
- No tracking; can't see progress

## Common Mistakes (3+)

1. **Too Ambitious**: Starting with "30-minute workout" instead of "5 push-ups." The tiny version succeeds; the ambitious version fails. Build volume later.

2. **No Cue**: "I'll meditate every morning" without anchoring to an existing routine. Intentions without cues have ~5% follow-through. Anchor to coffee, shower, breakfast.

3. **Reward Delayed**: Planning a weekend reward for daily habits. Dopamine hits must be immediate (<10 seconds). Weekend reward is too far away to wire the daily loop.

4. **Tracking Invisible**: Using an app you don't open. Physical trackers (wall calendar, journal) you see daily work better. Out of sight = out of mind.

5. **Changing the Cue**: You anchor to morning coffee, then you skip breakfast one day. The cue disappears. Protect your cue; build the habit into a non-negotiable routine.

6. **Multi-Launch Failure**: Starting exercise + meditation + journaling + reading all at once. You'll drop 2-3 within a week. One habit at a time.

## Anti-Patterns (3+)

1. **Reward That Undermines Goal**: Rewarding exercise with fast food or meditation with phone scrolling. The reward should reinforce, not contradict, the habit's purpose. Immediate physical/emotional reward is best.

2. **Motivation Dependence**: "I'll start when I'm more motivated." Motivation follows action, not the reverse. Cue + routine + reward *creates* motivation. Start with tiny routine today, not "tomorrow when I'm ready."

3. **Perfection Paralysis**: You miss one day and think "I broke the chain," so you quit. One miss is not failure. Return the next day and rebuild. Streaks are nice but not sacred.

4. **Tracking Obsession**: You track the habit but ignore all context. If you hit 90% compliance but feel miserable, something's wrong (routine too hard, cue unstable, reward weak). Adjust based on feel, not just numbers.

5. **Ignore the Boring Phase**: Week 2-3 is when 80% of habits die. Plan for it explicitly. Text a friend, see a therapist, or schedule a check-in with a coach. Acknowledge that this phase is normal and temporary.
