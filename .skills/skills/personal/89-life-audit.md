# Life Audit

## Purpose

You optimize your work but neglect your health. You invest in relationships but ignore finances. You drift across years without knowing if you're satisfied or just busy. This skill provides a comprehensive audit across six major life domains, scores satisfaction honestly, identifies gaps between where you are and where you want to be, and builds a concrete 90-day action plan to realign. You'll exit with clarity on your life trajectory and 2-3 concrete changes to begin immediately.

## When to Use

- You feel generally dissatisfied but can't name why
- You're contemplating a major life change (career pivot, relocation, relationship shift)
- It's been >1 year since you explicitly reflected on life direction
- You're high-achieving in one area (career) but neglecting others (relationships, health)
- You want to ensure you're living aligned with your values
- You're between chapters (post-graduation, after leaving a job, post-breakup)
- **Do NOT use when**: You're in acute crisis (seek support first), you just did a major life audit (wait 12 months), or you lack basic stability (secure housing, safety)

## Instructions

### Step 1: Define the Six Life Domains

These domains cover human wellbeing:

1. **Career & Work**: Job satisfaction, growth, learning, income, impact, alignment with values
2. **Relationships**: Romantic partnership (if applicable), family, friendships, community, social connection
3. **Health & Fitness**: Physical health, energy, fitness, nutrition, sleep, medical care
4. **Finances**: Income, savings, investments, debt, financial security, freedom
5. **Growth & Learning**: Skill development, reading, courses, creative pursuits, intellectual stimulation
6. **Fun & Recreation**: Hobbies, travel, adventure, play, joy, leisure

You can add domains (spirituality, contribution/volunteering) if they're central to your values.

### Step 2: Score Satisfaction Honestly

For each domain, rate your satisfaction on a scale of 1–10:

```
1-3:  Unsatisfied / problematic / needs urgent attention
4-6:  Mixed / could be better / acceptable but not ideal
7-8:  Good / satisfied / aligned
9-10: Excellent / thriving / couldn't ask for more
```

**Scoring rules**:
- Be honest, not aspirational. (Not "I should be satisfied," but "Am I actually satisfied?")
- Score based on current state, not compared to others. (Your standard, not Instagram's standard.)
- If you don't have a category (no romantic partner), score "not applicable" and focus on categories you do have.

Example scores:
- Career: 7 (good job, learning, but limited advancement)
- Relationships: 5 (close to family, could invest more in friendships)
- Health: 4 (know I should exercise more, sleep is inconsistent)
- Finances: 6 (stable income, high expenses, not saving enough)
- Growth: 6 (read occasionally, want to learn more)
- Fun: 5 (work-heavy, limited hobbies)

**Common pattern**: High scores in 2-3 domains (usually career + 1 other), low scores in the rest. This is normal but unsustainable.

### Step 3: Visualize and Analyze the Gap

Create a **satisfaction wheel**:

Draw a circle divided into 6 sections (one per domain). On a scale from center (1) to edge (10), shade each section based on your score.

```
        Career
        /    \
   Relationships  Growth
       |    |
       |    |
   Health  Fun
        \  /
      Finances
```

A balanced wheel (all scores 6+) is rare. Most wheels are lumpy—peaks and valleys.

**Gap analysis**: For each domain where you scored <6:

1. **Name the gap**: What's not working? Be specific.
   - Career 5: "Limited growth path in current role"
   - Health 4: "Inconsistent exercise, poor sleep"
   - Relationships 5: "Close to family, distant from friends"

2. **Identify root causes**: Why is this a gap?
   - Career: No learning budget, no mentor, small company
   - Health: No structured habit, irregular schedule
   - Relationships: Work-heavy, friends scattered geographically

3. **Impact**: If you don't address this, what happens in 5 years?
   - Career: Stagnation, boredom, lower earnings
   - Health: Chronic fatigue, illness, reduced quality of life
   - Relationships: Isolation, loneliness, weaker support network

### Step 4: Clarify Values and Non-Negotiables

Before setting goals, clarify what matters most.

Ask: "If I'm 80 and looking back, which domains matter most to my sense of a life well-lived?"

Most people say: relationships (family, close friends), health (being able to move and engage), meaningful work, and some form of growth or contribution. Career advancement and finances matter, but usually as means to other ends (security, autonomy, time with loved ones).

**Non-negotiables**: What are you unwilling to compromise on?

- "I won't sacrifice health for career advancement"
- "I won't move if it destroys my relationships"
- "I need 10 hours/week for creative pursuits or I feel empty"

Write these down. They'll inform your action plan.

### Step 5: Set 90-Day Targets

For each domain scoring <6, define a realistic 90-day target:

```
Domain: [NAME]
Current: [SCORE]
Target (90 days): [SCORE or specific outcome]
Rationale: [Why this matters]

Actions (3 concrete steps):
1. [Specific action with timeline]
2. [Specific action with timeline]
3. [Specific action with timeline]

Success metric: [How you'll know it's working]
```

**Rules for targets**:
- Small, specific wins (not "fix my life")
- Achievable in 90 days without heroics
- Address root cause, not symptom
- Link to values and non-negotiables

**Examples**:

**Career (5 → 7)**
- Current: Limited growth, feeling stagnant
- Target: Enroll in one learning program, schedule quarterly check-ins with mentor
- Actions:
  1. Identify 3 learning courses (AWS, leadership, writing) by end of week 1
  2. Enroll in one course by end of week 2
  3. Find a mentor and schedule first call by end of month
- Success: Complete course, have 2+ mentor sessions

**Health (4 → 6)**
- Current: Inconsistent exercise, poor sleep
- Target: 3x/week exercise routine, consistent 10pm bedtime
- Actions:
  1. Choose one form of exercise (running, yoga, gym) by end of week 1
  2. Schedule 3x/week into calendar by end of week 2 (habit stack to existing routine)
  3. Set bedtime reminder for 10pm by end of week 1
- Success: ≥80% adherence to both commitments

**Relationships (5 → 7)**
- Current: Close to family, distant from friends
- Target: Monthly dinner with 2-3 close friends, weekly family call
- Actions:
  1. Text 5 friends with specific dinner date by end of week 1
  2. Schedule recurring calendar blocks (Mon 7pm family call, Thu 6pm friend dinner)
  3. Rotate who I reach out to (don't let one person carry the relationship)
- Success: 3+ dinners booked, family call happens 3+ times

### Step 6: Identify Interconnections and Leverage Points

Domains aren't isolated. Improving one often improves others.

**Example**: Exercising (health) + doing it with a friend (relationships) + learning about nutrition (growth) = three domains improved with one habit.

**Leverage points**: Which changes would cascade into other improvements?

- Better sleep (health) → more energy for work (career) and social time (relationships)
- Learning new skill (growth) → more confidence (career) and interesting conversations (relationships)
- Financial stability (finances) → reduced stress (health) and time for fun (recreation)

Identify 1-2 leverage points and prioritize them in your action plan.

### Step 7: Create Your 90-Day Plan

Consolidate all actions into a coherent 90-day plan:

```
# 90-Day Life Realignment Plan

**Current State** (Wheel snapshot)
- Career: 5, Relationships: 5, Health: 4, Finances: 6, Growth: 6, Fun: 4

**Target State (90 Days)**
- Career: 7, Relationships: 7, Health: 6, Finances: 6, Growth: 7, Fun: 6

**Guiding Values**
- Meaningful relationships
- Health and energy
- Continuous learning
- Financial autonomy

**Non-Negotiables**
- 10 hours/week minimum for friends and family
- 4x/week exercise (non-negotiable for energy)
- 1 learning project per quarter

## 90-Day Action Plan

### Phase 1: Foundation (Days 1-30)
[ Goals and specific actions for week 1-4 ]

### Phase 2: Build (Days 31-60)
[ Goals and specific actions for week 5-8 ]

### Phase 3: Deepen (Days 61-90)
[ Goals and specific actions for week 9-12 ]

## Success Metrics
- [ ] Career: Completed 1 course + 2 mentor sessions
- [ ] Health: 80% adherence to exercise and sleep
- [ ] Relationships: 3 friend dinners + weekly family calls
- [ ] Growth: Finished one book
- [ ] Fun: 2 new hobbies explored

## Review Cadence
- Weekly check-in (Sunday): Are you on track? Any blockers?
- Monthly review (end of each phase): How's it going? Adjust as needed
- 90-day assessment: Rescore all domains. What worked?
```

### Step 8: Accountability and Review

**Schedule recurring check-ins:**
- Weekly 15-min review (Sunday)
- Monthly 60-min review (end of month)
- 90-day full re-audit

**Accountability options:**
- Share plan with a partner or coach
- Monthly report-out to a trusted friend
- Financial stake (Beeminder for habit tracking)

**Monthly review questions:**
- What worked? (Keep doing)
- What's stalled? (Why? What's the blocker?)
- Do I need to adjust the plan or the commitments?
- Am I being honest about effort, or am I coasting?

## Quality Gates (5+)

1. **Honest Scoring**: Are you rating your actual satisfaction, or how you think you should feel? (Real audit is honest and sometimes uncomfortable.)
2. **Specific Gaps**: Can you articulate exactly what's missing, or is it vague? ("I want better relationships" is vague; "I want monthly dinners with close friends" is specific.)
3. **Targets Achievable**: Can you hit 90-day targets without heroics or external miracles? (If it depends on someone else or perfect circumstances, adjust.)
4. **Actions Concrete**: Can you do action 1 by [DATE] without more information? (If not, it's too vague.)
5. **Values Aligned**: Does the plan honor your actual values, or are you chasing someone else's definition of a good life?
6. **Accountability Real**: Will someone know if you're off track? (Without accountability, plan is fantasy.)

## Examples

### Good Audit (Specific, Leveraged, Achievable)

**Current state**: Career 7, Relationships 4, Health 4, Finances 6, Growth 5, Fun 3

**Gap**: Overwork has compressed relationships, health, and fun. Career is good but at cost of everything else.

**90-Day focus**: Relationships and health (high leverage: both benefit from exercise with friends)

**Actions**:
- Join running group (meets 3x/week, Wed evening + Sat morning)
- Monthly friend dinners (1st Friday of month)
- Daily 10pm bedtime (sleep as non-negotiable)

**Result**: Exercise is now social (relationships + health), sleep improves, fun increases from running community. Career stays strong (you're more focused and energetic). Finance improves (spending on friend dinners instead of stress shopping).

---

### Bad Audit (Vague, Unachievable, No Accountability)

**Current state**: Unhappy in all domains

**Plans**: Get fit, travel more, improve career, fix relationships, read more, have fun

**Problems**:
- No specific scores
- No gap analysis; no root causes identified
- Too many targets; no prioritization
- No concrete actions (when, how, with whom?)
- No accountability; plan will be forgotten by week 2

## Common Mistakes (3+)

1. **Unbalanced Overhaul**: Trying to improve all six domains at once. You burn out by week 3. Focus on 2-3 domains with highest ROI.

2. **Vague Targets**: "Be healthier," "improve relationships," "learn stuff." Can't measure progress. Replace with specifics: "3x/week exercise," "monthly dinner with 5 close friends," "complete one course."

3. **Ignoring Root Causes**: You score relationships 4 and decide to "text friends more." But the real issue is you work 60 hours/week. Adding activities won't fix it; reducing work hours will. Address root cause.

4. **No Trade-Off Acknowledgment**: You want to double your work output (career 7 → 9) while tripling friend time (relationships 4 → 8). Impossible. Name the trade-offs explicitly: "I'm prioritizing relationships over career advancement."

5. **Accountability-Free Plan**: You write a beautiful 90-day plan and tell no one. By week 4, you've forgotten it. Share it with a partner, coach, or accountability group.

6. **Silo Thinking**: You improve career but ignore how it affects health and relationships. See interconnections. Leverage points exist where small changes cascade.

## Anti-Patterns (3+)

1. **Compare to Others**: You score relationships 5 and feel bad because your friend's score is 9. Your life is yours. Don't audit against others' scores; audit against your own values and 5-year vision.

2. **Shame Spiral**: You score 3 in a domain and feel like a failure. Normal. Most people have 1-2 low domains. The audit isn't judgment; it's awareness. Use it to change direction.

3. **Analysis Paralysis**: You spend 4 hours on the audit, crafting perfect targets, then never execute. Good audit is 60-90 minutes. Good execution is 90 days of action. Spend 80% of time on the latter.

4. **No Revision**: You did an audit last year and ignore it. Redo it every 12 months. Life changes; priorities shift. Keep the practice living.

5. **Ignore Seasons**: You plan as if life is static. Reality: career seasons differ (high-stress projects, slow periods), relationship seasons vary (new love, child birth, aging parents), health seasons shift. Build flexibility into your plan.
