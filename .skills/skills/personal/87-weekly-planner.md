# Weekly Planner

## Purpose

Most weeks are reactive: you chase urgent tasks, skip recovery time, and never address important-but-not-urgent work. This skill applies the Eisenhower matrix to separate urgent from important, time blocking to protect deep work, energy mapping to schedule tasks by energy requirement, buffer time to handle surprises, and a structured review to learn from each week. You'll exit with a realistic weekly plan that aligns with your actual energy, priorities, and constraints.

## When to Use

- You feel constantly behind or reactive
- You skip important work (strategic projects, learning, relationships)
- You're burned out from endless meetings or low-value tasks
- Your energy crashes mid-week
- You want to work smarter, not just longer
- You're preparing for a high-stakes week (launch, negotiations, performance reviews)
- **Do NOT use when**: You have <5 tasks per week (too simple to require this), you work in pure reactive mode (e.g., on-call engineer), or you're in vacation/sabbatical mode

## Instructions

### Step 1: Brain Dump All Tasks and Obligations

Spend 10 minutes writing down everything you need to do this week:

- Recurring meetings (all-hands, 1-on-1s, standup)
- Deadlines (reports due, deliverables, approvals needed)
- Projects (big rocks, strategic initiatives)
- Admin (email, expenses, scheduling)
- Health/personal (exercise, food shopping, family time)
- Social commitments (dinners, calls, events)

Don't edit or prioritize yet. Just dump. Aim for 25-50 items.

### Step 2: Categorize Using the Eisenhower Matrix

Draw a 2x2 grid:

```
                URGENT              NOT URGENT
IMPORTANT       Quadrant 1          Quadrant 2
                Crisis/Deadlines    Important projects

NOT IMPORTANT   Quadrant 3          Quadrant 4
                Interruptions       Time wasters
```

Place each task into one quadrant:

- **Q1 (Urgent + Important)**: Crisis, deadline tomorrow, customer issue. These take time but are inescapable.
- **Q2 (Important + Not Urgent)**: Strategic projects, learning, deep work, relationship building. This is where you want most time. Q2 is often where you *should* spend time but don't.
- **Q3 (Urgent + Not Important)**: Meetings someone else scheduled, replies to non-critical emails, busywork that feels pressing. Minimize these.
- **Q4 (Not Important + Not Urgent)**: Scrolling, low-value meetings, pseudowork. Eliminate.

Be honest. Meetings feel urgent but often aren't important. Strategic writing isn't urgent but is important. Move things ruthlessly.

### Step 3: Timebox Your Week

Estimate time for each task. Be realistic—most people underestimate by 30%.

Add up Q1 + Q2 hours. If it exceeds your available work time (40 hours for full-time, minus commute, meals, breaks), you have a problem. Something has to give.

**Weekly time budget example** (40-hour week):

- Meetings: 10 hours (recurring + ad hoc)
- Q1 tasks (urgent/important): 8 hours
- Q2 tasks (strategic): 12 hours
- Admin/email: 5 hours
- Buffer/slack: 5 hours
- Total: 40 hours

Notice the buffer. Without it, one urgent task derails the entire week.

### Step 4: Time Block Your Week

Create a weekly calendar (Monday–Friday, or your actual work days).

**Rules for time blocking:**

1. **Stack similar tasks**: Batch emails into 2 blocks (9am and 4pm), not throughout the day.
2. **Protect deep work in prime time**: Your best energy and focus are finite. Q2 (important) work goes in your peak hours, typically 9am–12pm.
3. **Front-load hard stuff**: Do cognitively demanding tasks early in the week and early in the day.
4. **Cluster meetings**: Block meetings into 1-2 days if possible (Monday + Friday), leaving Wed-Thu free for deep work.
5. **Schedule breaks**: Lunch, exercise, and walking are not optional. Block them.
6. **Reserve Friday afternoon for review and planning**: Not for new work.

**Example time block**:

```
MONDAY
9-11am:    Deep Work (Q2 strategic project)
11am-12:   Team standup
12-1pm:    Lunch
1-3pm:     Meetings (customer call + 1-on-1s)
3-4pm:     Admin + email
4-5pm:     Unblock others (Slack, messages)

TUESDAY
9-12pm:    Deep Work (Q2)
12-1pm:    Lunch
1-3pm:     Q1 tasks (urgent items)
3-4pm:     Review and adjustments
4-5pm:     End-of-day wrap

WEDNESDAY
9-12pm:    Deep Work (Q2)
12-1pm:    Lunch
1-3pm:     Meetings (only critical)
3-4pm:     Writing, reviews
4-5pm:     Thinking and planning
```

Use different colors for categories: Q1 in red, Q2 in blue, admin in gray, meetings in yellow.

### Step 5: Map Energy and Stamina

Not all hours are equal. You have peak energy (when you do hard thinking), standard energy (routine work), and low energy (admin, easy tasks).

**Common energy pattern** (yours may differ):

- **Peak hours**: 9–11am (best for deep work, decisions, creative thinking)
- **Standard hours**: 11am–3pm (good for meetings, collaborative work, moderate-difficulty tasks)
- **Low-energy hours**: After 3pm (admin, email, rote work, follow-ups)
- **Crash window**: 2–3pm (post-lunch dip; avoid important decisions)

Place your hardest Q2 task in your peak window. Save Q3 and Q4 for low-energy blocks. Don't schedule a critical negotiation at 4pm if you always crash then.

### Step 6: Add Buffer Time (15-20% of Week)

Surprise meetings, urgent emails, and unexpected crises are guaranteed. Without buffer, they destroy your plan.

**Buffer purposes:**
- Handle urgent things that emerge mid-week (Q1)
- Extend tasks that took longer than estimated
- Respond to unexpected needs from manager or team
- Breathe (catch up, decompress)

Protect your buffer. Don't pre-fill it with "nice to do" tasks. When the week is chaotic, buffer gives you space to absorb it without overworking.

**Buffer placement**: Scatter it—1 hour Tuesday afternoon, 2 hours Thursday morning, etc. Concentrated buffer is less useful than distributed.

### Step 7: Set Up a Weekly Review Ritual

Schedule 60 minutes Friday afternoon or Sunday evening.

**Review template**:

1. **What went well?** (2-3 wins, big or small)
2. **What didn't happen?** (Tasks you skipped or postponed)
3. **Why?** (Was it overestimation, interruption, deprioritization, or something else?)
4. **Energy pattern**: Did the time blocks match your energy? What surprised you?
5. **Blockers**: What slowed you down? (Unclear requirements, waiting on others, technical issues, meetings)
6. **Next week**: What's the top priority? What needs to move from next week into this week (or vice versa)?
7. **Sustainable?**: Did this week feel sustainable? Or did you work overtime/skip meals/burn out?

Write this review down (even 100 words). You'll see patterns over 4-8 weeks.

### Step 8: Adjust and Refine

After 2-3 weeks of blocking and reviewing, you'll see patterns:

- Meetings always run over → add 10-min buffers
- Deep work at 9am always interrupted → move earlier or find a quiet space
- Q1 tasks always expand → estimate 50% higher
- Friday afternoon always derailed → protect it more fiercely

Update your template based on reality. A plan that doesn't evolve is a fantasy.

## Quality Gates (5+)

1. **Eisenhower Honest**: Did you ruthlessly categorize, or is everything "important"? (If Q1 + Q2 > 25 hours, you need to cut or say no to something.)
2. **Time Budget Real**: Do estimated hours fit within actual available time? (If not, identify what to drop or postpone.)
3. **Peak Hours Protected**: Is your hardest Q2 task scheduled during your best energy, not your worst?
4. **Buffer Exists**: Is there 15-20% unallocated time for surprises?
5. **Review Ritual Set**: Is Friday review on the calendar as non-negotiable?
6. **Energy Aligned**: Does the schedule match your energy pattern, not an idealized version?

## Examples

### Good Weekly Plan (Realistic, Protected, Buffered)

**Week of March 10, 2026**

**Q2 priority**: Finish proposal draft

**Time blocks**:
- Mon 9–11am: Deep work on proposal (peak energy)
- Tue 9–12pm: Deep work continued
- Wed 1–3pm: Another deep work block (protect strategic work)
- Thu 3–4pm: Admin (low-energy hours)
- Fri afternoon: Review, respond to feedback

**Buffer**: 2 hours Tuesday, 3 hours Thursday (unscheduled)

**Meetings**: Clustered Mon 11am–12pm, Thu 10am–12pm

**Result**: Proposal gets 8 hours of protected deep work, meetings don't bleed across the week, buffer absorbs 2-3 urgent things that emerge, and Friday is reserved for review.

### Bad Weekly Plan (Unrealistic, No Buffer)

**Week plan**: "Do proposal, attend 8 meetings, finish 5 projects, email, admin."

Problems:
- No Eisenhower matrix; everything feels urgent
- No time block; work is reactive
- No buffer; first surprise derails entire week
- No energy mapping; might schedule hard work at 4pm (bad)
- No review; repeat same mistakes next week

## Common Mistakes (3+)

1. **Q2 Starvation**: Everything is Q1 or Q3, so strategic work never happens. Ruthlessly move non-urgent meetings (Q3) to give Q2 time.

2. **Overestimated Hours**: "I have 40 hours, so I'll plan 40 hours of work." You won't. Add 20% buffer and plan 32-35 hours of actual work.

3. **No Buffer Protection**: Pre-filling buffer with "nice to do" tasks. When crisis hits, you work 50-hour week. Protect buffer.

4. **Energy Mismatch**: Scheduling hardest work at worst energy (afternoon if you crash then). Match task difficulty to energy level.

5. **Review Skipped**: First week you do review, weeks 2-4 you skip it. Review is when you learn and adjust. Make it non-negotiable.

6. **Monday Overload**: Cramming everything into early week because you think Friday might be free. Spread Q2 across the week.

## Anti-Patterns (3+)

1. **Optimization Theater**: Spending 3 hours perfectly planning your week when 30 minutes would suffice. Plan in 30 minutes, execute, review and refine. Perfection is the enemy.

2. **Inflexible Time Blocks**: Your 9–11am deep work block gets interrupted; you refuse to reschedule because "that's when deep work happens." Rigidity kills plans. Have a backup time block for deep work if prime time is lost.

3. **Review Guilt Spiral**: You didn't hit 70% of plan, so you feel like a failure. Most weeks hit 60–70% of plan. That's normal. Focus on learning, not blame.

4. **No Communication**: You block your calendar but don't tell anyone. They schedule meetings over your deep work block. Update your team: "I'm protecting Wed 1–3pm for uninterrupted work."

5. **Quarterly Burnout Cycle**: You plan aggressively for 8 weeks, burn out by week 9, and take a week off to recover. Instead, ensure every week is sustainable (even if slightly behind "optimal"). Consistency beats heroics.
