# Goal Setter

## Purpose

Most goals fail not from lack of effort but from lack of clarity and structure. This skill converts your aspirations into a rigorous framework with measurable targets, sequential milestones, built-in accountability, pre-mortems to anticipate obstacles, and review cadences that keep you on track. You'll leave with a complete goal artifact that bridges the gap between intention and execution.

## When to Use

- You have a desired outcome but lack concrete steps
- You're unsure how to measure progress or completion
- You need to commit publicly or report to someone else
- You want to anticipate failure modes before they derail you
- You're restarting a goal after previous attempts stalled
- **Do NOT use when**: The goal is trivial (under 1 week of effort), requires only one action, or is wholly dependent on external approval you can't influence

## Instructions

### Step 1: Clarify the Vision Statement

Write a 1-2 sentence future state description without constraints. What does success look like in vivid detail? Avoid vague words like "better," "more," or "improve." Use sensory language: "I'm presenting quarterly results to a room of 50 investors" rather than "I'm better at public speaking."

Push on this until the vision is specific enough that someone else could recognize success without asking you.

### Step 2: Define the SMART Criteria

SMART stands for Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound. Work through each:

- **Specific**: What exactly are you accomplishing? (Not "get fit" but "run 5km without stopping")
- **Measurable**: How will you know you succeeded? Quantify it: reps, time, revenue, certification achieved, portfolio pieces created
- **Achievable**: Is this realistic given your constraints (time, budget, current ability)? Set a target that requires growth but isn't fantasy
- **Relevant**: Why does this matter? Link to a larger life domain (career advancement, health, relationships, creative expression)
- **Time-bound**: When is the deadline? Pick a specific date 3-12 months out depending on scope

Document all five criteria explicitly. Common mistakes: skipping measurable (making progress invisible) or setting impossible timelines (crushing motivation).

### Step 3: Break into Milestones

Divide the goal into 4-8 milestone checkpoints. Each milestone should:

- Build on the previous one (sequenced, not parallel)
- Be completable in 2-6 weeks
- Have a clear success criterion
- Represent 10-25% progress toward the final goal

Example: "Run 5km in 6 months"
- Mile 1 (Weeks 1-2): Run 1km 3x/week without stopping
- Mile 2 (Weeks 3-4): Run 2km 3x/week consistently
- Mile 3 (Weeks 5-6): Complete first 3km run
- Mile 4 (Weeks 7-8): Run 3km + add one 4km attempt
- Mile 5 (Weeks 9-10): Hit 4km consistently
- Mile 6 (Weeks 11-12): Complete first 5km test run

### Step 4: Identify Obstacles and Pre-Mortems

Close your eyes and imagine it's the deadline, and you failed. Write a eulogy for this goal. What killed it? Common obstacles:

- Time: conflicting commitments, lack of discipline
- Knowledge: missing skills or expertise
- External: resource costs, dependency on others' decisions
- Environmental: no gym nearby, no quiet space to study
- Emotional: fear of failure, perfectionism, lack of motivation

For each major obstacle, propose a mitigation strategy:
- **Eliminate**: Remove the obstacle entirely (stop going to noisy places)
- **Reduce**: Lessen its impact (use noise-cancelling headphones)
- **Prepare**: Have a response plan (if you miss a week, do 2x next week)
- **Accept**: Acknowledge it and adjust your timeline or target

### Step 5: Set Up Accountability Structure

Isolation kills goals. Choose your accountability mechanism:

- **Public commitment**: Tell 3+ people your goal, deadline, and one monthly milestone
- **Financial stake**: Pledge money to a friend or website (StickK, Beeminder) that you forfeit if you fail
- **Reporting cadence**: Schedule weekly check-ins with a partner or weekly review slot in your calendar
- **Visible tracking**: Use a habit tracker, spreadsheet, or wall chart that you see daily
- **Professional**: Hire a coach, join a cohort program, or sign up for a class with deadlines

The strongest approach: combine two mechanisms (e.g., public commitment + weekly check-ins).

### Step 6: Design Your Review Schedule

Progress invisibility leads to drift. Schedule:

- **Weekly check-in** (15 min): Log activity, tally metrics, note obstacles that emerged
- **Bi-weekly pulse** (30 min): Are you on track for the next milestone? Adjust if needed
- **Monthly review** (60 min): Assess milestone completion, celebrate wins, troubleshoot blocks, plan the next 4 weeks
- **End-of-goal review** (90 min): Did you hit the target? What enabled success? What would you do differently? What's next?

Use a template so each review takes the same structure and you can spot patterns.

### Step 7: Lock In and Commit

Write your final goal statement as a single document combining:
- Vision statement
- SMART criteria
- Milestone breakdown with dates
- Top 3-5 obstacles + mitigations
- Accountability mechanism and schedule
- First week's actions (be specific: which day, what time, what location)

Share with your accountability partner or stakeholder. Treat this as a binding commitment, not a suggestion.

## Quality Gates (5+)

1. **Specificity Test**: Could someone else recognize success without clarification? Can you measure it with a number?
2. **Milestone Independence**: Can each milestone be completed without the others, or does order matter? (Order should matter—they should be sequenced.)
3. **Obstacle Coverage**: Did you identify at least 3 realistic failure modes? Are they addressed?
4. **Accountability Teeth**: Is there real consequence (financial, social, or structural) for missing the deadline?
5. **Timeline Realism**: Did you build in a 20% buffer? Is the deadline not more than 12 months out? (Longer goals need sub-goals.)
6. **First-Week Clarity**: Can you execute week 1 actions today, or do you need other information first?

## Examples

### Good Goal (Specific, Accountable, Sequenced)

**Goal**: Get AWS Solutions Architect certification by 2026-07-31

**Vision**: I hold my AWS cert and land a DevOps role paying $130k+ within 6 months.

**SMART**:
- Specific: Pass AWS Solutions Architect Associate exam
- Measurable: 720+ score (passing); 3 practice exams at 75%+ first
- Achievable: 10 hours/week study for 24 weeks with exam prep course
- Relevant: Unlocks higher-paying DevOps roles and cloud credibility
- Time-bound: July 31, 2026

**Milestones**:
- Week 1-2: Complete AWS Fundamentals course (4 hours video)
- Week 3-4: Study EC2, RDS, S3 modules; take first practice exam
- Week 5-8: Deep-dive networking, IAM, disaster recovery
- Week 9-12: Practice exam 2 (target 75%+)
- Week 13-16: Review weak areas, white papers, architecture patterns
- Week 17-20: Practice exam 3; timed full-length tests
- Week 21-24: Final review, mock exams, test day

**Obstacles**:
- Fatigue from day job → 6am study sessions before work
- Weak on networking → schedule extra networking deep-dive
- Test anxiety → Do 5+ timed full-length practice exams
- Work travel → Download video PDFs for offline study

**Accountability**: Weekly email to manager with study hours and practice exam scores; $500 bet with colleague (forfeit if fail by Aug 1).

### Bad Goal (Vague, No Milestones, No Accountability)

**Goal**: "Get better at cloud computing"

Problems:
- "Better at" is unmeasurable. What does better look like?
- No deadline. When does "better" get evaluated?
- No milestones. How do you know if you're on track after week 1?
- No accountability. Who knows if you fail?
- No obstacles planned. You'll hit the first blocker and quit.

## Common Mistakes (3+)

1. **Milestone Overload**: Breaking a 6-month goal into 20 weekly milestones creates false urgency and kills motivation. Aim for 4-8 major milestones only.

2. **Vague Success Metrics**: "Improve my writing" has no finish line. Replace with "Complete a 50-page manuscript and get feedback from 3 beta readers by Dec 1."

3. **Missing First-Week Actions**: Goals without "this week I will..." rarely begin. Attach 3 concrete actions to your first milestone.

4. **No Obstacle Planning**: The goal dies at the first real blocker. Identify obstacles now, not when you hit them.

5. **Accountability Theater**: Telling people your goal without real stakes (money, public shame, deadline check-ins) doesn't work. Choose a mechanism with teeth.

6. **Timeline Disconnected from Milestones**: 4 milestones over 6 months means 1.5 months per mile. Ensure your timeline gives each milestone enough time.

## Anti-Patterns (3+)

1. **Ambitious Vagueness**: "I want to be a great leader" sounds inspiring but is unmeasurable and demotivating. Replace with a specific outcome tied to leadership development: "Complete an executive coaching program and get 360 feedback showing improvement in delegation and listening scores."

2. **Perfectionist Timelines**: Setting a goal that requires flawless execution every single week. Build in 1-2 "off" weeks in your timeline to account for reality (illness, emergencies, motivation dips). A 24-week goal should have buffer room.

3. **Accountability Abandonment**: You set accountability at week 1, then stop check-ins by week 4. Block your calendar for all check-in dates now. Treat them as non-negotiable as dentist appointments.

4. **Milestone Dependency Creep**: Goals that depend on external approval (waiting for someone to hire you, fund you, or decide if your work is good) need a revised timeline or added contingency milestones. Don't make the final milestone someone else's decision.

5. **Silent Struggle**: You hit a blocker at week 3 but don't tell anyone, hoping to solve it alone. Communicate obstacles to your accountability partner immediately—they may have solved this before or know someone who has.
