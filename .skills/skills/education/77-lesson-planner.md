# Lesson Planner

## Purpose
This skill generates complete, standards-aligned lesson plans with clear learning objectives mapped to Bloom's taxonomy, differentiated instructional activities for multiple readiness levels, embedded formative assessments, summative assessments, and realistic timing. It ensures lessons are cognitively rigorous, inclusive, and evidence-based.

## When to Use
- Planning single lessons or multi-day units
- Designing lessons for mixed-ability classrooms
- Creating lessons that require multiple cognitive levels (remember → analyze → evaluate)
- Developing lessons that need built-in assessment
- **Do NOT use when**: Planning entire semesters (use curriculum-designer instead), or for purely operational/logistical planning

## Instructions

### Step 1: Define Context and Standards
Gather essential information: grade level, subject, duration (single period or multi-day unit), number of students, relevant state/national standards (Common Core, Next Generation Science Standards, state standards). Document learner characteristics: reading levels, prior knowledge gaps, special accommodations (ELL, gifted, IEP). Identify which standards the lesson addresses and the specific alignment codes.

### Step 2: Write Measurable Learning Objectives Using Bloom's Taxonomy
Create 3-5 objectives using action verbs matched to cognitive levels. Use template: "Students will [verb] [content] as evidenced by [assessment]." Level examples: Remember (define, list), Understand (explain, classify), Apply (demonstrate, solve), Analyze (compare, distinguish), Evaluate (judge, critique), Create (design, compose). Each objective should be observable and measurable. Start with lower-order (remember/understand) and progress to higher-order (analyze/evaluate/create).

### Step 3: Design Hook/Opening (5-10 minutes)
Create a brief, engaging activation that captures attention and connects to prior knowledge. Options: provocative question, real-world scenario, video clip, mystery/puzzle, personal connection prompt. Explicitly state what prior knowledge students need and how this opening surfaces it. Anticipate misconceptions students may voice and plan how to address them.

### Step 4: Design Core Instruction (primary content delivery, 15-25 minutes)
Break complex concepts into teachable chunks. For each chunk, specify: content outline, instructional strategy (mini-lecture, think-pair-share, guided discovery, modeling), key vocabulary with student-friendly definitions, visual aids or manipulatives needed. Include a check-for-understanding moment after each major concept (thumbs-up/middle/down, exit ticket, quick quiz). Specify how you'll maintain engagement (active student roles, talk time, writing).

### Step 5: Design Differentiated Activities (20-30 minutes)
Create 3 parallel activities matched to readiness levels: Below Standard (simplified content, more scaffolding, concrete examples), At Standard (grade-level complexity), Advanced (complex scenarios, transfer tasks, real-world problems). Each activity targets the same objective but varies in complexity, pacing, and support. Include specific student grouping strategies and how you'll monitor each group. Provide alternate activities for students who complete early (extension tasks, not busy work).

### Step 6: Plan Formative Assessments (embedded throughout)
Identify 3-4 quick checks during the lesson: teacher observation rubric for activity work, student self-assessment checklist, peer feedback protocol, quick write on a core concept, think-pair-share exchange with specific listening criteria. Specify how results will inform re-teaching or acceleration decisions.

### Step 7: Design Summative Assessment and Closure (10-15 minutes)
Create an assessment that directly measures the learning objectives (aligned to Bloom's level). Examples: performance task with rubric, multi-part problem, concept map with labels, written explanation with evidence, creative product with standards-based scoring. Include a closure routine: students reflect on learning (what did you learn, what confused you), preview next lesson, celebrate progress.

### Step 8: Plan Materials, Pacing, and Accommodations
List all physical materials needed (textbooks, manipulatives, technology, handouts) with quantity and prep notes. Create a minute-by-minute pacing guide. For each learner who needs accommodations, specify precisely: extra time, reduced complexity, alternative assessment, additional visuals, vocabulary pre-teaching, small-group instruction. Include backup plan if technology fails.

## Output Template

```
LESSON PLAN: [Topic] | Grade [X] | Duration: [Time] | [Date]

STANDARDS ALIGNMENT: [Standard Code] - [Description]

LEARNING OBJECTIVES (Bloom's Level)
1. Students will [VERB] [CONTENT] as evidenced by [ASSESSMENT]. (Understand)
2. Students will [VERB] [CONTENT] as evidenced by [ASSESSMENT]. (Apply)
3. Students will [VERB] [CONTENT] as evidenced by [ASSESSMENT]. (Analyze)

HOOK (5 min) - Activation Strategy: [Description]
Prior Knowledge Surface: [What you'll ask/observe]
Anticipated Misconceptions: [What students might think]

CORE INSTRUCTION (20 min)
Chunk 1: [Concept] - Strategy: [Method]
  Check-for-Understanding: [Quick formative check]
Chunk 2: [Concept] - Strategy: [Method]
  Check-for-Understanding: [Quick formative check]

DIFFERENTIATED ACTIVITIES (25 min)
Below Standard Group (3 students): [Description with scaffolds]
At Standard Group (15 students): [Description]
Advanced Group (4 students): [Description with transfer tasks]

FORMATIVE ASSESSMENTS (ongoing)
- [Check-for-understanding 1 with rubric/checklist]
- [Check-for-understanding 2 with rubric/checklist]

SUMMATIVE ASSESSMENT & CLOSURE (15 min)
Task: [Description with rubric]
Closure Routine: [Reflection prompt + preview]

MATERIALS NEEDED
[List with quantities and prep notes]

ACCOMMODATIONS
- [Student name/IEP goal]: [Specific modification]
- [Student name/ELL level]: [Specific modification]

MINUTE-BY-MINUTE PACING
0-5 min: Hook
5-25 min: Core instruction
25-50 min: Differentiated activities
50-60 min: Assessment & closure

[What could go wrong, contingencies, connection to previous lesson]
```

## Quality Gates
1. **Bloom's Alignment**: Each objective uses a specific action verb; highest-level objective is at Analyze/Evaluate/Create
2. **Measurable**: All objectives specify "as evidenced by" with observable criteria
3. **Differentiation**: At least 3 activity levels; specific scaffolds listed; not just shorter/longer versions
4. **Formative Assessment**: At least 3 check-for-understanding moments with teacher response planned
5. **Pacing Reality**: Total time accounts for transitions; no activity listed as "unlimited time"
6. **Accommodations Specificity**: Names or student groups with precise modifications, not generic "visual aids"
7. **Connection to Standards**: Not just listed; learning objectives directly map to standard language

## Examples

### Good: Geometry Lesson Plan (Grade 7)
**Learning Objective 2: Students will ANALYZE properties of triangles by comparing angle sums and side relationships as evidenced by a written explanation with 2+ supporting examples.**
- Hook: "I'm building a bridge. Which triangle shape is strongest: acute, right, or obtuse? Discuss." (Surfaces prior knowledge about angle types)
- Core Instruction: Mini-lesson on angle-sum property with protractor measurement task
- Differentiated Activities: Below Standard uses pre-drawn triangles with angle measures labeled; At Standard measures and calculates angles; Advanced derives why angle sum = 180°
- Formative Check: Students sketch a 45-45-90 triangle; teacher observes accuracy
- Summative: Performance task—given constraints (one angle must be obtuse, one side must be 5 cm), design and justify a triangle

### Bad: History Lesson Plan
**Learning Objective: Students will learn about the Civil War.**
- Hook: "Today we're learning about the Civil War" (no activation, no misconception probe)
- Core Instruction: "Read textbook pages 234-239 and answer questions" (passive, no active engagement)
- Activity: "All students write a paragraph about a general" (no differentiation, low cognitive demand, vague prompt)
- Assessment: Quiz with true/false questions (assesses only recall, not analysis)
- Issues: Vague objective (what does "learn" mean?); no evidence of Bloom's levels; no differentiation; assessment misaligned to objective

## Common Mistakes

1. **Bloom's Confusion**: Writing all objectives at Remember level ("Students will list facts..."). Every lesson should have 1-2 higher-order objectives (Analyze/Evaluate/Create) to develop thinking skills, not just recall.

2. **Fake Differentiation**: Creating 3 activities that teach the same thing at slightly different speeds. True differentiation means different cognitive demand—advanced students should be analyzing/creating while below-standard students apply/understand simpler content. Both are rigorous at their level.

3. **Missing the Formative Checks**: Writing detailed instruction and summative assessment but no check-for-understanding. If 70% of students fail the summative task and you have no formative data, you don't know when comprehension broke down. Plan at least one CFU per 10-minute instructional chunk.

4. **Vague Accommodations**: Listing "extended time" without specifying the assignment, and "visual aids" without naming them. Good accommodation: "Marco (ELL Level 2): Sentence frame for written reflection—'The triangle had ___ angles that totaled ___ degrees because...'"

5. **Ignoring Prior Knowledge**: Starting instruction without confirming students have prerequisite skills. Build in explicit review—even one quick think-pair-share question surfaces gaps before 25 minutes of confused instruction.

## Anti-Patterns

1. **"Cookbook" Lessons**: Rigid, prescriptive lesson plans that leave no space for student questions or unexpected learning directions. Instead: Build in decision points ("If 60%+ students struggle with this concept, move to re-teach slot; otherwise accelerate") and allow 5-10% unscheduled talk time for tangents.

2. **Assessment Afterthought**: Designing instruction first, then adding a summative assessment that doesn't match objectives. Instead: Start with the summative assessment in mind (backward design), then build instruction to prepare students for that specific task.

3. **Equity Erasure**: One-size-fits-all lessons justified as "treating all students equally." Instead: Recognize students enter with different skills. Differentiation IS equity—meeting each student where they are, not at the same place.

4. **Engagement Performativity**: Creating "fun" activities (games, videos, crafts) that engage students but don't advance learning. Students have fun but walk away without deepened understanding. Instead: Activities should be engaging AND cognitively demanding. Ask: "Does this activity teach them the objective, or just distract them?"

5. **Assessment-Less Instruction**: Teaching without checks for understanding. Only finding out at the summative assessment (end of unit) that students didn't learn. Instead: Formative assessment is continuous—every 5-10 minutes, you know whether students understand. Use this data to adjust pacing and re-teach.

6. **Transition Time Ignored**: Planning 45 minutes of activities for a 50-minute period with no realistic transition time. Lessons overrun or feel rushed. Instead: Account for transitions (line up, materials distribution, moving to groups) explicitly. If you can't fit everything, cut something; don't expect the impossible.

## Lesson Planning Tips for Success

- **Start with the End in Mind**: Write the summative assessment first. What do you want students to be able to do? Then design instruction to teach that specifically.
- **Use Formative Data**: Don't wait until the unit test. Quick daily checks tell you if you're on track. Adjust instruction immediately.
- **Over-Plan Transitions**: Transitions eat time. Plan them explicitly—what students will do, where they'll sit, what materials they need. "They'll just figure it out" = 10 minutes lost chaos.
- **Build in Slack**: Include more formative assessment and practice than you think you need. If students nail the objective by Day 2, you have extension tasks ready (not more easy practice—more rigorous applications).
- **Document Accommodations**: Not "visual aids" but "Keisha (ELL 2): sentence frame with 3-word choices for conclusion; allowed to write in Spanish for processing."
