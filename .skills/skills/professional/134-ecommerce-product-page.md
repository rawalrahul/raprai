# E-Commerce Product Page Writer

## Purpose
An e-commerce product page is your sales person. It must convert browser curiosity into purchase intent through three mechanisms: (1) emotional connection (headline and hero image grab attention and make a promise), (2) rational justification (features and specs prove the product delivers), and (3) friction removal (FAQs, reviews, guarantees reduce purchase hesitation). This skill walks you through writing product pages that increase conversion rate, reduce return rate, and minimize support burden by pre-answering customer questions.

## When to Use
- Launching a new product on your e-commerce platform
- Optimizing underperforming product pages (low conversion rate or high return rate)
- Creating templates for your internal team to follow (consistency at scale)
- Writing for different market segments (same product, different value propositions for B2B vs. B2C)
- Developing A/B test variations to improve conversion
- **Do NOT use when**: product isn't ready to sell (incomplete specs, imagery missing), you're writing internal product documentation (use technical documentation instead), or your primary channel is wholesale/B2B (may require different approach focusing on business outcomes)

## Instructions

### Step 1: Write a Benefit-Focused Headline (<70 characters)
The headline is the most important element. It must answer: "What's in it for me?" (benefit) in a way that's specific enough to differentiate your product.

**Principle**: Lead with the primary benefit (what problem does this solve or what desire does it fulfill), not the product category or feature.

**Bad Headlines** (Feature-focused):
- "High-Performance Running Shoe" — tells me it's a shoe, not why I want it
- "Stainless Steel Coffee Maker" — tells me the material, not the benefit
- "15-Inch Laptop" — tells me the size, not what I can do with it

**Good Headlines** (Benefit-focused):
- "Run 20% Faster Without Foot Pain" — benefit (speed, pain-free) + specificity (20%)
- "Fresh Coffee in 60 Seconds" — benefit (convenience) + specificity (time)
- "Edit 4K Video on the Go" — benefit (capability + mobility)

**Headline Formula**: [Primary Benefit] + [Specificity/Differentiator] + [Emotional Trigger (optional)]

Examples:
- "Sleep 60% Better With Pressure-Distributing Technology" (benefit: better sleep, specificity: 60%, emotional: we all want better sleep)
- "Lose 10 Pounds in 30 Days—or Your Money Back" (benefit: weight loss, specificity: amount and timeframe, emotional: backed by guarantee)
- "Waterproof Backpack That Lasts 20 Years" (benefit: protected gear, specificity: 20 years, emotional: reliability/value)

**Constraints**:
- Under 70 characters (fits in meta title tag for SEO)
- No ALL CAPS (hard to read)
- No hyphens unless necessary (breaks readability)
- Specific numbers over vague claims ("30% more storage" beats "increased storage")

### Step 2: Write Feature-to-Benefit Bullet Points (5-7 bullets)
Customers scan; they don't read in detail. Bullets must be scannable and speak to benefits, not just features.

**Feature vs. Benefit**:
- Feature: "Dual-zone temperature control" — what it has
- Benefit: "Keep your cold drinks cold and warm drinks hot in one bag" — what it does for me

**Formula for Each Bullet**: [Benefit] via [Feature]

Examples:
- "Keep Your Hands Warm All Day (Insulated Thermal Lining)"
- "Work Faster With Silent Mechanical Switches (Cherry MX Red Switches)"
- "Never Lose Your Keys (Integrated Bluetooth Tracker)"

**Selection Criteria** (pick 5-7 that matter most):
1. Does this solve a key problem the customer has?
2. Is this a differentiator vs. competitors?
3. Is this something a customer would ask about?
4. Does this create emotional resonance?

Avoid bullets that:
- Describe obvious features ("Made of material")
- Are weaker than other bullets (cut the weakest)
- Use jargon without translating to benefit

**Example Bullet Set (Wireless Headphones)**:
- Hear Every Note Crystal Clear (Active Noise Cancellation)
- Battery Lasts 30 Hours on One Charge (Premium Lithium Battery)
- Seamless Switching Between 4 Devices (Multipoint Bluetooth)
- Fits Comfortably All Day (Ergonomic Memory Foam Ear Cups)
- Feel Like You're in the Studio (Premium Sound Drivers)
- Control Music Without Touching Your Phone (Built-in Voice Control)

### Step 3: Write a Persuasive Long Description (300-500 words)
The headline grabbed attention; the bullets justified the purchase rationally. Now the long description tells the story and removes final hesitation.

**Structure**:

**Opening Paragraph (50-75 words): The Story**
Make it relatable. Paint a scenario where the product solves the customer's problem:
"You're mid-hike when your phone dies, leaving you without navigation, music, or the ability to call for help. You realize your charger stays at home. This headache repeats weekly until you discover a better solution."

NOT: "Our product is engineered to solve charging problems."

**Middle Paragraphs (200-300 words): How and Why**
Walk through the product's design and philosophy:
- Why was it designed this way? (problem it solves)
- What makes it different? (competitive differentiation)
- Who is it for? (specific use case or customer type)
- How does it improve the customer's life? (emotional/practical benefit)

Example: "The SolarCharge Backpack was designed by hikers, for hikers. We spent 18 months field-testing with wilderness guides and outdoor enthusiasts to get every detail right. The solar panels are angled at 25 degrees—the optimal angle for sunlight capture in mountainous terrain. They charge at 15W, fast enough to keep your phone topped up during a full day hike. And unlike bulky power banks, the solar panels integrate seamlessly into the backpack design. You're not adding weight or sacrificing style; you're adding capability. Whether you're hiking, camping, or traveling abroad, the SolarCharge keeps you connected without depending on finding an outlet."

**Closing Paragraph (50-75 words): The Invitation**
End with a clear call-to-action and final reassurance:
"Ready to hike worry-free? Add the SolarCharge Backpack to your cart. Not sure it's right for you? Check out our FAQ below or read customer reviews. We're confident you'll love it."

### Step 4: Build Technical Specifications Table
Customers want facts. Provide a clear specifications table (not prose descriptions scattered throughout).

**Format**: Two-column table (Spec | Value)

**Include** (as relevant to product):
- Dimensions (Length x Width x Height)
- Weight
- Material composition
- Colors available
- Capacity/Storage/Output specs
- Battery life (if applicable)
- Warranty
- Shipping/Delivery timeframe
- Country of origin
- Certifications (waterproof rating, safety certifications, etc.)

**Example Table (Bluetooth Speaker)**:

| Specification | Details |
|---------------|---------|
| **Dimensions** | 6.7" L x 2.4" W x 2.4" H |
| **Weight** | 8.5 oz |
| **Material** | Aluminum alloy body with silicone base |
| **Battery Life** | 20 hours at 50% volume; 12 hours at 100% volume |
| **Charging Time** | 3.5 hours via USB-C |
| **Waterproof Rating** | IPX7 (submersible in up to 1 meter of water for 30 minutes) |
| **Bluetooth Version** | 5.0 |
| **Maximum Output** | 20W peak power |
| **Colors** | Midnight Black, Pearl White, Ocean Blue |
| **Warranty** | 2-year manufacturer's warranty |
| **Shipping** | Ships within 24 hours; arrives in 2-3 business days |

Make it scannable. Don't describe specs in paragraph form.

### Step 5: Create a Comparison Chart
Help customers understand how your product stacks against alternatives (without naming competitors, which can backfire).

**Format**: Multi-column table showing tier levels or key differentiators

**Example (Mattress Product Page)**:

| Feature | Basic | Standard | Premium |
|---------|-------|----------|---------|
| Memory Foam | 2 inches | 4 inches | 6 inches |
| Cooling Technology | None | Gel-infused | Advanced Phase-Change Material |
| Edge Support | Standard | Enhanced | Premium Reinforced |
| Warranty | 5 years | 10 years | 20 years |
| Trial Period | 30 nights | 100 nights | 100 nights |
| Price | $399 | $699 | $1,099 |
| Best For | Budget shoppers | Balanced comfort & value | Premium comfort seekers |

This helps customers understand trade-offs and self-select into the right product tier.

### Step 6: Write FAQ Section (400-600 words)
Pre-answer customer hesitations and common questions. This reduces support burden significantly.

**Categories of FAQs**:
1. **Product Fit Questions** — Will this work for me?
2. **Feature/Benefit Questions** — How does X work?
3. **Comparison Questions** — How is this different from competitors?
4. **Logistics Questions** — When will it arrive? Can I return it?
5. **Value Questions** — Is it worth the price?

**FAQ Examples (Fitness Tracker)**:

**Q: Will this fit my wrist if I have small wrists?**
A: Yes. The band adjusts from 5.5" to 8.5" wrist circumference. If your wrist is smaller than 5.5" we offer an extra-small band separately ($15) that adjusts from 4.5" to 6.5".

**Q: How often do I need to charge it?**
A: Battery lasts 10 days on normal use (tracking all day, 1 workout per day). Heavy use (all-day tracking + 2+ workouts per day) drains battery in 7-8 days. A full charge takes 90 minutes via USB-C.

**Q: Does it work with both iPhone and Android?**
A: Yes. Download the companion app (free) on iPhone (iOS 13+) or Android (6.0+). Syncs wirelessly via Bluetooth 5.0.

**Q: Is there a warranty?**
A: Yes. We provide a 2-year manufacturer's warranty covering defects in materials and workmanship. This doesn't cover physical damage or water damage (it's splash-resistant, not waterproof). It doesn't cover normal wear and tear.

**Q: What's your return policy?**
A: Try it for 30 days. If it's not right for you, return it for a full refund (minus original shipping). No questions asked. Return shipping is free.

**Q: Is it waterproof? Can I swim with it?**
A: It's splash-resistant but not waterproof. You can wear it in the shower or in light rain. Don't submerge it or swim with it (the sensor will be damaged).

**Q: How accurate is the step counter?**
A: About 95% accurate vs. manual count. It learns your stride over 1-2 weeks of use, so accuracy improves with time. Activities like pushing a stroller or using a bicycle may show inflated step counts (it's motion-based, not GPS-based).

**Q: If I have questions, how do I contact support?**
A: Email us at support@company.com (response within 24 hours) or call 1-800-555-1234 (Mon-Fri, 9am-5pm EST).

### Step 7: Optimize SEO Meta Title and Description
These appear in search results and affect click-through rate.

**Meta Title** (50-60 characters including spaces):
- Primary keyword first (e.g., "Wireless Earbuds")
- Primary benefit
- Brand name (if established)

Example: "Wireless Earbuds with 30-Hour Battery Life | Our Brand"

**Meta Description** (150-160 characters):
- Hook with benefit
- 2-3 key differentiators
- CTA
- NO keyword stuffing

Example: "Premium wireless earbuds with 30-hour battery, active noise cancellation, and premium sound. Free shipping on orders over $50. Shop now."

### Step 9: Create Review Response Templates
Customer reviews influence purchase decisions. Have responses ready for common review types.

**Response to 5-Star Review:**
"Thank you so much for the amazing review! We're thrilled the [product name] exceeded your expectations. Your feedback helps us keep improving. If there's anything else we can do for you, please don't hesitate to reach out!"

**Response to 4-Star Review (with a minor complaint):**
"Thank you for the thoughtful review. We're glad you love the [benefit], and we appreciate your feedback on [complaint area]. We're working on [improvement] and would love to hear more. Please email us at support@company.com if you have suggestions."

**Response to 3-Star Review (mixed feelings):**
"Thank you for taking the time to review. We're glad you appreciated [positive aspect]. We understand [concern] wasn't what you expected. This is valuable feedback. We'd love to help—please email support@company.com so we can discuss how we can improve your experience."

**Response to 1-2 Star Review (critical):**
"We're sorry the [product name] didn't meet your expectations. This feedback is important to us. We'd like to make it right. Please email support@company.com with details about what went wrong, and we'll work to resolve it immediately. We stand behind our products."

## Quality Gates
1. **Benefit-Focused Headline**: Does headline answer "What's in it for me?" with specific benefit, not just product category?
2. **Feature-to-Benefit Translation**: Do all bullets connect features to customer benefits? Can customer understand why each feature matters?
3. **Clarity**: Can a non-expert understand the product and its benefits after reading? No jargon without explanation?
4. **Objection Handling**: Do FAQ and guarantee sections pre-answer customer hesitations (price, fit, shipping, returns)?
5. **Scannable**: Is the page organized for scanning (bullets, headings, tables) not dense paragraphs?
6. **Trust Signals**: Are there reviews, guarantees, and specs that reduce purchase risk?
7. **Conversion Rate**: Do A/B tests show improved conversion rate (add-to-cart, sales) vs. previous version?

## Examples

### Good Headline
"Stay Dry and Comfortable All Day—Waterproof Running Shoes That Breathe"

**Why this works**: Primary benefit (stay dry, comfortable), differentiator (all day), specific (waterproof + breathable), emotional appeal (comfort matters).

### Bad Headline
"Premium Waterproof Running Shoes"

**Why this fails**: No benefit stated, vague (what does "premium" mean), just features.

### Good Bullets
- Sprint 20% Faster With Responsive Cushioning (Energy Return Foam)
- Never Slip on Wet Surfaces (Grip-Traction Outsole)
- Go From Trail to Road Without Changing Shoes (Versatile Traction Pattern)
- Feel the Road With Lightweight Design (7.2 oz per shoe)

**Why this works**: Each connects feature to customer benefit; specific numbers (20%, 7.2 oz); benefits speak to runner's motivations (speed, safety, versatility, responsiveness).

### Bad Bullets
- Premium Materials
- Advanced Engineering
- Available in Multiple Colors
- Excellent Quality

**Why this fails**: Vague (what does "premium" or "excellent" mean), no specific benefit, not motivating.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Feature-Focused Copy** — Describing what the product is/has instead of what it does for the customer. Anti-pattern: "Advanced AI algorithm for predictive analytics." Better: "Know what your customers will do next, so you can sell smarter."

2. **Burying the Headline** — Leading with technical specs or product category instead of benefit. Anti-pattern: "Computer Peripheral Device." Better: "Triple Your Typing Speed."

3. **Weak Differentiation** — Copy could apply to any competitor. Anti-pattern: "Quality product at a good price." Better: "Lifetime warranty + 60-day guarantee or your money back."

4. **Too Much Text** — Walls of paragraph copy that customers skip. Anti-pattern: 1000+ word descriptions in prose. Better: 300-400 word focused narrative + scannable bullets.

5. **Ignoring Objections** — No FAQ, guarantee, or specs section. Customers with hesitations abandon cart. Solution: Pre-answer 8-10 common questions.

6. **No Review Social Proof** — Page has no customer reviews or testimonials. Reduces credibility. Solution: Feature 3-5 of your best reviews prominently.

## Anti-Patterns

1. **The Vague Promise** — "Change your life" or "Transform your world" without explaining how. Anti-pattern: "This product will revolutionize everything." Better: "Reduce your morning routine by 15 minutes—so you have time for yourself."

2. **The Jargon Dump** — Technical specs and terminology without translating to customer language. Anti-pattern: "Features dual-core processing with 16GB RAM allocation." Better: "Loads instantly; never lags even with 100+ browser tabs open."

3. **The Competitor Comparison** — Explicitly naming and criticizing competitors. Anti-pattern: "Unlike Brand X, our product actually works." Better: Use comparison chart showing features/benefits; let customer conclude.

4. **The Feature List Without Benefits** — Listing specifications as if they sell themselves. Anti-pattern: "Stainless steel body, 5-year warranty, made in Japan." Better: Connect each to benefit. "Stainless steel body means it'll outlast other models (made to last 20 years); 5-year warranty backs it."

5. **The Fake Social Proof** — Customer reviews that sound like marketing copy ("This product is absolutely amazing and life-changing!"). Customers smell inauthenticity. Better: Real reviews with specific, relatable language and minor criticisms (more credible).
