# Crisis Communication Writer

## Purpose
A crisis is any event that threatens your organization's reputation, operations, or stakeholder trust: product failure, data breach, leadership scandal, safety incident, regulatory violation. Crisis communication must happen in hours, not days, and must address multiple audiences simultaneously with tailored messaging. This skill walks you through writing holding statements (within 1 hour), full response statements, internal employee communications, customer notifications, media Q&A, social media responses, and stakeholder-specific messaging—all coordinated and consistent. The tone must be empathetic (we understand impact), factual (no speculation), and forward-looking (here's what we're doing).

## When to Use
- A significant incident has occurred or is about to become public
- Media is actively investigating or reporting
- Employees are confused about what happened
- Customers may be directly or indirectly affected
- Regulators or law enforcement are involved
- Your organization's reputation is at immediate risk
- **Do NOT use when**: incident is minor/localized (use normal customer service), you don't yet have facts (don't speculate; wait for clarity), or legal counsel advises against communication (follow legal guidance)

## Instructions

### Step 1: Assess Severity and Stakeholder Impact (30-60 minutes)
Before writing, determine: What happened? Who's affected? What do we know vs. what's speculation?

**Incident Assessment Matrix:**

| Question | Answer | Implication |
|----------|--------|------------|
| What happened? | [Be factual] | Shapes messaging tone |
| Who is directly affected? | [List stakeholders] | Determines primary audience |
| Whose safety/security is at risk? | [If anyone] | Escalates urgency; requires transparency |
| What's public vs. still confidential? | [Distinguish] | Guides what we can/can't say |
| What do we not yet know? | [Be honest] | Informs the "here's what we're doing to understand" section |
| What am I uncertain about? | [Flag] | Prevents overstatement |
| What will the media focus on? | [Anticipate] | Shapes headline narrative |
| What are stakeholders most worried about? | [Guess] | Directly address in all communications |

**Examples:**
- **Product Issue**: A batch of your software crashes randomly, losing 1-2 days of user data for affected customers. 500 customers directly impacted. Thousands indirectly (they see social media reports and lose confidence). What we know: root cause identified, we're implementing a fix. What we don't know: why this slipped through QA. Media focus: Will your quality control be seen as inadequate? Stakeholder worry: Will this happen again? Will I lose my data?

- **Data Breach**: A security incident exposes 50K customer records (email, purchase history, but NOT payment information). Breach discovered, extent unknown, attacker identity unknown. Direct impact: 50K customers. Indirect: all customers wondering if they're safe. What we know: breach occurred, we discovered it, we notified law enforcement. What we don't know: How long was data exposed? Was it misused? Media focus: How could this happen? Stakeholder worry: Is my data compromised? Will my identity be stolen?

This assessment informs every communication.

### Step 2: Write Holding Statement (<1 Hour)
A holding statement is your first public acknowledgment. It must go out ASAP to prevent a communication vacuum that invites speculation.

**Requirements:**
- Under 150 words
- Acknowledges the incident (show you're aware)
- Expresses concern for affected stakeholders (empathy)
- Confirms you're investigating/responding (action taken)
- Commits to transparent follow-up (next communication timeline)
- NO speculation, NO defensiveness, NO complex explanations

**Template:**

"We are aware of [incident description—be factual, not sensational]. We understand this [impacts/concerns] our [customers/employees/users] and we take this extremely seriously.

We have immediately [action 1: isolated the issue, paused service, contacted authorities, etc.]. Our [team/security] team is [action 2: investigating, assessing scope, working with law enforcement, etc.].

We are committed to transparency. We will provide a full statement with details and next steps by [specific time, e.g., tomorrow at 9am ET]. In the meantime, if you need support, please contact [support channel].

Thank you for your patience."

**Holding Statement Example (Data Breach):**
"We have discovered a security incident affecting approximately 50,000 of our customer records. We are deeply concerned about the potential impact on our customers and are taking immediate action. Our security team has notified law enforcement and is working with external security experts to determine the full scope and timeline of the exposure. We are committed to transparency and will share detailed information about what happened, what we're doing to address it, and steps customers can take to protect themselves. We will provide a full statement by tomorrow at 9am ET. If you have concerns, please contact our support team at support@company.com or call 1-800-555-1234."

**Timing Consideration:**
This must go out FAST. Within 1 hour of deciding to make a public statement, holding statements should be live. Delay invites media speculation.

### Step 3: Develop Full Response Statement (2-4 hours)
The full statement provides facts, acknowledges impact, and outlines your response and next steps.

**Structure (800-1200 words):**

**Section 1: Acknowledge and Apologize (100-150 words)**
- State clearly what happened (no euphemisms)
- Acknowledge the impact on affected stakeholders
- Apologize sincerely (if appropriate)
- Show you understand why this matters

Example: "On March 3rd, we discovered that customer payment information was exposed due to a vulnerability in our payment processing system. Approximately 50,000 customers' records—including email, name, and payment information—were accessed by unauthorized third parties. We understand that this breach violates the trust our customers place in us. We apologize sincerely for this incident and its impact on your security and peace of mind."

No defensiveness. No "We take security seriously" before acknowledging harm (that's seen as dismissive).

**Section 2: What We Know (200-300 words)**
Facts about the incident:
- Timeline (when did it happen, when was it discovered)
- Scope (what data was exposed, how many people affected)
- Root cause (what vulnerability enabled this)
- Whether misuse has occurred (if known)
- What's still under investigation

Example: "The breach occurred between Feb 28 and March 2 when attackers exploited a vulnerability in our payment processing system that we became aware of on March 2. We immediately patched the vulnerability and engaged external security firm CyberSecure Inc. to investigate. Based on their preliminary findings, approximately 50,000 customers' records were accessed. The exposed data includes email address, full name, shipping address, and partial payment information (last 4 digits of card, expiration date). Full payment card numbers were NOT exposed due to encryption protocols in place. As of today, we have no evidence that exposed data has been misused, but we are monitoring actively."

Be specific. Numbers matter. Distinguish between speculation and confirmed facts.

**Section 3: Immediate Actions Taken (200-300 words)**
What have you done in the last 24-48 hours?
- Contained the breach (isolated affected systems)
- Engaged law enforcement
- Hired external experts
- Notified affected customers
- Patched vulnerabilities
- Enhanced monitoring

Example: "We have taken the following immediate actions: (1) Shut down the affected payment system and migrated processing to a clean backup, (2) Engaged the FBI and filed a formal incident report, (3) Hired CyberSecure Inc., a leading forensic security firm, to investigate and audit our systems, (4) Notified all 50,000 affected customers via email with guidance on protecting themselves, (5) Offered 12 months of free credit monitoring and identity theft protection to affected customers (at no cost to them), (6) Deployed additional monitoring and intrusion detection to prevent future incidents. Current status: Our platform is secure and operational."

**Section 4: What's Next (200-300 words)**
Your action plan and timeline:
- Full investigation completion timeline
- Remediation steps beyond immediate response
- Enhanced security measures for future
- Organizational changes/accountability
- How customers will be updated
- What customers should do now

Example: "Over the next 30 days, we will: (1) Complete a full forensic investigation with CyberSecure Inc., (2) Conduct a comprehensive security audit of all systems, (3) Implement enhanced encryption across all customer data, (4) Redesign our payment processing architecture with additional security layers, (5) Expand our security team to include dedicated incident response and threat monitoring roles, (6) Implement quarterly third-party security audits going forward (vs. annual previously). We will publish a detailed post-incident report by April 15 with findings and enhanced security measures. For customers: if you haven't already, enroll in the complimentary credit monitoring we've provided; change your password on our platform; monitor your accounts for unauthorized activity and report to us and your card issuer immediately if you notice suspicious charges."

**Section 5: Commitment to Doing Better (100-150 words)**
Forward-looking statement about culture/values:
- Reaffirm commitment to customer trust
- Acknowledge this shouldn't have happened
- Show you've learned
- Commit to transparency going forward

Example: "This incident represents a failure of our security commitment. We acknowledge we fell short. We're committed to rebuilding your trust through concrete action: enhanced security, transparency about vulnerabilities and our response, and accountability. We will publish quarterly security reports so you can see our progress. We will never be complacent about security; we will treat every vulnerability as a potential crisis and respond with urgency. Your trust is our most valuable asset, and we're recommitting to earning it."

**Call to Action:**
How can stakeholders get help/more info?
- Support channel (email, phone, chat)
- FAQ/additional resources
- Timeline for next update

Example: "If you were affected by this breach, please visit our support page for credit monitoring enrollment details and guidance. If you have questions, email us at security@company.com or call our dedicated support line at 1-800-555-1234. We will provide an update on our investigation by March 15th."

### Step 4: Write Internal Employee Communication (300-500 words)
Employees will hear about this. Communicate directly to prevent rumor and ensure they can support customers.

**Structure:**

**Section 1: Respect for Their Role (50-100 words)**
Acknowledge that employees are stakeholders too:
"We know many of you will be directly or indirectly affected by this incident. Customer-facing teams will fielding questions and concerns. Employees in product and security will be working around the clock to prevent recurrence. We want you to know we value your efforts and understand this is stressful."

**Section 2: What Happened (Same facts as public statement, but tone can be more direct internally) (150-200 words)**
Be factual. Don't sugarcoat, but don't catastrophize. Example: "We discovered a vulnerability in our payment processing system that exposed approximately 50,000 customer records. The exposure occurred between Feb 28 and March 2. We've contained the issue, notified affected customers, and are working with law enforcement and external security experts to investigate fully. This is serious, but it's not an existential threat to the company. We're handling it responsibly."

**Section 3: What We're Doing (100-150 words)**
- Organizational response (who's leading, what teams are involved)
- Support available to employees (mental health resources, talking points, FAQ)
- Expectations for customer interactions (empathy, honesty, escalation paths)

Example: "Our security team is leading the investigation with external experts. Our leadership team is meeting daily to oversee response. We've established a war room with representation from Security, Legal, Customer Support, and Product. All employees are authorized to direct customer inquiries to support@company.com or escalate to your manager; do not attempt to speculate or provide technical details you're uncertain about. We're hosting daily all-hands meetings at 9am and 4pm ET for updates and Q&A—your questions matter. A FAQ with talking points is available on our intranet."

**Section 4: How You Can Help (100-150 words)**
Give employees agency:
- Listen to customers without being defensive
- Escalate appropriately
- Report concerns or additional incidents
- Help inform improvements

Example: "If you're customer-facing, your job is to listen and empathize. Customers are scared; acknowledge their concerns. Provide factual information from our public statements. Don't speculate or apologize for company policy—that's not your job. If a customer tells you something we haven't publicly acknowledged, immediately escalate to your manager and security@company.com. If you have ideas for how we can prevent this in the future, please share them; we're learning and will be redesigning our security. Thank you for your patience and professionalism."

**Section 5: What's Next (50-100 words)**
Timeline and next communication:
"We will update all employees daily for the next week, then shift to weekly updates. Please check your email and intranet for updates. If you need support—counseling, time off, flexibility in your role—talk to your manager or HR. We're in this together."

### Step 5: Develop Customer Notification (200-400 words)
Customers most directly affected need personal communication (usually email).

**Tone: Empathetic but action-focused**
Don't bury the bad news in jargon. Lead with impact and next steps.

**Template:**

**Subject Line** (needs to be serious but not panic-inducing):
"Important Security Notice—Action Recommended"
NOT: "URGENT DATA BREACH!!!" (that triggers panic and spam filter)

**Email Body:**

"Dear [Customer],

On March 3rd, we discovered a security incident affecting your account. We are writing to inform you of what happened, what it means for you, and what we're doing about it.

**What Happened**
Our security team discovered a vulnerability in our payment system that allowed unauthorized access to customer records between February 28 and March 2. Your data was among the 50,000 customer records affected. The exposed information includes your email address, name, shipping address, and the last 4 digits of your payment card (your full card number was encrypted and not exposed).

**What This Means**
While we have no evidence your data has been misused, we recommend you take precautions: monitor your credit reports, review your accounts for unauthorized charges, and change your password on our platform. Your financial institutions are also notified and monitoring for fraudulent activity on your card.

**What We're Doing**
We have immediately (1) contained the breach, (2) patched the vulnerability, (3) notified law enforcement, (4) hired external security experts to investigate, and (5) enhanced monitoring to prevent recurrence. We're also providing you with 12 months of complimentary credit monitoring and identity theft protection.

**Next Steps for You**
1. Enroll in our complimentary credit monitoring: [link]
2. Change your password on our platform
3. Monitor your credit reports and financial accounts
4. Review your recent transactions for unauthorized charges

If you notice suspicious activity, report it immediately to us (security@company.com) and your financial institution.

We apologize for this incident and the concern it causes. Your trust matters to us, and we're committed to rebuilding it through concrete security improvements. We'll provide a full investigation report by April 15th.

Questions? Contact our support team at support@company.com or call 1-800-555-1234. We're here to help.

Sincerely,
[CEO Name]
Chief Executive Officer"

### Step 6: Prepare Media Q&A and Talking Points (300-500 words)
Prepare for likely reporter questions. Anticipate skepticism and defensiveness.

**Key Talking Points** (consistent across all media interactions):
1. "We discovered the incident ourselves and took immediate action to contain it."
2. "We're being fully transparent about what happened because our customers deserve to know."
3. "We're offering complimentary credit monitoring and identity theft protection to affected customers."
4. "Security is our responsibility, and we fell short. We're committed to doing better."
5. "The full card numbers were encrypted and not exposed; email and shipping addresses were."

**Anticipated Questions and Answers:**

**Q: How did this happen?**
A: "A vulnerability in our payment processing system allowed unauthorized access between Feb 28 and March 2. This is our failure—we should have caught this sooner. We've now patched the vulnerability and are working with external security experts to ensure this doesn't happen again."

**Q: Why didn't you catch this yourself?**
A: "That's a fair question. We fell short of our own security standards. We're implementing quarterly third-party audits and expanding our security team so we catch vulnerabilities before attackers do. We're redesigning our entire payment architecture with additional security layers."

**Q: Will customers' financial information be stolen?**
A: "We have no evidence of misuse to date, but we're monitoring actively. Full credit card numbers were encrypted and not exposed. We're providing affected customers with 12 months of complimentary credit monitoring so they'll know if their identity is misused. We recommend they also monitor their own accounts closely."

**Q: What should customers do?**
A: "Enroll in the complimentary credit monitoring we've provided, change your password, and monitor your accounts for unauthorized activity. If they notice anything suspicious, report it to us and their financial institution immediately."

**Q: Is your CEO stepping down?**
A: "Our leadership team is accountable for this incident. We're focused on making it right: transparent investigation, concrete security improvements, and regaining customer trust. Personnel decisions will be made based on what's best for the company and our customers."

### Step 7: Create Social Media Response Templates (100-200 words each)
Social media will be active. Have templated responses ready, but customize tone to platform.

**Twitter/X (Direct and concise):**
"We've discovered a security incident affecting ~50K customers. We're taking this seriously: we've contained it, notified law enforcement, and are investigating with external experts. All affected customers are notified with guidance. We're committed to earning back your trust through action. [Link to full statement]"

**LinkedIn (Professional but empathetic):**
"We're writing to share a security incident and our response. We discovered a vulnerability in our payment system that exposed ~50K customer records. We take full responsibility. We've immediately contained it, notified law enforcement, and offered affected customers complimentary credit monitoring. Transparency and concrete action will rebuild trust. [Link]"

**Facebook (Community-focused, warmer tone):**
"We're writing to you with transparency about something serious. We discovered a security issue affecting some of our customers. We've taken immediate action and are providing free credit monitoring and identity theft protection to everyone affected. We're committed to doing better and earning back your trust. [Link]"

**Instagram/TikTok (If applicable; younger audience):**
[Consider whether social-first video might be more authentic than text. If so: "We messed up. Here's what happened, what we're doing about it, and what we're committing to going forward." Keep tone authentic, not corporate.]

### Step 8: Develop Stakeholder-Specific Messaging (200-300 words each)
Tailor messaging to investors, regulators, partners, and board members.

**Investor/Analyst Communication (600-800 words):**
Focus on: containment, financial impact, competitive positioning, management response.

"The incident affects approximately 50,000 customers but does not impact revenue or operations. We estimate financial impact of breach response (credit monitoring costs, investigation, security enhancements) at approximately $2-3M over next 12 months. Operationally, the platform is secure and fully operational. Competitively, we're acting faster and more transparently than many peers, which should strengthen customer loyalty long-term. Our leadership team is united and focused on response and prevention."

**Regulatory/Legal Communication (500-700 words):**
Focus on: compliance, legal obligations, cooperation.

"We are notifying all relevant regulators per legal obligations (list specific regulations: CCPA, GDPR, state notification laws, etc.). We're cooperating fully with law enforcement investigation and have engaged external counsel to ensure compliance with all regulatory obligations. A detailed breach notification report will be filed by [deadline]."

**Partner/Integration Communication (300-400 words):**
Focus on: impact on integrations, timeline for platform stability, reassurance.

"The breach does not impact our integration endpoints or partner APIs. Our platform is fully operational and secure. Partners should continue normal operations. We're informing partners out of transparency, not because your integration is compromised. We'll provide updates if any integration-related actions are needed."

### Step 9: Establish Post-Crisis Review Communication (200-300 words)
After resolution, communicate what you learned and how you're improving.

**Post-Crisis Review Statement (30-60 days post-incident):**

"30 days after discovering our security incident, we're sharing what we've learned and how we're improving. Our security investigation is complete. Here's what we found: [findings]. Here's how we're fixing it: [concrete actions with timeline]. Here's how you'll verify we're doing better: [transparency measures, quarterly reports, third-party audits]. We fell short of our own standards. Your patience as we rebuild your trust means everything."

## Quality Gates
1. **Empathy + Facts Balance**: Does messaging show you understand impact while providing factual information? Neither dismissive nor panic-inducing?
2. **Audience Customization**: Is messaging tailored to different audiences (customers, employees, investors, media) with appropriate tone and focus?
3. **Transparency**: Are you acknowledging what happened, gaps in knowledge, and what you're doing—not hiding or spinning?
4. **Action-Focused**: Do all messages include concrete next steps and timelines, not vague commitments?
5. **Consistency**: Does messaging across channels tell the same core story?
6. **Speed**: Can holding statement go out within 1 hour? Full statement within 4 hours?
7. **Accountability**: Does leadership take responsibility rather than deflecting blame?

## Common Mistakes

1. **Leading with Defense** — "We take security seriously" before acknowledging harm. Customers see this as dismissive. Solution: Lead with empathy and facts. Defend only after acknowledging impact.

2. **Speculation** — "The breach may have affected..." or "We believe attackers possibly..." Speculative language erodes trust. Solution: Distinguish facts from investigation findings. Say "We have no evidence of misuse" not "Misuse is unlikely."

3. **Delayed Holding Statement** — Waiting to have all details before saying anything. Delay invites media speculation and rumor. Solution: Get holding statement out within 1 hour, even if you only have partial facts.

4. **Over-Technical Language** — Media and customers don't understand encryption, vulnerabilities, authentication protocols. Solution: Use plain language. "Your full credit card numbers were encrypted and not exposed" not "Data was protected via AES-256 encryption protocols."

5. **Single Message for All Audiences** — Same statement for media, customers, employees, investors. Misses opportunity to address specific concerns. Solution: Customize messaging while keeping core facts consistent.

6. **No Accountability** — Blaming external attackers or "bad actors" without acknowledging your organization's responsibility. Solution: Take responsibility: "This is our failure. We should have caught this. We're committing to doing better."

## Anti-Patterns

1. **The Delayed Response** — Leadership delays response while "gathering facts." By the time you respond, media narrative is already set. Anti-pattern: Waiting 24+ hours to acknowledge a breach. Better: Acknowledge within hours, even if you don't have full scope.

2. **The Deflection** — "We take the safety of our customers very seriously" repeated five times instead of specific facts and actions. Customers hear corporate spin, not genuine response. Anti-pattern: "We maintain the highest standards of security and are committed to..." Better: "Here's what happened, here's what we're doing."

3. **The Victim Narrative** — "We too are victims of these hackers" positioning the company as equally affected as customers. Erodes credibility. Anti-pattern: "As a company, we're also struggling with this incident." Better: "Our customers' trust is what matters; we're focused on their protection."

4. **The Vague Commitment** — "We're investing in security" and "We're committed to doing better" without specifics. No timeline, no accountability. Anti-pattern: Promises to "significantly enhance" security without detailing how. Better: "We're expanding our security team by 5 people, implementing quarterly third-party audits, and redesigning payment architecture—completion by June 1st."

5. **The Inconsistent Narrative** — CEO says one thing, CFO says another, legal team says another. Internal inconsistency becomes external confusion. Anti-pattern: Leadership giving different statements to different media outlets. Better: One coordinated message across all leadership.
