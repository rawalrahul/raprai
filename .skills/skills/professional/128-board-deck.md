# Board Deck

## Purpose
A board deck is the CEO's primary tool to manage the board, get approval for key decisions, and maintain aligned stakeholder relationships. Unlike investor updates (which are informational), board decks drive decisions. They cover strategy, performance, capital needs, and governance—often in 60-90 minutes. This skill combines financial acumen, strategic communication, and governance discipline. A strong board deck answers the questions board members are thinking; a weak one leaves them confused or uninformed.

## When to Use
- **Quarterly board meetings** (standard cadence for companies with boards)
- **Major strategic decisions** (fundraising, M&A, pivots, major hires)
- **Special meetings** (if material events occur mid-quarter)
- **Do NOT use when**: Giving informal updates (use shorter formats), or hiding bad news (address it head-on in the deck)

## Instructions

### Step 1: Understand Your Board & Their Priorities
Before building the deck, know your audience.

**Board Composition & Interests**:

Map your board:
- Board chair: [Name] (interests: [strategy, cash, sales?])
- Lead investor: [Name] (interests: [growth metrics, market validation?])
- Independent director: [Name] (interests: [governance, risk, profitability?])
- CEO: [You]
- CFO or observer: [Name]

**Questions board members are thinking** (your deck should answer these):
- Are we on track with our strategy? (Or do we need to pivot?)
- Is the market still there? (Or are we chasing a shrinking opportunity?)
- Are we managing cash well? (Or burning too fast?)
- Is the team right? (Are key people in place? Do we need new hires?)
- What are the biggest risks? (What could go wrong?)
- What decision do we need to make today? (Approval? Guidance?)

### Step 2: Structure the Deck Arc
Most board decks follow a narrative arc: Where are we? How are we doing? What's next? What do we need?

**Typical Deck Structure** (60-90 min meeting):

1. **Cover slide** (0 min; during intros)
2. **Agenda** (1 min; frame the meeting)
3. **CEO narrative / State of the company** (10-15 min; big picture)
4. **Financial review** (10-15 min; actuals vs. plan, burn, runway)
5. **KPI dashboard** (5 min; quick metric snapshot)
6. **Strategic initiatives status** (15-20 min; progress on big projects)
7. **Capital & fundraising** (if applicable) (5-10 min; are we fundraising?)
8. **Risk register** (5 min; what could derail us?)
9. **Governance items** (if applicable) (5-10 min; board resolutions, policy updates)
10. **Decisions needed** (5-10 min; explicit asks; what needs board approval?)
11. **Q&A & Discussion** (10-15 min; let board ask questions)
12. **Executive session** (optional; 10 min after CEO leaves if board wants private time)

### Step 3: Develop the CEO Narrative
This is your "state of the union." Set the tone and frame the quarter.

**CEO Narrative Template** (10-15 minutes = 500-750 words = 1-2 slides with speaker notes):

```
SLIDE: CEO NARRATIVE / STATE OF THE COMPANY

Title: "State of [Company]: [Key theme for quarter]"

Subheading: "We're [1 of 3: accelerating / recovering / stabilizing] our path to [market leadership / profitability / exit]"

Narrative (suggested structure):

1. Opening (60 seconds):
   "Q1 was transformational. We signed our largest customer ($50K ARR), expanded team to 12, and raised Series A ($2.5M).
   Today I want to share our strategy, our progress, and where we need board guidance."

2. Strategic context (2 min):
   "Our market [Fintech / Healthcare / Enterprise software] is growing 35% annually. Competitors include [X, Y, Z].
   We're differentiated by [our approach, team, technology]. We're pursuing [1 of 3: land-and-expand / enterprise / bottoms-up]
   go-to-market to capture share."

3. Progress against strategy (3-4 min):
   "Our strategy is [three pillars; describe each]

   Pillar 1: Product-market fit in [vertical / use case]
   → Progress: Signed 3 customers in fintech; 87 customers overall; NPS 68 (strong)

   Pillar 2: Build repeatable sales engine
   → Progress: Sales cycle improved to 45 days (was 62); CAC improved 22%; sales team growing

   Pillar 3: Build enduring team
   → Progress: Hired VP Sales; team morale strong; 0% attrition YTD
   "

4. Q1 highlights (1-2 min):
   "[Customer A] win (largest to date); Product [Feature] shipped; Raised Series A"

5. Key challenges (1 min):
   "We face [challenge 1] and [challenge 2], which we're addressing by [mitigation 1] and [mitigation 2].
   Medium confidence in resolution by [timeline]."

6. Outlook / What's next (2 min):
   "Q2 priorities: Land 4 more enterprise customers, ship [Feature X], grow team to 15.
   We're on track to [goal]. We need your help on [ask]."

7. Closing (30 sec):
   "I'm confident in our strategy and our team. Let me walk through the details."

Speaker notes (expand on each point):
[Use notes section to provide depth for questions; not on slide itself]
```

**Tips for CEO Narrative**:
- Lead with the number 1 thing the board needs to know (not 5 things)
- Use data to support claims (not assertions)
- Acknowledge challenges alongside wins (credibility)
- Show your thinking (why this strategy, why this timeline)
- End with what you need from the board (not "any questions?")

### Step 4: Present Financial Review (Actuals vs. Plan)
The board cares about financial discipline. Show you manage money well.

**Financial Review Slide**:

```
SLIDE: FINANCIAL REVIEW

P&L Summary (Q1 2024):

| Line Item | Q1 Actual | Q1 Plan | Variance | YoY |
|-----------|-----------|---------|-----------|------|
| Revenue | $127,500 | $135,000 | -6% | +18% |
| Gross margin | 72% | 70% | +2% | +3% |
| OpEx | $171,000 | $173,000 | -1% | +12% |
| EBITDA (operating loss) | -$35,000 | -$30,000 | -17% | -15% |
| Burn rate (monthly) | $35,000 | $30,000 | +17% | +12% |

Cash:
- Balance: $560,000 (as of March 31)
- Runway: 16 months (at current burn)
- Series A: Closes April [date]; extends runway to 27 months

Unit Economics:
- CAC: $6,200 (↓ 22% YoY; improving)
- LTV: $68,000
- LTV:CAC: 11x (healthy)
- Payback period: 10 months (strong)
```

**Board interpretation**:

Board members will ask:
- Why did revenue miss plan by 6%? → "Sales cycle longer than expected (IT reviews); mitigating with SOC 2 compliance"
- Why did burn increase 17%? → "Hired VP Sales early (was planned Q2); invested in Series A compliance"
- Are we on track to profitability? → "If we grow 5% MRR monthly and control costs, breakeven in [#] months"
- Do we have enough cash? → "Series A closes in April; if it doesn't, we have 16 months of runway to figure it out"

**Best practices**:
- Show actuals vs. plan (not just actuals)
- Explain variances (especially misses)
- Show trend (quarter-over-quarter or year-over-year)
- Include cash balance and runway (board is paranoid about running out of money)

### Step 5: Create a KPI Dashboard
Board members want a snapshot of health. One slide, 6-8 key metrics, trend visible.

**KPI Dashboard Slide**:

```
SLIDE: KPI DASHBOARD

[Format: Table or visual showing traffic light status]

| KPI | Target | Actual | Status | Trend |
|-----|--------|--------|--------|-------|
| MRR | $45,000 | $42,500 | 🟡 (94%) | ↑ 8% MoM |
| Customers | 90 | 87 | 🟡 (97%) | ↑ 3% MoM |
| CAC | <$7,000 | $6,200 | 🟢 | ↓ 22% YoY |
| Net dollar retention | >100% | 112% | 🟢 | ↑ 2% QoQ |
| Gross margin | >70% | 72% | 🟢 | ↑ 2% |
| Monthly burn | <$30,000 | $35,000 | 🟡 | Stable |
| Runway | >12 mo | 16 mo | 🟢 | Improving |
| NPS (customer satisfaction) | >60 | 68 | 🟢 | ↑ 13 pts |

Legend: 🟢 On track or better | 🟡 Caution (missed or at risk) | 🔴 Off track
```

**Board reading**:
- One glance shows overall health (mostly green → good)
- Yellow items are areas to discuss (why is customer count down? why is burn higher?)
- Trends show direction (most are improving → confidence)

### Step 6: Provide Strategic Initiatives Status
What big projects are underway? What's the update?

**Strategic Initiatives Status Slide**:

```
SLIDE: STRATEGIC INITIATIVES STATUS

Initiative 1: Enter Fintech Vertical
Status: On track (🟢)
- Goal: Sign 5 fintech customers by end of Q2
- Progress: 3 customers signed; 2 more in final negotiation
- Blockers: None
- Confidence: High (expected to close 2 more by April 30)
- Next: Close remaining 2; begin partnership conversations with fintech platforms

Initiative 2: Improve Enterprise Sales Efficiency
Status: Caution (🟡)
- Goal: Reduce sales cycle to 40 days (was 62 days in Q4)
- Progress: Improved to 52 days (IT security reviews add 2-3 weeks)
- Blocker: Lack of SOC 2 compliance (customers require it)
- Confidence: Medium (SOC 2 in progress; should complete June)
- Next: Accelerate SOC 2; hire dedicated security engineer if needed

Initiative 3: Build Product for Self-Serve (SMB)
Status: At risk (🔴)
- Goal: Ship self-serve product by April (reduces CAC for SMB segment)
- Progress: Design complete; engineering delayed due to tech debt
- Blocker: Engineering capacity; higher-priority fixes pushed this to May
- Confidence: Low (may not make April target)
- Next: Prioritize self-serve in May sprint; consider contractor support if needed

Initiative 4: Expand Team to 15 by Q2
Status: On track (🟢)
- Goal: Hire 3 more team members (VP Sales, Engineer, Designer)
- Progress: VP Sales hired (starts May); Engineer in final round (offer expected March); Designer recruiting started
- Blockers: None
- Confidence: High
- Next: Extend offers; complete onboarding process
```

**What the board wants to know**:
- Is each initiative on track? (Red/yellow/green status)
- What could derail it? (Blockers identified)
- What's the next step? (Specific action)
- Is the team confident? (Honest assessment)

### Step 7: Introduce Capital & Fundraising Updates
If fundraising is happening (or considering), be explicit.

**Capital & Fundraising Slide** (if applicable):

```
SLIDE: CAPITAL PLAN & FUNDRAISING

Current status:
- Series A: $2.5M raise from [Investors] closes April [date]
- Use of proceeds:
  - $1.2M for engineering team expansion (6 hires over 12 months)
  - $800K for sales & marketing (expand sales team, launch demand gen)
  - $500K for operating capital (runway extension; office, tools, etc.)

Series B outlook:
- Timing: Q4 2024 (12-15 months from now)
- Target raise: $5-7M (estimated)
- Purpose: Scale enterprise sales; expand product; international expansion
- Success metrics to demonstrate: 5M ARR, net dollar retention >120%, enterprise customer base growing

Capital efficiency:
- Current burn: $35K/month (improving 1-2% monthly as revenue grows)
- Breakeven timeline: 24-30 months (if current trajectory holds)
- Board decision needed: Do we want to optimize for profitability or growth? (This affects spending plan)
```

**Board decision frames**:
- Profitability path: Conservative hiring, slow growth, breakeven in 18 months (lower risk, lower upside)
- Growth path: Aggressive hiring, fast growth, raise Series B, scale to $50M+ ARR (higher risk, higher upside)
- Board should explicitly choose; CEO adjusts plan accordingly

### Step 8: Present Risk Register
Board cares about risks. Show you're not blindsided.

**Risk Register Slide**:

```
SLIDE: RISK REGISTER

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation | Owner | Timeline |
|------|-------------|--------|------------|-------|----------|
| **Competitive threat**: [Competitor] enters market with similar product | Medium | High | Differentiate via [go-to-market / team / product]; build moat via integrations | CEO | Ongoing |
| **Fundraising risk**: Series B market conditions deteriorate (tech winter) | Low | High | Build path to profitability now; demonstrate strong metrics; maintain investor relationships | CFO | Q4 2024 |
| **Key person risk**: [Key engineer] leaves; impacts product velocity | Low | Medium | Cross-train team on critical systems; provide equity incentives; improve culture | COO | Ongoing |
| **Market risk**: Fintech regulation tightens; impacts fintech TAM | Medium | High | Diversify into adjacent verticals (healthcare, admin); monitor regulation | CEO/Counsel | 2024 |
| **Customer concentration**: Top 3 customers = 30% of revenue | Medium | Medium | Diversify customer base; land 10+ enterprise customers by end of 2024 | VP Sales | 2024 |
| **Burn rate**: Spend exceeds plan; depletes cash faster | Low | High | Monthly budget reviews; hiring freeze if needed; Series A extends runway | CFO | Ongoing |

Mitigations summary:
- Low severity: Monitor and respond as needed
- Medium/High severity: Active mitigation underway; board should review quarterly
- Any risks realized mid-quarter: Escalate immediately
```

**Board expectation**:
- You've identified risks (not surprised)
- You're taking action to mitigate (not passive)
- You'll tell them if situation changes (transparency)

### Step 9: Address Governance & Required Approvals
Standard board items: approvals, policies, option pool refresh, etc.

**Governance Slide** (example):

```
SLIDE: GOVERNANCE ITEMS & DECISIONS

Item 1: Series A Equity Grant to Employees
- Proposal: Refresh option pool from 15% to 18% to accommodate future hiring
- Board action: Vote to approve [date] option pool refresh
- Rationale: Need to offer competitive equity packages to attract talent
- No investor objection anticipated

Item 2: Board Observation / Advisor Status
- Proposal: Grant board observation rights to [Series A investor] per term sheet
- Board action: Vote to approve observation rights for [Name] from [Investor]
- Already agreed in term sheet; formalizing here

Item 3: Committee Formation (if applicable)
- Audit Committee: [Independent director] (lead), [Other board member]
- Compensation Committee: [Other board member] (lead), [Independent director]
- Purpose: Oversee financials, compensation, risk
- Frequency: Quarterly (before board meeting)

Item 4: Policy Updates
- Updated whistleblower policy (per SOX readiness)
- Conflict of interest policy (standard)
- Board approval requested

Item 5: Executive Session
- After CEO leaves: Board + CFO + [Independent counsel if needed]
- Confidential items: CEO performance, compensation, sensitive strategic issues
- Time: 10 minutes (optional; if board wants private time)
```

### Step 10: Close with Specific Decisions Needed
End by asking for what you need.

**Closing Slide: Decisions Needed**:

```
SLIDE: DECISIONS NEEDED FROM BOARD

1. Approval of Series A use-of-proceeds plan (above)
   - Requested: Vote to approve $2.5M allocation
   - Timeline: Immediate (we're about to start hiring)

2. Feedback on Series B strategy
   - Requested: Board input on growth vs. profitability path
   - Options:
     a) Aggressive growth (hire 20+, target $10M ARR by end of 2025)
     b) Balanced growth (hire 10-12, target $5M ARR)
     c) Profitability focus (hire 5, target breakeven by end of 2025)
   - Timeline: Decide by end of Q1 so we can finalize hiring plans

3. Approval of [Board observer] for [Series A investor]
   - Requested: Vote to grant observation rights
   - Timeline: Today

4. Risk escalation approval
   - Requested: Authority to escalate to board immediately if [Specific risk] materializes
   - Example: If burn exceeds plan by >20% for 2 consecutive months, trigger emergency meeting
   - Timeline: Approve framework today

5. Strategic guidance on fintech vertical
   - Requested: Your feedback on go-to-market strategy, partner suggestions
   - Not a vote; discussion
   - Timeline: During strategic initiatives discussion
```

### Step 11: Prepare for Executive Session
After CEO leaves, board may want to discuss sensitive topics. Have materials ready.

**Executive Session Prep** (for board chair or CFO):

```
Topics board may want to discuss privately:

1. CEO Performance
   - Is CEO the right leader? Are we getting the execution we need?
   - Compensation/incentive alignment?
   - Development areas?

2. Key Person Risk
   - Is [Key employee] a flight risk? How do we retain them?
   - Any personnel issues to address?

3. Strategic disagreements
   - Does board disagree with CEO strategy? (Fintech focus? Series B timing?)
   - Any course corrections needed?

4. Valuation & Dilution
   - Is Series A valuation fair? Are we being diluted appropriately?
   - What valuation do we expect for Series B?

5. Investor Relations
   - Are other investors causing issues?
   - Do we need to manage any board member behavior?

6. Confidential financial issues
   - Are there write-offs or adjustments that need board discussion?
   - Any material unknown issues?

FACILITATOR NOTES:
- Board chair leads discussion (not CEO)
- CFO available for financial questions
- Keep executive session to 10 min (respect time)
- Document decisions; communicate with CEO (or keep confidential based on topic)
```

## Quality Gates

1. **Narrative Coherence**: Read slides 1-6 (without notes). Do they tell a cohesive story? Or are you jumping between ideas?

2. **Board Decision Clarity**: Is it obvious what decisions the board needs to make? If not, add a "Decisions" slide.

3. **Financial Accuracy**: Do your numbers match your books? Did you get CFO/controller sign-off before presenting?

4. **Risk Completeness**: Have you identified risks that could surprise the board? If a risk materializes mid-quarter and the board didn't know about it, that's a credibility problem.

5. **Time Allocation**: Does your deck fit in 60-90 minutes? (A 40-slide deck won't work.) Cut slides, consolidate, or plan a longer meeting.

6. **Tone Consistency**: Do all slides match in tone (professional, data-driven)? If one slide is "folksy" and others are formal, update for consistency.

## Examples

### Good Board Deck Structure

Cover, Agenda → CEO Narrative (big picture; strategy; progress on 3 initiatives) → Financials (actual vs. plan; burn; runway) → KPI Dashboard (status snapshot) → Strategic Initiatives (fintech, sales efficiency, product, team) → Series A (use of proceeds; Series B outlook) → Risks (identified; mitigated) → Governance (votes needed) → Decisions (specific board actions) → Executive Session Prep (separate document)

**Why it works**: Clear narrative arc; financial rigor; decisions explicit; board knows what to expect.

### Bad Board Deck Structure

Slide 1: Financial details (P&L, balance sheet, every line item)
Slide 2: Customer list (all 87 customers with ARR)
Slide 3: CEO thoughts on market (rambling; no data)
Slide 4: Random risk (mentioned but no mitigation)

**Why it fails**: No narrative; too much detail; no decisions; board is confused about the meeting's purpose.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Too much detail**: Including every line item from the P&L. Summarize; put details in appendix.

2. **Not enough context**: Showing numbers without explaining what they mean or why they matter.

3. **Surprises**: Telling the board bad news for the first time in the meeting. Call board members before the meeting; discuss sensitive topics in private calls first.

4. **No clear decisions**: Board leaves meeting without knowing what they're approving. Always end with explicit decisions.

5. **Defensive tone**: Explaining every miss rather than owning it and moving on. "We missed plan because [external reason]" vs. "We missed plan; here's what we learned and how we'll adjust."

6. **Ignoring red flags**: Pretending a problem isn't material. Board members know the business; they'll ask if something is wrong. Better to raise it yourself.

## Anti-Patterns

1. **The kitchen-sink deck**: Every activity, every metric, every idea. Deck becomes 50 pages. No one reads it. Cut to the essential 10-12 slides.

2. **Presentation as surprise**: CEO doesn't talk to board members before the meeting; board hears bad news for the first time during presentation. Awkward; damages trust. Pre-socialize key messages.

3. **Assuming shared context**: You know your business; board may not. Don't assume they remember last quarter's initiative. Briefly recap; then show progress.

4. **No narrative flow**: Slides are a list of topics, not a story. "Financial update, product update, market update" tells no story. Instead: "We're executing on our strategy [state it]. Here's the proof [data]. Here's what we need from you [ask]."

5. **Ignoring time constraints**: Planning a 90-minute deck for a 60-minute meeting. Either condense or extend the meeting.

6. **Board performance theater**: Trying to impress the board with jargon or complexity. Clarity and honesty impress more. Be direct.
