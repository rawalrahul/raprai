# Video Script Writer

## Purpose
A video script is not a written article turned into audio. It's a choreographed performance balancing three elements: (1) audio narrative (what's said), (2) visual direction (what's shown), and (3) timing/pacing (how long each element takes). Different platforms require different scripts: YouTube rewards long-form (8-12 minutes), TikTok demands short-form (15-60 seconds), LinkedIn favors mid-form (1-3 minutes). This skill walks you through writing scripts that maximize engagement, retention, and platform-specific metrics.

## When to Use
- Creating YouTube, TikTok, LinkedIn, or Instagram video content
- Producing product demos, tutorials, or educational videos
- Shooting explainer videos or testimonials
- Creating promotional or brand videos
- Directing a film crew or solo creator on what to shoot
- **Do NOT use when**: you're writing for purely audio content (use podcast script instead), you need voiceover narration without visual direction, or you're creating live-streaming content (different format)

## Instructions

### Step 1: Understand Platform Constraints and Pacing
Different platforms have different viewer expectations and attention spans.

**Platform Breakdown**:

**YouTube (8-12 minutes ideal length)**
- Viewer expectations: Depth, value, entertainment, willingness to invest time
- Pacing: Slower, more room for detailed explanations and transitions
- Hook placement: 3-5 second hook is critical (first 5 seconds determine if viewer stays)
- Retention: Mid-roll content dips occur around 30%, 60%, and 90% marks—address with B-roll changes or narrative shifts
- CTA placement: Outro (last 30 seconds) for subscribe/like
- Monetization: Favors longer content (more ad placement)

**TikTok (15-60 seconds)**
- Viewer expectations: Entertainment, quick wins, trend-based content, fast-paced
- Pacing: Very fast (visual change every 1-2 seconds)
- Hook placement: 0-1 second hook (before they can scroll)
- Retention: Constant movement/change of visual; static shots lose viewers
- CTA placement: Overlays or verbal mention (saving last second for CTA reduces engagement)
- Monetization: Favors high-volume posting

**LinkedIn (1-3 minutes)**
- Viewer expectations: Professional value, career/business relevance, authenticity
- Pacing: Medium (business tone, less frantic than TikTok, more brisk than YouTube)
- Hook placement: 2-3 second hook (professional audience slower to engage but values quality)
- Retention: Personal connection and authenticity matter (less edited = more authentic)
- CTA placement: Verbal CTA or text overlay promoting share/comment/follow
- Monetization: Depends on engagement (comments, shares)

### Step 2: Write the Hook (0-3 seconds)
The hook determines whether viewers keep watching or scroll past. It must be platform-specific.

**Hook Formula**: [Powerful statement or question] + [Promise]

**YouTube Hook** (3-5 second audio setup):
"In the next 10 minutes, you'll learn the exact strategy that took my SaaS from $0 to $100K in MRR—and it doesn't involve hiring a huge sales team."

**TikTok Hook** (0-1 second, on-screen text or visual):
Text overlay: "I made $100K in a month with this weird trick"
Visual: Fast-cut montage or dramatic reaction

**LinkedIn Hook** (2-3 second):
"I've interviewed 50 founders about what's really holding back their companies. The answer surprised me."

**Characteristics** (all platforms):
- Specific (not "I'll share a tip" but "the exact strategy")
- Benefits-focused (promise a clear outcome)
- Searchable keywords (for YouTube SEO)
- No spoiler (save the reveal for the body)
- Authentic (not clickbaity for YouTube/LinkedIn; clickiness okay for TikTok)

### Step 3: Structure the Script in Two-Column Format
This format separates audio from visual direction, making it clear what's being said and shown simultaneously.

**Format**:
```
| TIME | AUDIO | VISUAL |
|------|-------|--------|
| 0:00-0:05 | [What the host/voiceover says] | [What's on screen—shots, graphics, text] |
| 0:05-0:10 | [Continued audio] | [Visual continues/changes] |
```

**Example** (YouTube, First 30 seconds):

| TIME | AUDIO | VISUAL |
|------|-------|--------|
| 0:00-0:03 | "In the next 10 minutes, you'll learn the exact SaaS growth strategy that took me from $0 to $100K MRR." | Wide shot of host at desk, looking at camera, confident. Upbeat music starts. |
| 0:03-0:05 | "And the best part? It doesn't require a sales team." | Cut to screen share: SaaS dashboard showing $100K MRR. Music continues. |
| 0:05-0:10 | "I'm going to walk you through exactly what I did, the mistakes I made, and the one thing that changed everything." | Cut back to host. Lean in (shows engagement). |
| 0:10-0:15 | "But first, if you haven't subscribed yet, hit subscribe—I release one growth video every week." | Host points to camera (CTA gesture). Lower third graphic: "SUBSCRIBE" |
| 0:15-0:20 | "Let's dive in." | Transition: Fast-cut montage of 3-4 previous successful companies (5 frames each) over music. Sets up credibility. |
| 0:20-0:30 | "Back in 2021, my SaaS had $0 revenue. I was bootstrapped, had no funding, and honestly, I was terrified." | Host on camera telling the story. Slightly slower pacing (audience settling in). Background subtle (desk, clean office). Music fades. |

### Step 4: Build Content Blocks With Timing Guidance
After the hook, break the video into logical content blocks.

**Content Block Structure**:
- Block Title: "Why Most SaaS Founders Fail at Growth"
- Duration: 2:15-3:45 (90 seconds)
- Key Points: 3 reasons, each explained in 30 seconds
- Audio Pacing: 1 reason per 30 seconds, with brief pauses
- Visual Pacing: B-roll change every 10-15 seconds

**Example Block**:

**BLOCK 2: The Three Reasons SaaS Founders Fail (2:15-3:45)**

| TIME | AUDIO | VISUAL |
|------|-------|--------|
| 2:15-2:25 | "Most SaaS founders I talk to make the same three mistakes. If you can avoid these, you're already ahead of 95% of people." | Host on camera. Serious, empathetic tone. |
| 2:25-2:35 | "First: They optimize for CAC (customer acquisition cost) before they understand LTV (lifetime value). They're spending $500 to acquire a customer who only stays 6 months and spends $300." | On-screen graphic: $500 CAC arrow vs. $300 revenue. Numbers in red (loss). Host voice continues over. |
| 2:35-2:50 | "You have to reverse the logic. First, extend LTV. Get customers to stay longer and spend more. Then, optimize CAC. Most people do it backwards." | Screen share: Spreadsheet showing LTV math. Host circles key metrics. Upbeat music underneath. |
| 2:50-3:00 | "Second mistake: They hire a salesperson before they validate their sales process. A salesperson can't sell a product nobody wants." | B-roll: Office clips of people collaborating. Fast-cut (1-2 second clips). |
| 3:00-3:15 | "You need a process first. You need to know: What's your ICP? What's your objection? What's your close rate? Only then do you hire someone to execute it." | On-screen graphic: Sales process framework (3 boxes: ICP → Objections → Close Rate). Host explains each. |
| 3:15-3:25 | "Third: They don't track the right metrics. They're obsessed with vanity metrics—signups, free trials—when they should be tracking what matters: paid conversions and retention." | Dashboard footage: Google Analytics. Host highlights key metrics in green, clicks away from vanity metrics. |
| 3:25-3:45 | "Here's the dashboard I use to track what actually matters. [Pause to let viewer see it] Track these four metrics, and you'll know if your growth is real or just noise." | Full-screen dashboard. Labels: "Paid Conversions, Retention Rate, CAC, LTV" appear on screen one by one (2-3 second each). |

### Step 5: Provide B-Roll Notes (Visual Descriptions for Editor)
B-roll is the footage that plays while audio continues. Be specific about what you need.

**B-Roll Description Format**:
- Location/Type: Where footage should be shot or sourced from
- Duration: How long this clip should play
- Tone: Energy level (upbeat, serious, contemplative)
- Purpose: What visual impression it creates

**Examples**:

"**B-Roll: SaaS Office Environment (0:20-0:45)**
- Source: Stock footage (Unsplash, Pexels) or film crew shoots office
- Duration: 25 seconds total
- Tone: Professional, energetic, collaborative
- Shots needed: (1) Wide of office (5 sec), (2) Close-up of team discussing at whiteboard (7 sec), (3) Laptop screen with analytics/dashboard (8 sec), (4) Team celebrating/cheering (5 sec)
- Purpose: Establish credibility and team culture before diving into growth strategy"

"**B-Roll: Fast-Cut Montage of Success Metrics (3:15-3:45)**
- Source: Screen recordings of client dashboards or templates
- Duration: 30 seconds
- Tone: Exciting, data-driven
- Shots needed: Dashboard metrics appearing one at a time, numbers rising/animating, charts ticking up
- Purpose: Visualize abstract metrics, create sense of momentum"

### Step 6: Add Lower Third Text and On-Screen Graphics
Text on screen reinforces key points and breaks up talking-head footage.

**Types of On-Screen Text**:

**Lower Third** (appears at bottom of screen):
- Host name + title (when first introduced)
- Key stat or insight being discussed
- Resource link

**Full-Screen Graphics**:
- Diagrams or frameworks
- Charts, graphs, metrics
- Step-by-step processes
- Quotes or call-outs

**Text Overlay**:
- Emphasis on key words
- Timestamps or chapter markers
- Social media handles or URLs

**Example Script With On-Screen Cues**:

| TIME | AUDIO | VISUAL | ON-SCREEN TEXT |
|------|-------|--------|-----------------|
| 2:50-3:00 | "Most founders optimize CAC before LTV. It's backwards." | Dashboard showing metrics | [ANIMATED] "CAC Before LTV = ❌" |
| 3:00-3:10 | "Here's the logic: extend LTV first, then optimize CAC." | Host gesturing, explaining | [ANIMATED] "LTV First → CAC Second = ✓" |
| 3:10-3:20 | "If your LTV is $1000, you can afford to spend $300 on CAC. But if LTV is $300, you need to drop CAC to $100." | Screen share: Math example | [Calculation displays on screen]: "LTV: $1000 → CAC Budget: $300" vs. "LTV: $300 → CAC Budget: $100" |

### Step 7: Integrate CTAs (Calls-to-Action) Strategically
Don't wait until the end to ask for engagement. Plant CTAs throughout, but strategically.

**CTA Timing**:

**Early CTA** (within first 30 seconds, YouTube only):
- "If you find this valuable, subscribe—I post growth videos every week"
- Gesture: Point to camera, confident tone
- On-screen: Lower third graphic "SUBSCRIBE"
- Purpose: Capture casual viewers who decide early

**Mid-Roll CTA** (optional, in second half):
- "This strategy worked for [Client Name], who grew from $10K to $100K MRR in 12 months. If you're doing B2B SaaS, this framework applies to you too"
- Gesture: Natural, conversational
- On-screen: Link or mention (not aggressive)
- Purpose: Reinforce relevance

**Outro CTA** (last 30 seconds):
- Primary CTA: "Subscribe for next week's strategy video"
- Secondary CTA: "Leave a comment—what's your biggest growth challenge?"
- Tertiary CTA: "Check the description for the free template I mentioned"
- On-screen: Graphics showing all three options
- Gesture: Natural (don't oversell in the last 30 seconds)

**Platform-Specific Guidance**:
- **YouTube**: Encourage subscribers hard (early + outro). End cards for related videos.
- **TikTok**: Avoid CTA in final second (kills engagement). Use text overlay or soft mentions.
- **LinkedIn**: Encourage comments/shares over subscribers. "Let me know in the comments" performs better than "subscribe."

### Step 8: Design Pacing and Rhythm
Pacing is about visual change, not just audio change.

**Pacing Guidelines**:

**YouTube (8-12 min video)**:
- Static talking-head shot: 30-60 seconds max before changing visual
- B-roll shot: 10-15 seconds per clip
- Screen share/graphics: 10-20 seconds per graphic
- Music/energy shifts: Every 1-2 minutes to maintain retention
- Transitions: 0.5-1 second (smooth fades/cuts)

**TikTok (15-60 sec video)**:
- Every 0.5-2 seconds: New shot or visual change
- Talking head: Rarely more than 5 seconds continuous
- B-roll/graphics: 1-3 second clips
- Music: Trending audio critical; synced to beat
- Transitions: Fast cuts or trendy effects

**LinkedIn (1-3 min video)**:
- More static than TikTok, less than YouTube
- Talking head: 5-20 seconds before change
- B-roll/graphics: 5-10 second clips
- Music: Subtle, not dominant
- Transitions: Smooth, professional (no TikTok-style quick cuts)

**Retention Curve Management**:
Video engagement typically dips at 30%, 60%, and 90% of video duration. Plant engagement hooks before these points:
- At 30% mark: Shift to new topic, change visual environment, introduce guest, drop a surprising stat
- At 60% mark: Interactive element (ask question, poll), change tone/energy, introduce new section
- At 90% mark: Recap or strong call-to-action

### Step 9: Provide CTA and Outro Guidance (Last 30 seconds)
The outro is your last impression.

**Outro Structure** (30 seconds):

| TIME | AUDIO | VISUAL | ON-SCREEN |
|------|-------|--------|-----------|
| [M:SS-M:30] | "If this strategy resonated, subscribe—I release one video like this every week to help you grow your SaaS faster. I'll see you next week." | Host on camera, natural smile, direct eye contact | Subscribe graphic + next video thumbnail |
| [M:30-M:45] | [Optional second CTA] "What's your biggest growth challenge right now? Let me know in the comments—I read every one." | Host leans back (relaxed), inviting tone | Comment graphic or poll |
| [M:45-END] | [Optional music/silence] | B-roll or end card showing related videos | End cards with related videos or channel link |

**Guidelines**:
- No hard sell (you've earned trust; don't blow it)
- Make CTAs easy to find (describe the action clearly)
- Acknowledge viewer time investment ("Thanks for spending the last 10 minutes with me")
- Leave on a positive note (smile, gratitude, excitement)

## Quality Gates
1. **Platform-Appropriate Pacing**: Does script match platform conventions (8-12 min for YouTube, 15-60 sec for TikTok)?
2. **Hook Strength**: Do first 3 seconds compel viewer to keep watching?
3. **Visual-Audio Balance**: Is the script directing visuals, not just providing audio over generic B-roll?
4. **B-Roll Specificity**: Can editor execute B-roll descriptions without asking clarifying questions?
5. **Retention Planning**: Are there visual/topic shifts before anticipated drop-off points (30%, 60%, 90%)?
6. **CTA Placement**: Are CTAs strategic and non-intrusive?
7. **Timing Accuracy**: Does word count and pacing match target duration?

## Examples

### Good Hook (YouTube)
"I grew a SaaS from $0 to $100K MRR in 18 months without hiring a sales team. In the next 12 minutes, I'm going to walk you through the exact playbook."

**Why it works**: Specific outcome ($100K), relevant differentiator (no sales team), promise of value (playbook), curiosity hook (how?).

### Bad Hook (YouTube)
"Today we're going to talk about SaaS growth strategies. There are a lot of ways to grow your SaaS."

**Why it fails**: Vague, no promise, no specificity, no reason to watch (applies to any SaaS video).

### Good B-Roll Description
"Fast-cut montage of SaaS dashboards showing metrics (revenue, users, churn). Each metric appears for 1-2 seconds, numbers animating upward. Music upbeat, pacing fast. Source: Screen recordings of Stripe, Intercom, Google Analytics dashboards or template graphics. Total duration: 15 seconds. Purpose: Visualize abstract growth metrics and create momentum."

**Why it works**: Specific shots, duration, tone, source, and purpose. Editor knows exactly what to create.

### Bad B-Roll Description
"Show some growth charts and numbers"

**Why it fails**: Vague, no specifics, editor has no direction.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Radio Script, Not Video Script** — Script focuses entirely on audio, no visual direction. Resulting video is boring talking head. Solution: Think in two parallel tracks: audio AND visual. Change visuals every 10-15 seconds.

2. **Ignoring Platform Constraints** — Writing a 5-minute script for TikTok (should be 15-60 seconds). Solution: Know platform expectations and constrain accordingly.

3. **Weak Hook** — Hook doesn't differentiate or compel. Viewers bounce. Solution: Test hook with 10 people. Does it make them want to watch?

4. **Vague B-Roll Descriptions** — Editor has to ask a dozen clarifying questions. Solution: Be specific—shot names, duration, tone, source, purpose.

5. **No Retention Hooks** — Video retention drops significantly at 30%/60%/90% marks without intervention. Solution: Plan topic/visual shifts before these points.

6. **Over-CTAs** — Video feels like one big ask; viewers feel sold to. Solution: Be strategic. Early subscribe + outro ask for YouTube. Comment ask for LinkedIn. Minimize for TikTok.

## Anti-Patterns

1. **The Static Talking Head** — Host talks to camera for 5+ minutes straight. No B-roll, graphics, or visual change. Boring. Anti-pattern: Entire video is host sitting at desk. Better: Host + B-roll + graphics cycling, visual changes every 10-15 sec.

2. **The Assumption of Comprehension** — Script assumes viewer understands context (jargon, industry terms) without explanation. Viewer gets lost. Anti-pattern: "Optimize your CAC" without explaining what CAC is. Better: "CAC, or customer acquisition cost, is..."

3. **The CTA Disguised as Content** — Video is mostly promotion, not value. "Buy my course, join my community, sign up for my newsletter" repeated throughout. Anti-pattern: "This strategy worked for me. Want my full course? Click here. By the way, I also offer..." Better: Deliver value first; soft CTA at end.

4. **The Pacing Mismatch** — Fast-paced audio with slow visuals (or vice versa). Creates jarring, unprofessional feel. Anti-pattern: Rapid-fire dialogue over static screenshots. Better: Match audio pacing to visual pacing.

5. **The Over-Produced** — Excessive animation, effects, and graphics make video feel corporate/fake. Loses authenticity (especially for LinkedIn). Anti-pattern: Every word animated, 20 transitions per minute, effects everywhere. Better: Minimal, purposeful graphics that enhance narrative.
