# Event Planner

## Purpose
Event planning turns vision into reality under time and budget constraints. A well-planned event runs smoothly; a poorly planned one creates stress, overspend, and poor attendee experience. This skill combines project management (timelines, checklists), financial planning (budgets, vendor negotiation), logistics (floor plans, run-of-show), and risk management (contingencies, backup plans). Success means hitting budget, timeline, and delivering the intended attendee experience.

## When to Use
- **Planning a corporate event** (conference, offsite, product launch, annual meeting)
- **Planning a personal event** (wedding, birthday, graduation)
- **Managing multiple vendors and timelines** (need coordination framework)
- **Large-scale events** (100+ attendees)
- **Do NOT use when**: Simple informal gatherings, or delegating to professional event planner (they'll have their own systems)

## Instructions

### Step 1: Create a 12-Week Countdown Timeline
Work backward from event date. Critical milestones drive action.

**Weeks 12-10 (Vision & Budget)**:
- [ ] Define event: Type, attendee count, date, location, objective, desired experience/tone
- [ ] Set budget: Total amount and allocation by category (venue, catering, AV, decor, marketing, contingency)
- [ ] Identify event lead/owner: Who makes final decisions?
- [ ] Form planning committee: Who needs to be involved in decisions?

**Weeks 9-8 (Venue & Contracts)**:
- [ ] Site visit and select venue (finalize date, capacity, setup time)
- [ ] Negotiate and sign venue contract (note: cancellation policy, setup/breakdown time, included services)
- [ ] Reserve backup venue in case primary falls through (optional but wise for large events)
- [ ] Book key vendors: Catering, AV company, florist/decor (long lead times for these)
- [ ] Arrange insurance if required (event liability, vendor cancellation)

**Weeks 7-6 (Details & Logistics)**:
- [ ] Finalize headcount and catering menu; confirm final numbers with caterer (often 2 weeks before)
- [ ] AV needs: Develop run-of-show (timeline of presentations, speakers, video)
- [ ] Determine floor plan: Layout of tables, stage, buffet, bar, exits
- [ ] Design agenda and speaker schedule (if applicable)
- [ ] Create attendee communication plan (save-the-date, invitations, reminders)

**Weeks 5-4 (Outreach & Confirmations)**:
- [ ] Send invitations; establish RSVP deadline
- [ ] Confirm all vendor details: Arrival times, parking, contact persons, setup requirements
- [ ] Finalize AV script, video playback, lighting cues
- [ ] Create name badges, programs, signage if needed
- [ ] Begin attendee communications: Reminder emails, direction emails, agenda emails

**Weeks 3-2 (Final Prep)**:
- [ ] Final headcount to caterer (usually ~2 weeks before)
- [ ] Final run-of-show walk-through with AV and venue team
- [ ] Final floor plan and table assignments
- [ ] Confirm speaker attendance and any last-minute logistical needs (laptop type, slide format, speaking time)
- [ ] Detailed timeline for event day (minute-by-minute schedule for team)
- [ ] Prepare event day binders for staff (agenda, vendor contact info, emergency procedures)

**Week 1 (Final Checks)**:
- [ ] Confirm all vendor final details: Times, contact persons, numbers, parking
- [ ] Do final walk-through at venue (check lighting, acoustics, layout, bathrooms, emergency exits)
- [ ] Brief event day team: Who does what, when? Contingency plans?
- [ ] Prepare name badges, signage, place cards
- [ ] Check weather (if outdoor); activate contingency plan if needed

**Event Day (Execution)**:
- [ ] Arrive early (2-3 hours before start)
- [ ] Walk through AV, lighting, sound check
- [ ] Verify vendor setup: Catering stations, bar, registration table
- [ ] Be present during event; manage issues as they arise
- [ ] Greet key attendees, monitor experience

**Post-Event (Weeks 1-2)**:
- [ ] Thank vendors with prompt payment
- [ ] Collect feedback from attendees (survey or interviews)
- [ ] Debrief with planning team: What worked? What didn't? Lessons learned?
- [ ] Archive budget, invoices, evaluations for future reference

**Accessibility Considerations**:
- [ ] Wheelchair accessible entrance and seating areas
- [ ] Accessible restroom locations marked
- [ ] Parking for disabled attendees
- [ ] ASL interpreter if applicable (arrange 2+ weeks ahead)
- [ ] Dietary accommodations noted on registration (vegetarian, gluten-free, allergies)

### Step 5: Build Risk & Contingency Plan
Anticipate problems. Have backup plans.

**Risk Matrix**:

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation | Contingency |
|------|-------------|--------|------------|-------------|
| Weather (outdoor event) | High | High | Check forecast 1 week out | Tent rental on standby; move indoors if possible |
| Speaker doesn't show | Medium | High | Call speaker 1 day before; confirm morning-of | Backup speaker on call; pre-recorded message video |
| AV equipment fails | Low | High | Rent from reputable vendor; test day-of | Backup projector/laptop on site; switch to presentation via TV |
| Catering runs out | Low | Medium | Have final headcount; order 10% extra | Pivot to appetizer-only format if lunch runs out |
| Severe traffic delays attendees | Medium | Low | Send directions 1 week before; note traffic apps | Start 15 min late; accept late arrivals through first session |
| Attendee medical emergency | Low | High | Know location of nearest hospital; have first aid kit | Designate staff member as medical point-person; call 911 if needed |
| Key staff becomes ill | Low | High | Cross-train 2+ people on each role | Assign backup staff; be prepared to cover roles personally |
| Sponsor last-minute cancellation | Low | Medium | Secure sponsors early; lock in commitments | Have generic logo/signage; adjust budget accordingly |

**Contingency Planning**:
For each high-probability or high-impact risk, document:
- Who decides to activate the contingency plan?
- What's the decision trigger? ("If rain amounts exceed X inches", "If speaker cancels by X date")
- Who executes? (Name, contact info, backup contact)
- Timeline: How much notice is needed? Can it be done same-day?

**Internal Debrief** (with planning team, 1-2 weeks post-event):

- [ ] Financial: Did we come in on budget? Where were overages? Underspends?
- [ ] Timeline: Did the event run on time? Where did we slip?
- [ ] Attendance: Actual vs. projected? Impact on catering, seating, AV?
- [ ] Attendee experience: What feedback did we receive? Any complaints?
- [ ] Vendor performance: Which vendors would we use again? Who to avoid?
- [ ] Staff performance: Who excelled? Who needs coaching?
- [ ] What surprised us (good or bad)?
- [ ] Top 3 things to do same way next time?
- [ ] Top 3 things to do differently next time?

**Success Metrics** (define upfront):
- Attendance rate: Actual / Invited (e.g., 70% of 300 invites = 210 attendees)
- Budget variance: (Actual spend - Budgeted) / Budgeted (e.g., 102% = 2% over budget)
- Attendee satisfaction: Average survey rating (target: 4.0+ / 5.0)
- Net Promoter Score (NPS): "Would you recommend this event to a colleague?" (target: 50+)
- Engagement metrics: Attendees who attended 2+ sessions, participated in networking, stayed full day

## Quality Gates

1. **Timeline Realism**: Have you planned an event before? Are 12 weeks enough for your event size/complexity? Adjust if needed. Large conferences may need 16-20 weeks.

2. **Budget Accuracy**: Are estimates based on actual vendor quotes or assumptions? Get quotes for major vendors (venue, catering, AV) before finalizing budget. Assumptions underestimate.

3. **Floor Plan Feasibility**: Can you walk the plan? Do bottlenecks exist (e.g., single exit, long buffet line blocking views)? Test the layout.

4. **Run-of-Show Adherence**: In past events, did you stick to times or run over? Build in 10-15 min buffers for transitions.

5. **Contingency Completeness**: For each high-risk item, can your team execute the backup plan same-day with the resources you have? If not, plan earlier.

6. **Post-Event Feedback Loop**: Is there a plan to capture attendee feedback and measure success? Without this, you can't improve next time.

## Examples

### Good Event Plan

200-person corporate conference; 12-week timeline from kickoff to event; $35K budget; detailed run-of-show with 5-min increments; vendor comparison matrices showing why caterer A was chosen; risk matrix identifying weather, speaker no-show, AV failure; contingency for each (tent, backup video, backup projector); post-event survey template ready to deploy; success metrics defined (70% attendance, <5% budget variance, 4.0+ satisfaction).

**Why it works**: Comprehensive; anticipates problems; has backup plans; measures success.

### Bad Event Plan

"Event is [Date]. Budget is $30K. Venues: [List 3 without comparison]. Catering: TBD. Agenda: TBD. Please confirm."

**Why it fails**: No timeline; no vendor selection rationale; budget not detailed; no contingency thinking; no way to measure success.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Too-short timeline**: Trying to plan a large event in 6 weeks when key vendors need 10-12 weeks' notice. Start earlier.

2. **Budget underestimate**: Not including contingency (typically 10%), forgetting delivery fees, underestimating catering per-person costs. Get itemized quotes.

3. **No floor plan**: Assuming tables will work out day-of; leads to unexpected bottlenecks. Draw it out first.

4. **Vague run-of-show**: "Keynote happens mid-morning"; no specific time or AV cues. Leads to confusion. Be specific: 9:05am, 30 min, test slides at 9:04.

5. **Ignoring contingencies**: Only one speaker, no backup. One AV projector with no spare. Flooding without tent. Plans fail to survive reality.

6. **Not closing the feedback loop**: After event, never collecting feedback or asking "what went wrong?" so you repeat mistakes next time.

## Anti-Patterns

1. **The perfectionist planner**: Spending 40 hours on event for 50 people. 80% of benefit comes from 20% of effort. Focus on what attendees experience; delegate or skip minutiae.

2. **Over-engineering**: Selecting the most expensive options ("only the best") when mid-range vendors deliver same results. Budget wisely.

3. **Last-minute scramble**: Not following the timeline, deciding things a week before, activating contingency plans. Stress and lower quality. Start on time.

4. **Scope creep**: Event grows from 100 to 200 attendees without budget adjustment. Catering, venue, AV all need to scale. Lock scope and budget early.

5. **Communication failure**: Attendees don't know start time, location, parking. Send clear, early communications with details and maps.
