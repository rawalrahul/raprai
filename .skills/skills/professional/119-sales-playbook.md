# Sales Playbook

## Purpose
A sales playbook is the team's collective intelligence: buyer profiles, qualifying criteria, messaging, scripts, and tactics proven to move deals. Strong playbooks translate domain knowledge into teachable, repeatable processes. New reps move faster; experienced reps become more consistent. The playbook also provides accountability—when deals don't close, you can diagnose whether the process was followed or the buyer wasn't a fit. This skill combines market research, call/email templates, deal-closing frameworks, and competitive intelligence.

## When to Use
- **Onboarding new sales reps** (playbook is the curriculum)
- **Scaling the sales team** (processes ensure quality as headcount grows)
- **Launching a new product or market segment** (need new qualifying criteria, messaging, battlecards)
- **Improving win rates** (auditing playbook adherence to find gaps)
- **Do NOT use when**: Responding to one-off customer situations (use judgment calls), or creating generic content marketing (playbook is for sales, not demand gen)

## Instructions

### Step 1: Define Buyer Personas (3-5 Key Types)
For each persona, document:
- **Title and role**: Exact job function
- **Goals**: Top 3 business objectives this person cares about
- **Challenges**: Pain points they face
- **Buying criteria**: What matters to them in a vendor decision
- **Influence level**: Do they decide, recommend, veto, or just use?
- **Tech stack & tools**: What they currently use
- **Timeline sensitivity**: Do they move fast or slow? Why?

**Example Persona: CFO at Mid-Market SaaS**

**Title**: Chief Financial Officer, B2B SaaS ($50-250M ARR)
**Goals**:
- Improve operational efficiency and reduce headcount by 15% without loss of output
- Upgrade finance stack to reduce manual work and improve visibility to cash flow
- Better forecasting and variance analysis for quarterly planning

**Challenges**:
- Finance team is manually consolidating data from 6+ systems (ERP, CRM, HCM, etc.)
- Month-end close takes 5 days; too slow for agile planning
- Spreadsheet-based forecasting is error-prone and siloed by department

**Buying Criteria**:
- ROI calculator: "How much manual work hours does this save?" (Wants to see headcount reduction)
- Integration: "Does it plug into our existing ERP and BI tool?"
- Time-to-value: "How long until we're in month-end close within 2 days?"
- Support: "Do you have a dedicated implementation partner?"

**Influence level**: Economic buyer (approves budget); often defers implementation details to Controller
**Tech stack**: NetSuite ERP, Anaplan for planning, Tableau for BI, Workday HCM
**Timeline sensitivity**: Fast-moving; often operates under board-mandated efficiency targets. Deals move in 3-6 months if ROI is clear.

### Step 2: Define Ideal Customer Profile (ICP)
Aggregate personas into a firmographic and behavioral profile. This defines who you should pursue and who you should pass.

**ICP Criteria** (weighted by importance):

1. **Company size** (Revenue, Headcount, or ARR): $50-500M revenue | 200-2000 employees
2. **Industry/Vertical**: SaaS, fintech, healthcare tech (avoid regulated heavily or early-stage)
3. **Use case fit**: Have finance consolidation or forecasting pain (not sales ops optimization)
4. **Technology maturity**: Modern cloud stack; willing to integrate APIs
5. **Budget readiness**: Typically funded by finance org or tech budget (not cost-cutting mode)
6. **Geography**: US-based (for now); English-speaking teams
7. **Company health**: Growing (not contracting); recent funding or positive cash flow

**Disqualifiers**: Heavily regulated (banking, insurance); Excel-only culture; budget-constrained

### Step 3: Design the Qualification Framework
Choose one: MEDDIC, BANT, or SPIN. Each has strength for different sales cycles.

**MEDDIC** (for complex B2B sales, 6-12 month cycles):
- **Metrics**: What business outcome do they want to achieve? (Reduce close time from 5 to 2 days)
- **Economic Buyer**: Who controls the budget and final approval?
- **Decision Criteria**: What are their must-haves vs. nice-to-haves?
- **Decision Process**: What steps are needed? Committee review? Board approval?
- **Identify Pain**: What's the business pain (not feature desire) that motivates change?
- **Champion**: Who inside advocates for us? Has credibility and authority?

**BANT** (for mid-market, 3-6 month cycles):
- **Budget**: Do they have funding approved? How much? (Approved by CFO for $500K tech initiative)
- **Authority**: Who makes the final decision? (CFO + CIO together; CFO owns the buy)
- **Need**: What's the business need? (Month-end close is 5 days; target 2 days)
- **Timeline**: When do they want to implement? (By Q1 for board reporting on efficiency gains)

**SPIN** (for complex sales with skeptical buyers):
- **Situation**: Understand their current state (5-day close, 6 systems)
- **Problem**: Identify their problem without assuming (manual work, visibility gaps, error risk)
- **Implication**: Help them see consequence of problem (Board scrutiny if forecast is wrong; finance team burnout)
- **Need-Payoff**: Help them articulate desired outcome (2-day close, one source of truth, confidence in forecast)

Example MEDDIC qualification for your SaaS:

**Metrics**: "How would you measure success? If we could get you to a 2-day close by Q1, would that be sufficient, or are you targeting something else?"
**Economic Buyer**: "Beyond the CFO, who else signs off on finance tool purchases?"
**Champion**: "Has anyone on your team used modern finance platforms at previous companies?"
**Pain**: "Tell me about the last month-end close. What was the hardest part?" (Listen for consolidation, errors, cycle time)
**Process**: "Walk me through your approval process for new tools. Would this go to IT, or is it finance-owned?"

### Step 4: Build Objection Handling Scripts
List common objections and proven responses. Structure: Acknowledgment + Reframe + Evidence + Question.

**Objection 1: "We're not ready to change our processes."**
**Response**: "That makes sense—change is real work. The CFOs we work with typically feel the same way initially. The key is: if your team is spending 40 hours a month on consolidation, and this tool can cut that to 10 hours, you're not really changing process; you're automating what you already do. Does automation of your current close process sound realistic?"
[Goal: Separate "process change" fear from "tool adoption" reality. Reframe as efficiency, not transformation.]

**Objection 2: "Your price is too high. We'd rather build it ourselves or use a cheaper alternative."**
**Response**: "That's a fair consideration. Most finance leaders have explored that. What I'd suggest: the real cost isn't the software; it's your team's time to build and maintain a solution. For a finance team of 8-10, that's $2-3M/year in labor. Our tool costs $X/year and saves you 200+ hours annually. But I understand the upfront sticker shock. What if we modeled out total cost of ownership—tool plus team time—for your scenario? That sometimes shows the clearer picture."
[Goal: Shift from price to value. Offer concrete ROI analysis.]

**Objection 3: "We need to see a competitor's implementation or talk to someone in our industry."**
**Response**: "Absolutely, that's smart diligence. I have a customer, [similar company in fintech], that went live in 8 weeks and cut their close cycle by 3 days. They're open to a reference call. I'll set that up. Before that, can I ask: what specific concern do you want to validate with a peer? That way I can brief them so you get the most useful conversation."
[Goal: Provide proof without ceding to objection. Prime the reference call for success.]

### Step 5: Create Competitive Battlecards
For each key competitor, document:
- **Strengths**: What they're genuinely good at
- **Weaknesses**: Where they lose deals to you
- **Typical customer**: What buyer profile they serve best
- **Pricing**: Their model and typical deal size
- **Integration**: What systems they work with
- **Sales messaging**: How they position themselves
- **Our counter-positioning**: How we differentiate

**Battlecard Example: Competitor X (Modern Finance Platform)**

**Their Strengths**: Strong UX; solid Salesforce integration; some real-time dashboarding
**Their Weaknesses**: Limited ERP integrations; slow implementation (16+ weeks); limited customization for complex consolidation
**Typical customer**: Modern SaaS, tech-first, smaller deals ($50-150K ACV)
**Pricing**: $50K/year for 20 users; per-user seat model (scales expensively)
**Our differentiation**:
- "Competitor X works great if you're all-in on Salesforce. But most finance teams use NetSuite or SAP. We integrate deeply with both, saving you weeks of API work."
- "Their implementation takes 16 weeks. We target 8 weeks with pre-built templates for your close process."
- "They charge per seat, which gets expensive as your team scales. We charge by data volume, so you can expand teams without hitting a cost ceiling."

### Step 6: Build Email Cadence & Sequences
Map out multi-touch sequences for different deal stages. Include: cold outreach, follow-up, value prop re-engagement, and check-in.

**Cold Outreach (Email 1 of 5)** — Goal: Get a 15-minute discovery call
**Subject**: "Finance Close Process Visibility for [Company Name]"
**Body**:
"Hi [Name],

I came across [Company Name] because we've been working with several SaaS companies in your space who were struggling with month-end close visibility.

Most finance teams we talk to spend 3-5 days closing the books—manually consolidating GL entries, variance analysis, forecast updates—all from spreadsheets and disparate systems.

I thought you might be dealing with something similar. If you are, I'd love to understand your current process and see if there's a way we could help streamline it.

Are you open to a 15-minute call next week?

Best,
[Name]"

[Smart: Specific pain point; brief; invitation, not pitch]

**Follow-up (Email 2, 3 days later)** — Goal: Gently persist
"Hi [Name],

Wanted to make sure my last note didn't get lost in the shuffle. I have two 15-minute slots on [dates]; either works for me if you'd like to chat.

No pressure if it's not a good time; I'll check back in a few weeks.

Best,
[Name]"

**Engagement (Email 3, 5 days later if no response)** — Goal: Add value; re-engage
**Subject**: "[Industry] Finance Leaders on Month-End Automation (3-minute read)"
"Hi [Name],

I came across this research from Gartner on how finance teams are automating month-end processes. Thought it might be useful.

[Link to brief PDF or article]

One insight: companies that automate consolidation typically see a 40% reduction in cycle time within 6 months.

If this is a priority for your team, I'd be happy to share how other companies in [industry] have approached it.

Talk soon,
[Name]"

[Smart: Offers value; uses proof; soft ask]

**Re-engagement (Email 4, if they viewed Email 3)** — Goal: Calendar the call
"Hi [Name],

Thanks for checking out that research. Curious: is month-end visibility a priority for your finance team this year?

I ask because two companies we work with—[Company A] and [Company B]—just wrapped implementations that cut their close cycle in half. If it's relevant, I'd love to show you what they did.

Otherwise, I'll stop bothering you. But if it's worth a conversation, let's grab 15 minutes next week.

[Calendar link]

Best,
[Name]"

### Step 7: Write Discovery Call Script
Structure: 1) Build rapport, 2) Understand situation, 3) Identify pain, 4) Explore impact, 5) Check fit, 6) Next steps.

**Opening** (2 min):
"Thanks for taking time today. I know you're busy, so I'll be respectful of your time. My goal is to understand your current finance close process and see if what we do might be relevant. Sound good?"

**Situation** (3-4 min):
"Tell me about your close process. How many days does it typically take, and what are the biggest time sinks?" [Listen. Don't pitch.]

**Problem Identification** (3-4 min):
"Of those challenges, which one causes the most friction for your team or your executives?" [Dig into consequences, not just the problem.]

**Implication** (2 min):
"If we could solve [pain], what would that mean for your team? More time on strategy? Less month-end stress?" [Get them to articulate value.]

**Fit Check** (2 min):
"Just to make sure we're on the same page: If we could get you to a 2-day close without a ton of process change, would that be worth a deeper conversation?" [Qualify: Is this real need + real budget?]

**Next Steps** (1 min):
"If it does sound relevant, I'd like to show you how we've solved this for similar teams. Would a 30-minute demo next week make sense?" [Move to next stage clearly.]

### Step 8: Build Demo Flow & Talking Track
Demos should flow: Problem → Solution → Proof. Not: Feature dump.

**Demo Narrative** (30 min):

1. **Context** (2 min): "You mentioned your close cycle is 4 days, heavily manual. What if I showed you how [Company similar to you] cut that to 1.5 days with just configuration, no process change?"

2. **Problem Walkthrough** (3 min): "Walk through their old close process on screen" — Consolidate GL from 3 systems, manual variance, send to CFO, revise, re-send. "Sound familiar?"

3. **Solution Walkthrough** (15 min): Live system demo, focused on their pain points:
   - Automatic GL consolidation (show data flowing in)
   - Variance analysis dashboard (pre-built templates)
   - Collaboration features (comments, approvals, audit trail)
   - Integration to their ERP (show API flow)

4. **Proof** (5 min): "Here's what [similar customer] achieved: 2 days saved/month, 99.8% accuracy, zero manual errors in GL."

5. **Next Steps** (5 min): "Next is a technical fit call with your IT/ERP team, then we scope implementation. Typically 8 weeks soup-to-nuts. Any concerns?"

**Key rule**: Demo their use case, not your tool. Use their data if possible (sandbox environment). Show before/after for their specific process.

### Step 9: Define Deal Close Tactics & Urgency Levers
Document proven close techniques:

**Urgency Levers**:
- **Budget reset**: "Budget cycles reset Jan 1. If we're going to get you live in Q1, we need to finalize terms by mid-Dec."
- **Seat changes**: "Your team grew 2 people this year. Our pricing will increase in Q2. If we can get you live before then, you lock in today's rate."
- **Competitive threat**: "Two of your peers just went live with our solution. The longer you wait, the faster they pull ahead on efficiency."

**Close Techniques**:
1. **Assumptive close**: "When we implement in Q1, we'll need your IT team available for 2 weeks. Who should I coordinate with?"
2. **Alternative close**: "We can start with Finance consolidation and add forecasting in year 2. Or go all-in and tackle both in the first wave. What makes sense for you?"
3. **Takeaway close**: (Use sparingly) "I sense some hesitation. We can walk away here and you stay with your current close process. Or we can schedule the technical fit call and move forward. Which?"

### Step 10: Establish Win Rate Benchmarks & Success Metrics
Define what success looks like for the sales process:

**Pipeline metrics**:
- % of pipeline from ICP (target: 80%+)
- Average deal size (track by segment)
- Sales cycle length (target: X days from discovery to close)
- Win rate vs. competitors (track separately)
- Customer acquisition cost (CAC) payback period

**Rep performance metrics**:
- Quota attainment (%)
- Number of qualified opportunities per month
- Average deal size
- Win/loss ratio
- Time-to-close for won deals

**Playbook adherence metrics**:
- % of reps following qualification framework (MEDDIC, BANT)
- % of opportunities with documented business case before demo
- % of demos using the approved demo narrative
- Customer health score at close (predicts expansion/retention)

Example benchmark: "Reps should achieve 40% win rate against direct competitors, 60% against feature-lite alternatives, with average deal cycle of 90 days and $150K average contract value."

## Quality Gates

1. **ICP Clarity Test**: Can a new rep, given a list of 10 companies, correctly identify which 6-7 are ICP and which 3-4 aren't? If not, ICP definition is too vague. Add firmographic or behavioral detail until it's unambiguous.

2. **Objection Coverage**: Have you heard these objections on actual calls? List the 5-7 most common. If playbook has 12 objections but reps only encounter 3, priorities are misaligned.

3. **Persona-to-Message Alignment**: Do your email templates and demo flows speak to the pain points in your personas? If CFO persona cares about ROI but demo focuses on UI, misalignment exists.

4. **Competitive Rigor**: Are battlecards based on actual customer conversations and win/loss data, or guesses? Conduct quarterly win/loss interviews to update.

5. **Script Authenticity**: Do scripts sound natural when read aloud? Have a rep record themselves delivering the discovery call script. If it sounds robotic, rewrite for conversational tone.

6. **Benchmark Reality**: Are KPI targets based on team performance or aspirational? If top reps achieve 60% win rate but target is 75%, target is unrealistic. Set benchmarks on top 25% performance.

## Examples

### Good Qualification in Action

**Email**: "Hi Sarah, I came across ZetaBank because we work with several fintech firms managing cross-border payments. Your close cycle is likely a headache—lots of manual reconciliation across corridors. Do you deal with that?"

**Discovery call**: "Tell me about your month-end close. How many days? What's the hardest part?" [Sarah: "5 days; GL consolidation from 8 entities."] "And if you could cut that to 2 days, what would your team do with the freed-up time?" [Sarah: "Finally have time for analysis instead of firefighting."]

**Fit check**: "So if we could get you to 2 days without changing your GL processes, that'd be worth exploring further?" [Sarah: "Absolutely. And we have budget approved."] [QUALIFIED: Pain is real, timeline exists, economic buyer confirmed.]

### Bad Qualification in Action

**Email**: "We have a great solution for finance teams. Let's chat."

**Discovery call**: Reps launches into demo without understanding their process. "Here's what our tool does..." [No connection to Sarah's 5-day close problem.]

**Outcome**: Demo doesn't resonate; deal stalls; Sarah's objection becomes "not a fit."

## Common Mistakes

1. **Persona without persona research**: Guessing what buyers want instead of interviewing them. Spend 3 hours on customer calls; update personas quarterly based on real feedback.

2. **Qualification framework misuse**: Having MEDDIC on paper but reps skip it in practice. Train reps monthly on MEDDIC discipline. Review calls/notes to ensure Economic Buyer, Metrics, and Champion are always documented.

3. **Objection scripts that don't match your reality**: Scripting objections you rarely hear. Focus on the top 5. Quality beats quantity.

4. **Demo fidelity drift**: Initial playbook has a tight demo flow; after 6 months, reps are improvising and showing off features. Audit quarterly. Have managers sit in on demos and score against the playbook.

5. **Ignoring deal velocity**: Playbook works, but deals take 180 days. Diagnose: Is it stakeholder alignment? Budget timeline? Competitive pressure? Add velocity tactics (urgency levers, timeline check-ins) to shorten cycle.

6. **Not measuring playbook adherence**: You have a playbook but don't track if reps are using it. Set up a simple scorecard: Did they qualify with MEDDIC? Did they do a 15-min discovery before demo? Track weekly.

## Anti-Patterns

1. **The 200-page playbook nobody reads**: Distill to essential scripts, frameworks, and examples. If it's more than 30-40 pages, it's too long. Link to supporting docs (market research, case studies).

2. **Playbook assumes all deals are the same**: Different deal sizes and buyer maturity levels need different flows. Create "fast-track" (warm inbound, obvious fit) and "complex" (large deals, multiple stakeholders) playbooks.

3. **Sales-operations misalignment**: Sales playbook defines a 90-day cycle, but operations hands over to CS at day 60 (before ramp is complete). Coordinate so playbook and operations timelines align.

4. **Ignoring rep feedback**: Reps flag that Email 3 template doesn't resonate; you ignore it. Playbooks should evolve based on field feedback. Monthly: gather rep input on what's working and what needs updating.
