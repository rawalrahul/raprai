# Podcast Show Notes Writer

## Purpose
Show notes are the written record of your podcast episode. While the audio is ephemeral (listened to once, hard to search or reference), show notes are evergreen content that: (1) improve podcast discoverability through search and podcast directories, (2) help listeners find specific segments via timestamps, (3) provide reference material listeners can come back to, and (4) give you shareable content for social media and email. Comprehensive show notes can double the reach of an episode. This skill walks you through writing show notes that maximize discovery and listener value.

## When to Use
- Publishing every podcast episode (non-negotiable; show notes are part of every release)
- Distributing across multiple podcast platforms (Spotify, Apple Podcasts, etc.)
- Driving traffic from podcast directories and search engines (SEO matters for podcasts)
- Building email/social content library (show notes are the source of all derivative content)
- Hosting transcripts for accessibility (deaf/hard-of-hearing listeners, context search)
- **Do NOT use when**: episode hasn't been fully produced/reviewed yet (wait for final audio), you're time-constrained (do a minimal version, at minimum: title, summary, guest bio, resource links), or podcast is internal only (show notes still matter for internal search and reference)

## Instructions

### Step 1: Craft Searchable Episode Title (<60 characters)
The title is the first impression and the primary SEO element. It must be search-friendly while being attention-grabbing.

**Formula**: [Benefit/Question] + [Key Topic] + [Guest Name (optional)]

**Bad Titles** (vague, not searchable):
- "Episode 42" — no SEO value; not memorable
- "How to Be Better" — too vague; what are we improving?
- "Our Latest Insights" — generic; could apply to any episode

**Good Titles** (specific, searchable, compelling):
- "How to Negotiate Your Salary (Even in a Down Market)" — benefit (negotiate), context (down market), searchable
- "AI for Therapists: New Tools, New Ethics" — topic (AI), audience (therapists), tension (ethics)
- "Why Your Startup Fails (And How to Avoid It)" — emotional angle (failure), solution (how to avoid)
- "Building a 7-Figure Coaching Business With Amy Chen" — benefit (7-figure), audience (coaches), guest name
- "The Hidden Costs of Burnout: A Neuroscientist Explains" — specific angle (neuroscience), benefit (understanding costs)

**SEO Optimization**:
- Include target keyword (the term listeners/searchers would use)
- Keep under 60 characters (fits in most podcast directory displays)
- Use power words ("How to," "Why," "The Truth About," "Surprising")
- Avoid clickbait that doesn't match content

### Step 2: Write a One-Line Hook (20-40 words)
The hook appears in podcast directory listings and email previews. It must intrigue without spoiling.

**Formula**: [Problem or Promise] + [Surprise or Benefit]

**Examples**:
- "Most founders fail in their first 5 years. The ones who don't have one thing in common—and it's not what you think."
- "You've heard burnout is about working too hard. A neuroscientist reveals the real culprit (and how to fix it)."
- "Negotiating your salary is easier than you think—if you know the three phrases that always work."

**Characteristics**:
- Specific, not generic
- Creates curiosity (not answered by the title alone)
- Relevant to target listener
- No spoilers (save the reveal for the episode)

### Step 3: Write Episode Summary (150-200 words)
The summary is where listeners decide if they should invest 30-60 minutes. It must be concise, benefit-focused, and honest.

**Structure:**

**Opening Sentence**: The core promise or problem
"In this episode, we explore the neuroscience of burnout and why willpower isn't the solution."

**Body (3-4 sentences)**: Key takeaways or topics covered
"We discuss why burnout is a physical phenomenon (not just feeling overwhelmed), the role of your nervous system, and three concrete practices to recover. Our guest, Dr. Sarah Chen, shares research from her lab at Stanford and her own journey recovering from severe burnout."

**Closing Sentence**: Who this is for
"This episode is essential listening for anyone in high-stress roles—doctors, lawyers, entrepreneurs, caregivers—and anyone who's ever wondered if they're just 'not built for stress.'"

**Example (Full Summary)**:
"Burnout isn't about being lazy or weak—it's a neurobiological state. In this episode, Stanford neuroscientist Dr. Sarah Chen explains the science of burnout, why typical solutions (just work less, meditate more) often fail, and three evidence-based practices that actually reset your nervous system. She shares her own story of recovering from severe burnout and why she's now dedicating her research to helping high-performers maintain health while pursuing ambitious goals. If you're burning out or worried you might be, this episode provides both understanding and actionable solutions. Learn the hidden costs of ignoring burnout signals, and how to tell the difference between healthy stress and harmful burnout."

**Guidelines**:
- Write for people who've never heard your podcast
- Benefits over features (not "We talk about X" but "You'll learn X and here's why it matters")
- Honest about episode content (don't oversell)
- 150-200 words (enough to be informative, short enough to read in 30 seconds)

### Step 4: Add Guest Bio (if applicable) (100-150 words)
Who's talking? Why should listeners care?

**Structure:**

**Name and Title**: Immediately clear
"Dr. Sarah Chen, Neuroscientist at Stanford University"

**Why They Matter** (2-3 sentences): Credentials and expertise
"Dr. Chen earned her PhD in neuroscience from UC Berkeley and has spent 12 years researching stress, burnout, and resilience. Her research has been published in Nature Neuroscience and featured in The New York Times and Harvard Business Review."

**Personal Touch** (1-2 sentences): Something human, not just credentials
"She's also been through severe burnout herself, which sparked her research focus. Outside the lab, she's an ultramarathon runner (which, as she jokes, is either a sign of great stress management or the source of her own burnout)."

**How to Connect** (1 sentence): Website, social, newsletter
"You can find her research and free resources at SarahChenNeuroscience.com or follow her on LinkedIn."

**Example (Full Bio)**:
"Dr. Sarah Chen is a neuroscientist at Stanford University studying stress, burnout, and resilience. She earned her PhD at UC Berkeley and has spent 12 years researching how our brains respond to chronic stress. Her work has been published in leading journals and featured in major media. She's deeply interested in this topic not just as a researcher but as someone who experienced severe burnout while pursuing her PhD—and who had to learn recovery the hard way. She's now helping others understand burnout's science and how to actually recover. Find her at SarahChenNeuroscience.com or on LinkedIn."

**Note**: Always ask guests for bio approval before publishing.

### Step 5: Create Timestamps and Topic Labels (300-400 words total)
Timestamps help listeners jump to segments they care about. They also provide structure that improves SEO.

**Format**: [MM:SS] Topic Name

**Granularity**: Every 5-10 minutes of audio, depending on episode structure. Aim for 8-15 timestamps per 45-60 minute episode.

**Naming Convention**: Benefit-focused, scannable, searchable
- Good: "03:15 The Neuroscience of Burnout (Why Rest Doesn't Always Help)"
- Bad: "03:15 Sarah talks about science stuff"

**Example (45-minute episode)**:

00:00 Intro: Why burnout is a neuroscience problem, not a character flaw
02:45 Guest introduction: Dr. Sarah Chen's background and what sparked her research
05:30 What is burnout? (Definition beyond "I'm tired")
07:15 The neurobiology of stress: How your nervous system gets stuck
09:45 The stress recovery paradox: Why rest alone doesn't fix burnout
12:30 The three stages of burnout (and where intervention still works)
15:00 Cortisol, adrenaline, and why you can't "meditation your way out" of burnout
18:20 Personal story: Sarah's own burnout journey (and what she learned)
21:45 Solution #1: Nervous system reset practices (vagal breathing and cold exposure)
25:30 Solution #2: Social connection and why it's neurobiologically crucial
28:15 Solution #3: Movement and exercise (the research is shocking)
31:00 How to tell if you're in burnout vs. just stressed
33:30 For leaders: How to spot burnout in your team before it's too late
36:15 Preventing burnout: What the research says about sustainable high performance
39:00 Rapid-fire Q&A: Caffeine, sleep, meditation, and medication
42:15 Resources Sarah recommends (books, apps, research papers)
44:30 Outro and call-to-action

**Guidelines**:
- Start with intro segment
- Capture topic shifts (listener uses timestamps to navigate)
- Include guest stories/personal revelations (listeners jump to these)
- Note big promises and resolutions (e.g., "Solution #1")
- End with resources or call-to-action
- Be consistent in format (so listeners know what to expect)

### Step 6: Highlight Key Takeaways (3-5 bullets)
Distill episode's core insights into scannable bullets.

**Formula**: [Insight] that [Why It Matters]

**Examples**:
- "Burnout is a neurobiological state where your nervous system stays in 'fight or flight' even when the threat is gone—understanding this reframes burnout as a physical problem, not a personal failure"
- "Rest alone doesn't fix burnout; your nervous system needs active recovery practices like cold exposure, breathwork, or social connection to downshift"
- "The people most vulnerable to burnout are high-performers who are great at pushing through stress signals; recognizing these signals early is key"
- "Leaders can spot burnout in their teams by watching for decreased creativity and humor (the first cognitive casualties of burnout)"
- "Sustainable high performance requires 'green zone' periods—times of active recovery and nervous system downregulation—not just grinding"

**Characteristics**:
- Specific (not "Burnout is bad")
- Memorable (something the listener didn't expect)
- Actionable (something they can do with this knowledge)
- 5-10 words each (scannable)

### Step 7: Compile Resource Links (as mentioned in episode)
Listeners want to dive deeper. Provide a curated resource list.

**Format**: [Resource Name] by [Author] - [Brief Description] ([Link](URL))

**Categories**:
- Books/Reading
- Research Papers/Studies
- Tools/Apps
- Websites/Blogs
- Courses/Workshops
- Guest's Work

**Example**:

**Books**:
- "The Upstairs Brain" by Sarah Chen (Dr. Chen's forthcoming book on neuroscience of resilience) — Pre-order available at [link]
- "Peak Performance" by Brad Stulberg and Steve Magness — One of the foundational books on sustainable high performance
- "Why Zebras Don't Get Ulcers" by Robert Sapolsky — The classic on stress, nervous system, and disease

**Research**:
- Chen et al. 2023. "Chronic stress and neural plasticity" in Nature Neuroscience — The research behind burnout's neurobiological impact
- Porges 2015. "Polyvagal Theory" — The foundational theory on vagal tone and nervous system recovery

**Tools/Practices**:
- Wim Hof Method (breathing and cold exposure practice) — Recommended by Sarah for nervous system reset
- Headspace or Insight Timer — For guided nervous system downregulation practices
- HRV Tracker app (Apple Health or Whoop) — To measure your nervous system recovery

**Follow the Guest**:
- Dr. Sarah Chen's Research Lab: [link]
- Sarah's LinkedIn: [link]
- Free resources on burnout recovery: [link]

**Guidelines**:
- Include URLs (make sharing easy)
- Provide brief context (why this resource)
- Prioritize quality over quantity (8-15 resources, not 50)
- Update links periodically (nothing is more frustrating than dead links)

### Step 8: Include Full Transcript or Transcript Link (as applicable)
Transcripts improve accessibility and SEO dramatically. If you have a transcript, link to it here.

"**Full Episode Transcript**: [Download PDF] or [Read on Website]"

If transcript is behind a paywall (Otter.ai, Descript, Rev), link to that:

"**Transcript**: Full episode transcript available to paid subscribers at [Patreon/Newsletter link]"

### Step 9: Create Social Media Pull Quotes (2-3 quotes)
Give yourself (and listeners) ready-made content for sharing.

**Format**: [Quotation] — [Speaker Name, timestamp if applicable]

**Characteristics**:
- Surprising or quotable (memorable, not obvious)
- Standalone comprehensible (doesn't need episode context)
- 1-2 sentences (fits in social post with context)
- Actionable or thought-provoking

**Examples**:

"'Burnout isn't about being lazy—it's a neurobiological state where your nervous system is stuck in fight-or-flight mode.' — Dr. Sarah Chen, 07:15"

"'Rest alone doesn't fix burnout. Your nervous system needs active recovery: cold exposure, breathing practices, or movement.' — Dr. Sarah Chen, 09:45"

"'The first thing burnout kills in your brain isn't energy—it's creativity and humor. Leaders, watch for this in your team.' — Dr. Sarah Chen, 33:30"

### Step 10: Optimize for Podcast Directories (SEO Keywords)
Podcast directories (Spotify, Apple Podcasts, Google Podcasts) use episode titles, descriptions, and keywords for search ranking.

**Keywords to Include** (naturally, not forced):
- In Title: "Burnout," "Neuroscience," "Recovery"
- In Summary: "burnout," "stress," "resilience," "nervous system," "neuroscience," "high-performance"
- In Tags/Categories: Burnout, Mental Health, Science, Business, Self-Improvement (depends on platform)

**Podcast-Specific SEO Tips**:
- Title keywords matter most (appear early)
- Summary keywords help (listen to podcast directories' recommended description length)
- Guest names add discoverability (people search for guest names)
- Consistency in format helps (regular listeners expect predictable structure)
- Cross-link to past episodes (if relevant) — helps with internal SEO

### Step 11: Add Call-to-Action
End with a clear ask (subscribe, share, support).

**Options**:
- "Subscribe on [Platform] for weekly episodes"
- "Share this episode with someone who's burning out"
- "Leave a review on Apple Podcasts; it helps us reach more listeners"
- "Join our free newsletter for resources and transcripts: [link]"
- "Support this show on Patreon: [link]"
- "Tell us: What helped you recover from burnout? Reply in the comments or email [email]"

Pick 1-2 asks; more becomes noise.

## Quality Gates
1. **Searchable Title**: Does title include target keyword? Is it under 60 characters?
2. **Summary Clarity**: Can someone unfamiliar with podcast understand the episode's value from the summary alone?
3. **Accurate Timestamps**: Do timestamps align with actual audio? Are topic names descriptive?
4. **Complete Guest Bio**: If guest, are credentials, expertise, and personal touch clear?
5. **Resource Links**: Are all links working? Are resources actually mentioned in episode?
6. **Scannable Format**: Can reader skim and get value in <2 minutes?
7. **Engagement**: Does show notes include at least one CTA (subscribe, share, support)?

## Examples

### Good Episode Title
"Why You're Burned Out (And How Your Nervous System Can Recover)"

**Why it works**: Benefit (burnout explanation, recovery), searchable keywords (burnout, nervous system, recover), under 60 characters, specific.

### Bad Episode Title
"Ep. 47: Our Best Episode Yet"

**Why it fails**: No keywords, vague, not searchable, no intrigue.

### Good Summary
"You've tried rest, meditation, and reducing hours—yet you're still burned out. The problem isn't willpower; it's your nervous system. In this episode, Stanford neuroscientist Dr. Sarah Chen explains the neuroscience of burnout and three evidence-based practices that actually reset your stress response. She shares her own severe burnout story and what recovery really looked like. If you're burning out or want to prevent it, this episode provides both understanding and actionable solutions."

**Why this works**: Problem stated (rest alone doesn't work), solution teased (nervous system practices), credibility (Dr. Chen, Stanford), personal story included, benefits clear (understanding + action), benefit-focused not feature-focused.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Vague Summary** — "We discuss burnout" without saying why listener should care. Solution: Lead with problem/benefit. "If you're burned out despite rest, this episode explains why and offers solutions."

2. **Missing Timestamps** — Long episode, no timestamps. Listeners can't navigate. Solution: Break every 5-10 minutes with descriptive topic labels.

3. **Generic Takeaways** — "This was a great conversation" without specific insights. Solution: Extract surprising, actionable takeaways that a skimmer would want to know.

4. **Broken Resource Links** — Links to resources mentioned are dead/incorrect. Frustrating for listeners. Solution: Verify every link before publishing; update periodically.

5. **No Guest Bio** — Listener doesn't know who guest is or why they matter. Solution: Always include 100-150 word bio with credentials, expertise, and human touch.

6. **Hiding Transcript** — Transcript is available but not linked in show notes. Listeners miss it. Solution: Prominent link to transcript (or clear note on where to find it).

## Anti-Patterns

1. **The Keyword Spam** — Title packed with keywords unnaturally: "Burnout Burnout Burnout: Burnout Recovery with Dr. Burnout." Looks spammy, hurts credibility. Better: Keyword-rich but natural title: "Why You're Burned Out (And How Your Nervous System Can Recover)."

2. **The Over-Detailed Timestamps** — Timestamps every 30 seconds with excessive detail. Noise, not helpful. Anti-pattern: "00:30 Sarah says hello" "01:00 Sarah talks about burnout" "01:30 Sarah continues." Better: Topic changes and key moments only.

3. **The Fake Resources** — Listing resources not mentioned in episode. Listener finds resource isn't discussed. Anti-pattern: Curating list of "related resources" not in episode. Better: Only list what you actually discussed.

4. **The Generic Hook** — "Join us for an interesting conversation" applied to every episode. No differentiation. Better: Specific, surprising insight unique to this episode.

5. **The Forgotten CTA** — Show notes end abruptly with no ask. Listener doesn't know what to do. Better: "Subscribe on [platform], share with a friend, or support on Patreon."
