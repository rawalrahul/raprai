# Social Media Content Strategist

## Purpose
Generate platform-native social content that respects each platform's algorithmic preferences, character constraints, and audience behavior patterns. This skill transforms generic messaging into scroll-stopping, algorithm-favored posts by applying platform-specific formatting rules, hook placement techniques, and engagement triggers that increase visibility without relying on paid promotion.

## When to Use
- Creating LinkedIn articles, posts, or carousel content for B2B audiences
- Writing Twitter/X threads that encourage engagement and thread continuation
- Drafting Instagram captions with hashtag strategies and visual direction notes
- Scripting TikTok videos with pattern interrupts and hook timing
- Building week-long content calendars with platform variation
- **Do NOT use when**: creating company-wide policy announcements (use formal channels), writing legal disclaimers (use compliance team), or producing video content that requires extensive production planning

## Instructions

### Step 1: Identify Platform Fundamentals
Before writing ANY content, lock in platform rules:
- **LinkedIn**: 3,000 character limit; algorithm prioritizes comments/shares in first hour; B2B audience expects value or credential building; Fridays 8-10 AM peak engagement
- **Twitter/X**: 280 characters (or 4,000 for Premium); threads break at Tweet 2-3 for decision to continue reading; emoji and line breaks critical for scanability; hashtags weaken engagement (use 0-2)
- **Instagram**: 2,200 character caption limit; first 125 characters visible before "more" truncation (put hook there); captions support hashtags after line break; hashtags in comments preserve aesthetic (30 max); reels algorithm heavily favors watch time completion
- **TikTok**: Hook in first 3 seconds non-negotiable; trending audio amplifies reach; caption should reinforce not repeat video; hashtags in caption (5-10 max); 15-60 second sweet spot for algorithm preference

### Step 2: Craft the Hook (Platform-Specific)
Each platform requires different hook mechanisms:
- **LinkedIn hook**: Lead with the benefit or controversy ("Most companies waste 40% of training budget"). Use a number, question, or counterintuitive statement
- **Twitter hook**: First 5-10 words must make reader want to click or reply; puns, questions, or contrarian takes
- **Instagram hook**: First line of caption visible without expanding; pose a relatable problem or aesthetic question matching the image
- **TikTok hook**: Visual hook (text overlay, cut/transition, costume change) in 0-1 second PLUS audio hook (trending sound, distinctive voice tone, loud music cue)

### Step 3: Apply Platform Formatting Rules
Respect the display mechanics of each platform:
- **LinkedIn**: Use line breaks between thoughts; bullet points for lists; single emoji max (placement matters—after value statement); avoid ALL CAPS
- **Twitter**: Line breaks between thoughts within a thread; use formatting judiciously (bold for keywords); emoji count matters for character economy
- **Instagram**: Capitalized first line draws eye; use line breaks to create visual breathing room; line of emojis or dots as section dividers; strategic bold using capitalization
- **TikTok**: Captions stay on-screen 2+ seconds; use text overlay in video, caption should layer meaning (not just describe)

### Step 4: Build Engagement Mechanisms
Embed platform-native engagement triggers:
- **LinkedIn engagement triggers**: "Controversial opinion:" prefix (increases comment ratio); "Genuine question:" invites response; "Tag someone" soft CTAs (frames as recommendation, not sales); ask questions that require nuanced answers
- **Twitter engagement triggers**: Numbered lists (people reply with hot takes); "What's hot/not" structures; emoji reactions (🔥 vs 🤔); quote-tweet invitation ("thoughts?")
- **Instagram engagement triggers**: Question stickers in Stories; caption questions that create polarized takes ("hot take: [opinion]"); calls to comment "first thought" or "agree/disagree"; save-worthy aesthetic (carousel structure)
- **TikTok engagement triggers**: "POV:" hooks (Point of View creates parasocial intimacy); pattern interrupts (cut to different background, filter, costume); duet/stitch invitation ("react in comments"); audio momentum (trending sounds carry engagement forward)

### Step 5: Optimize Hashtag Strategy by Platform
Hashtag placement and selection varies drastically:
- **LinkedIn**: 3-5 hashtags max, on separate line below content, no generic corporate hashtags; use #HR #Learning #Leadership—niche over broad
- **Twitter**: 0-2 hashtags only (reduces engagement); use only if trending or niche community specific; test without hashtags first
- **Instagram**: 30 hashtags allowed but split into 3 groups (5 popular 50K-500K posts, 10 mid-tier 500K-2M, 15 niche under 50K); comment-post hashtags to preserve visual aesthetic; rotate hashtags weekly to avoid shadow-ban risk
- **TikTok**: 5-10 hashtags maximum; mix #FYP or #ForYouPage (discovery algorithm signal), trending #[SoundName], niche community tags; avoid "dead" hashtags (under 100K views)

### Step 6: Assemble and Adjust for Platform Context
Combine all elements respecting platform audience expectations:
- **LinkedIn**: Add credibility (numbers, research, credentials); acknowledge tension (not all-positive); assume reader is time-scarce and competitive
- **Twitter**: Assume reader scrolls fast; provide exit ramps (hook → punchline or hook → thread continuation); expect pushback (frame for debate)
- **Instagram**: Assume reader is visual-first; captions layer emotional/aspirational meaning onto image; treat carousel text as visual design element
- **TikTok**: Assume reader will stop scrolling at first second of friction; every frame must earn attention; captions are secondary to audio/visual

## Output Template

### LinkedIn Post
```
[HOOK - 1 sentence with number/question/contradiction]

[Body - 2-3 sentences elaborating value or insight]

[Perspective shift or personal confession - creates relatability]

[Call to engagement - question or soft recommendation]

#NicheHashtag1 #NicheHashtag2 #NicheHashtag3
```

### Twitter/X Thread
```
Thread Title: [Hook that makes reader want to click]

Tweet 1: [Hook - first 10 words pull reader in] [numbered opener "1/" for series signal]
Tweet 2: [Elaboration] "2/"
Tweet 3: [Evidence/stat] "3/"
...
Final Tweet: [Payoff or insight] "{{X}}/{{End}}"
```

### Instagram Caption
```
[Visible hook in FIRST LINE - max 50 chars before "...more"]

[Narrative or emotional layering]

[Visual direction in brackets if carousels: "Swipe for [benefit]"]
.
[30 hashtags on separate line OR in first comment]
```

### TikTok Script
```
Trending Audio: [Song/Sound name + why it matches]
Length: {{SEC}} seconds
Hook (0-1s): [VISUAL description: text overlay, cut, or costume]
B-Roll: [What's on screen during audio]
Caption: [Reinforces video meaning, NOT description]
Call to Action: {{TYPE}} [duet/comment/share invitation]
```

## Quality Gates
- [ ] Hook appears in first sentence (LinkedIn), first 10 words (Twitter), before "...more" (Instagram), or first 3 seconds (TikTok)
- [ ] No generic corporate language ("innovative," "synergistic," "paradigm shift")—replace with specific mechanisms or numbers
- [ ] Character count respects platform limits AND leaves 10-15% buffer for mobile display variance
- [ ] Hashtags match platform norms: 3-5 LinkedIn, 0-2 Twitter, 30 Instagram (split niche/mid/popular), 5-10 TikTok
- [ ] Content includes engagement mechanism specific to platform (question for LinkedIn, numbered list for Twitter, sticker question for Instagram, pattern interrupt for TikTok)
- [ ] No cross-platform posting (each platform version shows unique formatting, hook placement, hashtag strategy)
- [ ] All claims include supporting numbers or research cites (not "drives engagement" but "increased comment rate 34%")

## Examples

### Good Output (LinkedIn)

```
70% of employees skip mandatory training videos.

Most companies assume compliance = comprehension. They measure completion rate (did they sit through it) not retention rate (can they actually apply it).

I tested this with a mid-market tech company. They had a standard 12-minute compliance module with 89% completion rate. But exit quiz showed 34% understood the key concepts.

We rebuilt it as a 3-minute micro-scene with a character failure (not narrator lecture). Completion stayed 89%, but quiz jumped to 76% retention.

The fix: Stop explaining the rule. Show the rule failing, then show the fix. That three-second difference rewires how brains encode information.

Curious if this matches your company's experience—what's your compliance training completion versus comprehension gap?

#Learning #TrainingDevelopment #EmployeeRetention
```

### Bad Output (what to avoid)

```
Training is important and companies should invest in it. Our innovative approach to employee development is synergistic with organizational goals.

We help companies transform their training culture. Contact us today!

#Training #HRTech #Learning #Development #Corporate #Business #Talent #Culture
```

## Common Mistakes

1. **Mistake**: Same content posted across all platforms with just emoji added → **Fix**: Rewrite for each platform's hook mechanism and algorithm. LinkedIn needs credential-building or controversial opinion; Twitter needs debate-ability; Instagram needs visual inspiration; TikTok needs pattern interruption.

2. **Mistake**: Hashtag dumping (30+ hashtags on Twitter, 0 on Instagram) → **Fix**: Twitter (0-2 only), Instagram (30 split into niche/mid/popular), LinkedIn (3-5 niche), TikTok (5-10 including trending audio name).

3. **Mistake**: Burying hook below long preamble → **Fix**: Hook in first sentence (LinkedIn), first 10 words (Twitter), or first visual frame (TikTok). Every platform algorithm measures early abandonment—hook readers before they scroll.

4. **Mistake**: All-positive, corporate-safe messaging → **Fix**: LinkedIn thrives on tension ("Most companies get this wrong"), Twitter on debate, TikTok on vulnerability or humor. Safe content has 60% engagement of polarizing content.

5. **Mistake**: Treating captions as descriptions instead of storytelling → **Fix**: Instagram captions should make reader FEEL something before they see image connection. TikTok captions should layer new meaning, not describe what's visible.

## Anti-Patterns

- Never post identical content across platforms (algorithm optimizes for platform-native behavior, not universal messaging)
- Never exceed platform character limits by even 1 character (display cuts text, breaking flow and engagement)
- Never use hashtags as sentence fragments on Twitter (#SoExcited #Innovation #Growth—kills engagement by 45%)
- Never include hook AFTER link or CTA (once reader leaves platform, engagement metrics stop counting)
- Never write TikTok captions over 1 line or longer than 10 seconds of video (overlaps with hook and loses viewers)
- Never assume same hashtag strategy works across platforms (Instagram's 30 maximum differs completely from LinkedIn's 3-5 niche approach)
- Never skip first-second visual hook on TikTok (platform scrolls past in 1 second if not arrested; audio matters second)
