# Copywriting Frameworks

## Purpose
Convert readers into customers by structuring copy around psychological frameworks that move people from attention → interest → desire → action. Copywriting differs from other writing: every word must contribute to conversion. Features sell to people who already want the solution; copy sells to people who don't know they have a problem yet.

## When to Use
- Writing landing pages and sales pages (B2B/B2C, new or existing products)
- Crafting ad copy for paid campaigns (Google Ads, Facebook, LinkedIn) with high-performing headlines
- Writing product descriptions, email subject lines, and CTAs that drive conversion
- Rewriting underperforming pages with conversion-focused frameworks
- Creating launch emails and promotional copy with urgency and social proof
- **Do NOT use when**: Writing blog content (use blog-post-writer instead), technical documentation, customer support, or internal communications

## Instructions

### Step 1: Choose Framework Based on Audience Psychology
Match framework to reader state: AIDA (Attention-Interest-Desire-Action) for cold traffic who don't know you; PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solve) for aware audience who know the problem; BAB (Before-After-Bridge) for decision stage (close to buying); 4Ps (Picture-Promise-Proof-Push) for emotional/lifestyle products. AIDA best for SaaS and B2B (unknown problem → solution). PAS best for pain-aware audiences (already frustrated). BAB best for comparison/objection stage. 4Ps best for consumer goods, fashion, luxury. Choose one framework for entire page and stick with it; don't mix frameworks.

### Step 2: Write Attention-Stopping Headline
Headline = make-or-break element; 80% of readers read headline only, don't scroll. Use these formulas: (a) Benefit + number: "Save 12 Hours Per Week on Planning" (b) Curiosity gap: "The One Skill Nobody Teaches New Managers" (c) Reverse expectations: "Why Expensive Doesn't Mean Better" (d) Specific claim: "Get 10x More Leads Without Hiring Sales" (e) Fear of missing out: "95% of Retailers Use This—Your Competitors Are." Test headline on yourself: does it make you want to read more? If you'd scroll past, so will they. Avoid: generic ("Welcome"), jargon, vague benefit ("Better Results"). 70-90 characters max.

### Step 3: Build Proof Stack (Data, Social, Trust)
Readers are skeptical by default; proof overcomes skepticism. Three types: statistical (hard numbers), social (testimonials, user count, reviews), and trust signals (certifications, partnerships, awards). Each should be 1-2 sentences + specific number. Bad: "Trusted by many companies." Good: "Used by 12,000+ retail teams including Whole Foods and Target." Include stat with numbers, not percentages alone: "47 companies" not "majority." Place strongest proof in first section (after headline) to establish credibility immediately. Reserve most impressive proof near CTA (before the ask).

### Step 4: Identify & Agitate the Core Problem
Reader doesn't buy the solution; reader buys relief from the problem. Describe their current pain in specific terms (not generic). Example: "Spending 8+ hours on weekly planning, spreadsheets breaking down at Q4 scale, planning forecast accuracy at 60%—all while your team waits." Make them feel the cost: time, money, stress, opportunity cost. This is not exaggeration; it's specificity. Use their language (if researched): if they say "chaos," use "chaos" not "suboptimal processes." Agitate = acknowledge the cost of not fixing it, not threats.

### Step 5: Present Solution as Obvious Next Step
After agitation, introduce your solution as the natural remedy (not salesy pitch). Use "bridge" language: "The answer: a platform that replaces spreadsheets and runs forecasts in minutes." Position three benefits tied to the problems you agitated. Not: "Our AI-powered solution." Better: "AI that learns your patterns (so you don't have to rebuild forecasts monthly)." Each benefit should answer "so what?" Pair benefit with proof (stat, testimonial) immediately after, not all together at the end.

### Step 6: Craft Psychological Triggers & Power Words
Scarcity: "Only 3 spots left" (if true). Urgency: "Closes Friday at 11:59 PM" (specific, not "limited time"). Social proof: "Join 12,000+ teams" (specific number). Authority: "Built by former Stripe engineers" (credibility). Reciprocity: "Free X-day trial" (gives first). Exclusivity: "Invite-only for beta users." Use power words with specificity: "cutting-edge" is weak ("cutting edge" alone = meaningless); "cuts planning time by 60%" = specific power. Track which triggers work for your audience (varies by industry). A/B test scarcity vs. urgency in headlines.

### Step 7: Place CTA High & Repeat Lower
CTA is not just a button at the bottom. Place primary CTA above the fold (within first screen height) for cold traffic. Repeat softer CTA mid-page (not as pushy) and then primary CTA again near bottom. CTA button text should match promise, not "Submit" or "Learn More" generics. Better: "Get Free 14-Day Trial" or "See Pricing" (matches what they'll get). Make button color contrast with page (not a subtle shade). For paid ads, CTA copy in ad + button should match (consistency = higher conversion).

## Output Template

### AIDA Framework (Unknown Problem → Solution)
```
HEADLINE (Attention): {{Benefit + number, curiosity gap, or reverse expectation}}

[Visual: hero image or video demonstrating outcome]

INTEREST SECTION:
{{The specific pain point with numbers and examples}}

{{Proof element 1: "Used by X companies" or "Y% faster"}}

DESIRE SECTION:
{{Three key benefits, each tied to agitated problem}}

{{Proof element 2: Customer testimonial or case stat}}

[Visual: screenshot or feature demo showing before/after]

ACTION:
{{CTA button: "Get Free 14-Day Trial"}}

{{Softer option: "See pricing" or "Schedule 20-min demo"}}
```

### PAS Framework (Problem-Aware Audience)
```
PROBLEM HEADLINE: {{Name the specific pain in their words}}

{{Agitation paragraph: Cost of not fixing (time, money, stress)}}

AGITATE (Deep into pain):
{{More specific examples of failure cost}}
{{Proof: How many people suffer this → social proof}}

SOLVE (Present remedy):
{{Your solution in one sentence}}

{{How it works: 3 steps, each tied to a pain point you agitated}}

{{Proof: Before/after metrics or testimonial}}

[Visual: workflow or feature that solves the problem]

CTA: {{High-friction ask (trial) or low-friction ask (demo)}}
```

### BAB Framework (Close to Purchase)
```
BEFORE: {{Current state (painful, frustrating)}}
{{Visual or description of before state}}

AFTER: {{New state (desired outcome)}}
{{Specific, measurable, emotional}}
{{Visual of after state}}

BRIDGE: {{Here's how we make that happen}}
{{3-step process or key differentiators vs. alternatives}}

{{Proof: Social proof, case study, guarantee}}

CTA: {{Final ask, minimize friction: "Start Now"}}
```

### 4Ps Framework (Emotional/Lifestyle)
```
PICTURE: {{Paint scene where reader's desired future is obvious}}
{{Sensory details, emotion, lifestyle}}

PROMISE: {{What they'll feel, have, or be able to do}}

PROOF: {{Evidence this is real: testimonials, reviews, authority}}

PUSH: {{CTA with scarcity or urgency}}
```

## Quality Gates

- [ ] Headline uses one of five formulas (benefit+number, curiosity gap, reverse expectation, specific claim, FOMO); passes "would you click?" test
- [ ] Problem is described with specific numbers and examples (not generic: "Save 8 hours/week on planning" not "save time")
- [ ] Proof stack includes 3 elements: statistical data, social proof (count or testimonial), trust signal (certification/partnership)
- [ ] Solution presented as obvious next step to problem agitation, not as sales pitch
- [ ] Each benefit tied to specific problem agitated earlier in copy
- [ ] CTA appears above fold + mid-page + end; button text matches promise ("Get Trial" not "Submit")
- [ ] Framework chosen matches audience state (AIDA for cold, PAS for problem-aware, BAB for comparison, 4Ps for emotional)
- [ ] Power words paired with specificity ("60% faster" not "faster than before")
- [ ] At least one psychological trigger present (scarcity, urgency, social proof, authority, exclusivity)
- [ ] Page reads as one coherent argument, not disconnected sections

## Examples

### Good Output (Excerpt - PAS Framework, SaaS)

**HEADLINE (Problem):**
"Stop rebuilding forecasts when demand spikes. AI does it for you."

**AGITATE (Pain depth):**
Every Q4, the same story: spreadsheets crash at scale, your team manually rebuilds forecasts in a panic, accuracy drops to 60%, you miss revenue targets. The forecast you built in September is useless by October.

**Social Proof (Early agitation):**
12,000+ retail teams gave up on manual forecasting. Here's why.

**SOLVE:**
Forecast Pro learns your demand patterns and updates automatically. No spreadsheets. No manual rebuilds. 92% accuracy year-round.

---

**How It Works:**
1. **Upload historical data** (one click)
2. **AI learns your patterns** (done in hours)
3. **Automatic updates every week** (no re-forecasting)

**Proof (Stat):**
"We went from 60% to 92% forecast accuracy in 3 weeks. Q4 planning was stress-free for the first time in years." — Sarah Chen, VP Planning, Whole Foods

**Proof (Testimonial + Case Stat):**
Companies using Forecast Pro waste 8 fewer hours per week on manual forecasts. For a team of 4, that's 32 hours/week = $2,400/week back to core work.

**CTA (Above fold):**
[Get Free 14-Day Trial]

**Softer CTA (Mid-page):**
See how it works → [Watch 3-min demo]

---

**Analysis:**
- Headline: problem-specific ("Stop rebuilding forecasts") + benefit ("AI does it")
- Agitate: specific numbers (60% accuracy, Q4 panic) and emotional detail (spreadsheets crash)
- Early proof: specific count (12,000+ teams) = credibility
- Solution: one sentence, benefits not features
- Each benefit tied to pain (automatic updates = no manual rebuilds; no spreadsheets = no crashes)
- Proof: 92% accuracy (stat), before/after (social), second proof = time/money benefit
- CTAs: high friction (trial) above fold, low friction (demo) mid-page
- Power words: "Stop", "Automatically", "Learn" = action-oriented

### Bad Output (What to Avoid)

**HEADLINE:**
"Advanced AI-Powered Forecasting Solution"

**Body:**
Our platform leverages cutting-edge machine learning to optimize your demand planning processes. We utilize proprietary algorithms that integrate seamlessly with your existing data infrastructure. Our solution provides enterprise-grade scalability and robust analytics capabilities for forward-thinking organizations.

**Features:**
- Advanced machine learning algorithms
- Cloud-based architecture
- Integrations with major ERP systems
- Customizable dashboards
- Real-time analytics

**Testimonial:**
"Great solution. Very impressed with the capabilities." — John D., Acme Corp

**CTA:**
[Learn More]

---

**Issues Identified:**
- Headline: generic jargon, no problem named, no benefit clear
- No agitation of pain or cost
- Copy focused on features ("machine learning", "cloud-based") not benefits
- Testimonial is vague, no metric, no detail
- No proof of scale (how many customers?) or credibility
- CTA vague and low-intent ("Learn More" = no commitment)
- No psychological trigger (no scarcity, urgency, social proof with numbers)
- No before/after comparison
- Reads like a feature list, not persuasive copy

## Common Mistakes

1. **Mistake**: Leading with features instead of benefits and pain
   → **Fix**: Start with problem or pain the reader has, not what your product does. "Stop wasting 8 hours/week" (pain) before "AI automates forecasting" (feature).

2. **Mistake**: Proof is vague ("Trusted by industry leaders", "Award-winning solution")
   → **Fix**: Specific proof only. "Used by 12,000+ retail teams including Whole Foods" not "used by many companies." Number must be in the proof statement.

3. **Mistake**: CTA buried at bottom or repeated in same form throughout
   → **Fix**: CTA above fold (within first screen height) for cold traffic. Use high-friction CTA once (trial), then softer option mid-page (demo).

4. **Mistake**: Using jargon and corporate language that impresses nobody
   → **Fix**: Use reader's language. If they say "chaos," use "chaos." Avoid: "leverage," "synergies," "optimize," "cutting-edge." Be specific instead.

5. **Mistake**: Agitation without resolution feels mean; resolution without agitation feels salesy
   → **Fix**: Agitate problem deeply, then present solution as obvious next step. Problem first, solution second.

## Anti-Patterns

- **Never** use vague proof ("industry leaders", "award-winning", "trusted by many"). Specific number or named customer only.
- **Never** mix frameworks. Pick one (AIDA, PAS, BAB, or 4Ps) and follow it through. Mixing confuses the read.
- **Never** make CTA generic ("Submit", "Learn More", "Click Here"). Button text should match promise ("Get Free Trial", "See Pricing").
- **Never** present features as benefits. "Machine learning" is a feature; "92% forecast accuracy" is a benefit. Benefit = what the customer gets.
- **Never** place CTA only at the bottom for cold traffic. Above fold or it gets missed. Repeat mid-page and end, but vary intensity.
- **Never** use power words without specificity. "Fast" is weak; "60% faster than spreadsheets" is strong. Pair adjectives with numbers.
