# Word Policy Document

## Purpose
Write clear organizational policies that are actually used. This skill structures policies with purpose statements (why), scope (who it applies to), definitions (precision), clear policy language (shall, must, should), step-by-step procedures (how), roles and responsibilities (accountability), and exceptions processes (flexibility). Strong policies guide behavior without feeling punitive.

## When to Use
- "Write a [topic] policy" (vacation, remote work, hiring, data security)
- "Create a handbook section" on [governance area]
- "Document our [process]" formally
- **Do NOT use when**: Creating employment law (consult a lawyer); or writing guidance docs (use word-report-builder instead)

## Instructions

### Step 1: Policy Header and Document Setup
Professional formatting and metadata.

**Policy Header Section** (top of page)
```
POLICY DOCUMENT

Policy Title:                    {{Remote Work Policy}}

Version:                         {{1.0}}
Effective Date:                  {{March 1, 2024}}
Last Revised Date:               {{March 1, 2024}}
Next Review Date:                {{March 1, 2025}}
Policy Owner (Author):           {{Name, Title, Dept}}
Approved By:                     {{Executive sponsor or board}}
Approval Date:                   {{Date approved}}
Related Policies:                {{Other related policy IDs}}
Revision History:                {{Keep log of changes}}
```

**Font and Formatting**
- Title: 16pt bold
- Headers: 12pt bold
- Body: 11pt standard
- Color: Black text on white; headers in dark blue or company color

**Document Setup** (Home → Styles)
- Heading 1: Policy title
- Heading 2: Main sections (Purpose, Scope, Definitions, etc.)
- Heading 3: Subsections and procedures
- Body: Standard paragraph formatting

### Step 2: Purpose Statement
Why does this policy exist? What problem does it solve?

**Purpose Section** (1–2 sentences, be clear)
```
1. PURPOSE

This policy establishes guidelines for remote work arrangements at
[Company Name] to enable work flexibility while maintaining productivity,
collaboration, and company culture. This policy applies to all employees
and outlines when remote work is permitted, approval processes, and
expectations for performance and communication.
```

**Bad vs. Good**
- **Bad**: "This policy covers remote work" (describes, doesn't explain why)
- **Good**: "This policy enables flexibility for employees while ensuring we maintain collaboration and meet delivery deadlines" (states intent and outcome)

### Step 3: Scope Statement
Who does this apply to? Any exceptions?

**Scope Section** (clear boundaries)
```
2. SCOPE

This policy applies to all full-time and part-time employees of [Company].

This policy does NOT apply to:
- Contractors or temporary workers (see Contractor Agreement)
- Executive leadership (see Executive Remote Work Guidelines)
- Employees in roles requiring full-time on-site presence (e.g., facilities,
  reception—listed in Appendix A)

Geographic Scope:
This policy applies to all [Company] offices globally, with adjustments
for local labor law (see Section 5: Local Variations).
```

**Be Specific**
- Who: Employee types, roles, locations
- Who not: Contractors, executives, specific role families
- When: Effective date; any phase-in period
- Where: Which offices or locations

### Step 4: Definitions Section
Precision prevents disputes and ambiguity.

**Definitions Section** (numbered, alphabetical)
```
3. DEFINITIONS

For purposes of this policy, the following terms are defined as:

3.1 "Approved Remote Days" means the specific day(s) each week an employee
    is permitted to work from a location other than their assigned office,
    as documented in their Remote Work Agreement (Section 6).

3.2 "Core Hours" means 10:00 AM – 3:00 PM in the employee's local timezone,
    during which all employees must be available for meetings and
    synchronous collaboration.

3.3 "Manager Approval" means written confirmation from the employee's direct
    manager (email is acceptable) granting remote work arrangement.

3.4 "Productivity Standards" means delivery of assigned work by deadline and
    participation in required meetings (see Section 4.2).

3.5 "Remote Work Arrangement" means a documented agreement between [Company],
    manager, and employee specifying frequency, location, and expectations
    of remote work.

3.6 "Work-from-Home Eligible Role" means positions where work can be
    performed outside the office without material impact on operations,
    as determined by department leadership.
```

**Drafting Tips**
- Define ambiguous terms before using them in policy statements
- Use consistent definitions throughout (define once, use same term everywhere)
- Include measurements where possible (not vague like "reasonable")

### Step 5: Policy Statements (The Rules)
Clear, enforceable requirements using "shall", "must", "should", "may".

**Language Hierarchy**
- **MUST / SHALL**: Mandatory; non-compliance is a policy violation
- **SHOULD**: Strongly recommended; non-compliance is not ideal but not violation
- **MAY**: Optional; permitted but not required

**Policy Statement Section**
```
4. POLICY STATEMENTS

4.1 ELIGIBILITY FOR REMOTE WORK

4.1.1 Employees may request remote work arrangements if:
      (a) Their role is classified as "Work-from-Home Eligible" (Appendix A)
      (b) They have been employed for at least 90 days
      (c) Their current performance rating is "Meets Expectations" or higher
      (d) Their manager approves the arrangement in writing

4.1.2 The following employees are NOT eligible for remote work:
      (a) Employees in roles requiring full-time office presence
      (b) Employees on a performance improvement plan (PIP)
      (c) Probationary employees (<90 days)

4.2 FREQUENCY AND SCHEDULE

4.2.1 Remote work may be granted for up to 3 days per week (not to exceed
      60% of scheduled hours), subject to business needs.

4.2.2 Remote days MUST be consistent week-to-week (e.g., "Monday, Tuesday,
      Thursday every week" is preferred over ad-hoc arrangements).

4.2.3 Employees MUST be available during Core Hours (10 AM – 3 PM local time)
      for meetings and collaboration, whether working remotely or on-site.

4.2.4 Department leadership MAY require in-office presence for team events,
      all-hands meetings, or project kick-offs, with 5 business days notice.

4.3 COMMUNICATION EXPECTATIONS

4.3.1 Employees working remotely SHALL:
      (a) Communicate their location and availability to their manager at
          the start of the workday
      (b) Respond to emails and messages within 2 business hours during
          working hours
      (c) Attend all scheduled meetings (whether in-office or virtual)
      (d) Inform their manager of any schedule changes by 5 PM the prior day

4.4 TECHNOLOGY AND SECURITY

4.4.1 Employees SHALL use [Company]-approved technology to access company
      systems and data:
      (a) VPN MUST be used for all connections to company networks
      (b) Multi-factor authentication MUST be enabled on all accounts
      (c) Company laptop is the only device for accessing Confidential
          Information

4.4.2 Employees SHOULD work in a secure, private location free from
      unauthorized access or observation.

4.4.3 Employees SHALL NOT share their login credentials or allow others
      access to their computer.
```

**Formatting**
- Number every statement (4.1, 4.1.1, 4.2, etc.) for easy reference
- Use bold for MUST/SHALL/SHOULD to emphasize requirement level
- Separate into logical subsections (Eligibility, Frequency, Communication, etc.)

### Step 6: Procedures (Step-by-Step How-To)
Make compliance easy; don't require guessing.

**Procedures Section** (numbered steps)
```
5. PROCEDURES

5.1 HOW TO REQUEST REMOTE WORK ARRANGEMENT

Step 1: Complete the Remote Work Request Form (Appendix B)
        Include: Proposed schedule, business justification, job responsibilities
        that require on-site presence (if any)

Step 2: Review with your direct manager
        Schedule a 15-minute meeting to discuss the arrangement, including
        any concerns and expectations.

Step 3: Manager approval (within 5 business days)
        Manager emails HR confirming approval or denial. If denied, manager
        MUST provide reason (e.g., "Role requires client-facing presence").

Step 4: Execute Remote Work Agreement (Appendix C)
        Both employee and manager sign the agreement confirming schedule,
        expectations, and conditions.

Step 5: HR processing (within 2 business days of signature)
        HR updates systems, notifies payroll/benefits if applicable, and
        provides employee with confirmation email.

Effective date: Arrangement begins the Monday following all signatures.

Timeline: Request to start ~2 weeks

5.2 HOW TO CHANGE OR DISCONTINUE REMOTE WORK

Changes (e.g., from "2 days to 3 days per week"):
- Submit updated Remote Work Request Form to manager
- Manager approves/denies within 5 days
- If approved, changes take effect the following Monday

Discontinuation:
- Either employee or manager may discontinue with 2 weeks' notice
- Employee notice: Email to manager
- Manager-initiated: Manager notifies employee in writing (30 days notice)

5.3 PERFORMANCE MONITORING

Managers SHALL:
- Monitor productivity using same metrics as on-site employees (no lower
  standards)
- Conduct regular one-on-ones (at least bi-weekly) to assess performance
  and collaboration
- Provide feedback quarterly

Employees MUST:
- Maintain same performance standards as on-site peers
- Deliver work on schedule
- Communicate proactively about blockers or issues
- Participate in team meetings and collaboration

If performance declines, manager may discontinue remote work arrangement
and require return to on-site work.
```

**Writing Tips**
- Use numbered steps (1, 2, 3...) for clarity
- Include timelines (e.g., "within 5 business days")
- Link to forms/templates ("Appendix B")
- Assign responsibility (who does what?)

### Step 7: Roles and Responsibilities
Who enforces? Who decides? Who appeals to?

**Roles Section**
```
6. ROLES AND RESPONSIBILITIES

6.1 EMPLOYEE RESPONSIBILITIES

Employees working remotely SHALL:
- Maintain productivity and meet performance standards
- Communicate availability and location
- Comply with technology and security requirements (Section 4.4)
- Inform manager of changes to approved schedule
- Complete required time tracking or reporting

6.2 MANAGER RESPONSIBILITIES

Managers MUST:
- Review and approve/deny remote work requests within 5 business days
- Assess whether role is eligible for remote work
- Monitor productivity; treat remote employees same as on-site
- Document performance issues and communicate with employee promptly
- Approve schedule changes or request return to office with business
  justification

6.3 HUMAN RESOURCES RESPONSIBILITIES

HR SHALL:
- Maintain policy; review annually for updates
- Process approvals and track remote work arrangements
- Handle appeals and exceptions
- Provide guidance to managers on policy application
- Track compliance and report metrics to leadership

6.4 DEPARTMENT LEADERSHIP RESPONSIBILITIES

Department heads SHOULD:
- Ensure operational needs are met while granting flexibility
- Review the policy with their teams annually
- Approve department-wide policy variations (with HR sign-off)
- Support managers in enforcing policy fairly and consistently
```

**Accountability Structures**
- Clear owner for each responsibility (not vague "manager and HR together")
- Timelines for decisions (5 days, not "soon")
- Escalation path (if manager denies unfairly, employee appeals to whom?)

### Step 8: Compliance and Enforcement
What happens if someone violates the policy?

**Compliance Section**
```
7. COMPLIANCE AND ENFORCEMENT

7.1 VIOLATIONS

Violations of this policy include:
- Working remotely on non-approved days
- Violating security requirements (not using VPN, sharing credentials)
- Missing core hours without manager approval
- Failing to maintain productivity standards
- Misrepresenting availability or work location

7.2 CONSEQUENCES

First violation: Manager issues written warning. Employee and manager
                meet to clarify expectations.

Second violation: Formal written warning (documented in employee file).
                 Employee may be required to return to office temporarily.

Third violation: Removal of remote work privileges for 90 days. May result
                in disciplinary action up to termination (per employee
                handbook).

Serious violations (e.g., security breach):
Immediate suspension of remote work; disciplinary action per handbook.

7.3 APPEAL PROCESS

If an employee disputes a compliance decision:
1. Employee requests a meeting with manager within 5 business days
2. If unresolved, employee escalates to HR
3. HR reviews and makes final determination within 10 business days
4. Decision is documented in writing

Appeal does not delay implementation of disciplinary action.
```

**Key Elements**
- Violations defined clearly (not subjective)
- Consequences graduated (first warning lighter than second)
- Appeal process exists (fairness)
- Process is documented (accountability)

### Step 9: Exceptions and Approval Authority
When rules don't apply; who decides?

**Exceptions Section**
```
8. EXCEPTIONS AND WAIVERS

8.1 EXCEPTIONS (cases where policy rules may not apply)

The following exceptions MAY be granted by HR with business justification:

Exception 1: "Always-on-site" role becomes remote-eligible due to
             technology/process changes
             → Requires: Department head + HR approval

Exception 2: Employee works in multiple locations (e.g., client site + home)
             → Requires: Manager + HR approval

Exception 3: Compressed schedule (e.g., 4 long days in office, 1 day remote)
             → Requires: Manager approval (standard process, alternative
               schedule)

Exception 4: Temporary remote work (< 3 months for specific project)
             → Requires: Manager approval + temporary agreement

8.2 APPROVAL AUTHORITY

Standard approvals: Manager (within policy bounds)
Exceptions: HR Director + Employee's Manager
Major variations (e.g., 100% remote for previously on-site role): VP + HR + Manager

All exceptions MUST be documented in writing.

8.3 HOW TO REQUEST AN EXCEPTION

Submit: Exception Request Form (Appendix D) to your manager and HR
Include: Reason for exception, duration, impact on operations
Timeline: 10 business days for review and response
```

## Quality Gates
- [ ] Purpose statement explains WHY, not just what
- [ ] Scope clearly identifies who is covered and who is exempt
- [ ] All key terms are defined (precision, not ambiguity)
- [ ] Policy statements use MUST/SHOULD/MAY language clearly
- [ ] Procedures are step-by-step (not vague "follow the process")
- [ ] Roles and responsibilities are assigned to specific roles
- [ ] Violations and consequences are defined; consequences graduated
- [ ] Appeal/exception process exists (policy isn't one-way)
- [ ] Related documents are listed (policy doesn't exist in isolation)
- [ ] Review date is set (not a static, stale document)

## Examples

### Good Output (excerpt)
```
2. SCOPE

This policy applies to all full-time and part-time employees.

Does NOT apply to:
- Contractors (see Contractor Agreement, Section 6)
- Customer-facing roles requiring on-site presence (Appendix A lists roles)
- Employees on performance improvement plan (PIP)

4.2 FREQUENCY

Employees MAY work remotely up to 3 days per week.
Remote days MUST be consistent (e.g., "Monday, Tuesday, Thursday" every week).
Employees MUST be available Core Hours (10 AM–3 PM) whether remote or on-site.

5.1 HOW TO REQUEST REMOTE WORK

Step 1: Complete Remote Work Request Form (Appendix B)
Step 2: Manager reviews and approves within 5 business days
Step 3: Sign Remote Work Agreement (Appendix C)
Step 4: HR processes; arrangement starts the following Monday

Timeline: Request to start = ~2 weeks

7. ENFORCEMENT

First violation: Written warning + meeting with manager
Second violation: Formal warning (in file) + temporary office requirement
Third violation: Loss of remote privileges for 90 days

Appeal: Employee meets with manager within 5 days; escalates to HR if unresolved.

8. EXCEPTIONS

Example 1: Multi-location role (client site + home)
          → Approved by: Manager + HR

Timeline: 10 days for decision
```

### Bad Output (what to avoid)
```
Purpose: "This policy covers remote work" (describes, doesn't explain why)
Scope: Unclear who is exempt; "certain roles" (vague)
Definitions: Missing (ambiguous terms used later)
Policy: "Employees should work remotely when appropriate" (no boundaries)
Procedures: "Follow the process" (what process? no steps)
Roles: "Manager and HR will handle this" (unclear who decides what)
Enforcement: No consequences listed; no appeal process
No definitions, no timeline, no escalation path for disputes
```

## Common Mistakes

1. **Mistake**: Purpose is just a description ("This policy covers remote work").
   → **Fix**: Explain WHY: "This policy enables flexibility while maintaining productivity and security."

2. **Mistake**: Scope is vague ("Some employees may work remotely").
   → **Fix**: Be explicit: "Employees in eligible roles (Appendix A) may request 1–3 days/week."

3. **Mistake**: Policy uses undefined terms (what's "appropriate"? who decides?).
   → **Fix**: Define all ambiguous terms in Section 3.

4. **Mistake**: Procedures are vague ("Follow the normal process").
   → **Fix**: Step-by-step: "Step 1: Submit form. Step 2: Manager approves. Step 3: HR processes."

5. **Mistake**: No consequences defined; policy is advisory, not enforced.
   → **Fix**: Clear violations + graduated consequences (warning → documentation → suspension → termination).

## Anti-Patterns
- Never write a policy that's too vague to enforce. (If terms are undefined, employees/managers will interpret differently.)
- Never skip the exception process. (No policy works 100% of the time; exceptions handled badly breed resentment.)
- Never make employees guess at procedures. (Step-by-step instructions prevent confusion and frustration.)
- Never forget to review/update policies. (Stale policies lose credibility; review annually.)
- Never lack an appeal process. (Fairness requires employees can contest decisions.)
- Never mix policy (rules) with guidance (best practices) in one document. (Policy = mandatory; guidance = suggested.)
