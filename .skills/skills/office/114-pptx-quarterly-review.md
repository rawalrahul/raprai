# PPTX Quarterly Review

## Purpose
Create quarterly review presentations that inform leadership on business health and strategic progress. This skill structures QBRs around financial performance, operational metrics (KPIs), customer/market highlights, wins and losses, risk and issue tracking, and forward-looking priorities. A strong QBR provides transparency, surfaces risks early, and gains alignment on next steps.

## When to Use
- "Build a quarterly business review" for {{audience}}
- "Report quarterly results" to board or executive leadership
- "Present Q3 performance" with financials, KPIs, and next priorities
- **Do NOT use when**: Presenting detailed data analysis (use 108-pptx-data-storyteller); or training (use 111-pptx-training-deck)

## Instructions

### Step 1: QBR Structure and Flow
Standard narrative flow for business reviews.

**Recommended Slide Sequence** (30–45 min presentation)

1. **Title / Agenda** (Slide 1, 2 min)
   - Title: "Q3 2024 Business Review" or "Q3 Results & Next Priorities"
   - Agenda: 4–5 main topics

2. **Executive Summary** (Slide 2, 3 min)
   - 3 bullets: Key metrics (revenue, margin, customer count)
   - Overall health status (Green/Amber/Red)
   - One-sentence call-out of biggest win or risk

3. **Financial Performance** (Slides 3–5, 8 min)
   - Revenue: Plan vs. Actual (chart + variance explanation)
   - Margin / Profitability: Trend and drivers
   - Expense management: Budget vs. Actual

4. **Operational Metrics (KPIs)** (Slides 6–8, 8 min)
   - Customer growth: Additions, retention, churn
   - Operational efficiency: Time-to-delivery, defect rates, etc.
   - Employee/team metrics: Headcount, engagement, attrition

5. **Customer & Market Highlights** (Slides 9–10, 4 min)
   - Major customer wins / contract expansions
   - Market feedback / competitive position
   - Product/feature adoption

6. **Wins & Losses** (Slides 11–12, 5 min)
   - Top 3 wins (specific examples, quantified impact)
   - Top 2–3 losses (customer churn, missed opportunities, with context)

7. **Risk & Issue Register** (Slide 13, 4 min)
   - Open risks (probability × impact)
   - Mitigations or escalations in progress

8. **Strategic Initiatives Progress** (Slide 14, 4 min)
   - 3–4 major initiatives launched this quarter
   - Status (On Track / At Risk / Completed)

9. **Next Quarter Priorities & Targets** (Slide 15, 3 min)
   - 3–5 key priorities for Q4
   - Quantified targets (revenue goal, customer adds, etc.)

10. **Q&A** (Slide 16, variable time)
    - "Questions?" slide with contact info

**Total Time**: ~40 minutes + Q&A

### Step 2: Executive Summary Slide
First slide after agenda—leaders may read only this.

**Slide Format**
```
Title: "Q3 2024: On Track with Strong Customer Growth"

Key Metrics (highlight row):
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Revenue: $2.4M (Plan $2.3M, +4% vs. Plan)     ✓      │
│ Gross Margin: 48% (Plan 46%, +2 pts)          ✓      │
│ Customer Count: 1,247 (Plan 1,100, +13%)      ✓      │
│ NPS: 72 (Plan 70, +2 pts)                      ✓      │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

Overall Status: 🟢 GREEN (exceeding plan)

Biggest Win: Enterprise segment grew 40% YoY; 3 new $500K+ deals
Biggest Risk: ⚠️ Platform scaling—data growth exceeds infrastructure budget

Next Steps: 3 key priorities for Q4:
  1. Enterprise sales acceleration (3 more enterprise deals)
  2. Platform infrastructure upgrade (prevent outages)
  3. Product roadmap alignment (address top 5 feature requests)
```

**Formatting**
- Green checkmark (✓) if metric beat plan
- Red X if metric missed
- Yellow caution if on-plan but trending wrong

## Quality Gates
- [ ] Executive summary is 1 slide and answers "How are we doing?" in 30 seconds
- [ ] Financial metrics are compared to plan (Actual vs. Plan, not just raw numbers)
- [ ] KPI charts show trend, not just current snapshot
- [ ] Wins are specific, quantified, and show impact (not vague success stories)
- [ ] Losses are addressed transparently with root cause and action (not hidden)
- [ ] Risk register exists and shows mitigation status (not just problem list)
- [ ] Strategic initiatives are tied to business outcomes (not busy work)
- [ ] Q4 priorities are ranked and specific; success criteria are clear
- [ ] Presentation fits 40–45 minutes (respects time constraints)
- [ ] Tone is balanced: celebrating wins while being honest about risks

## Examples

### Good Output (excerpt)
```
EXECUTIVE SUMMARY:
Revenue: $2.4M (Plan $2.3M, +4% ✓)
Margin: 48% (Plan 46%, +2pts ✓)
Customer Count: 1,247 (Plan 1,100, +13% ✓)
Status: 🟢 GREEN (exceeding on all metrics)

Biggest Win: Enterprise segment grew 40% YoY; TechCorp $1.2M deal
Biggest Risk: ⚠️ Platform scaling—addressing with infrastructure upgrade

FINANCIAL: Revenue beat plan due to enterprise outperformance

OPERATIONS: Churn down to 3.5% (from 5%); NPS at 72

RISKS: Infrastructure capacity (mitigation: upgrade in progress by Oct 31)

Q4 FOCUS: Enterprise acceleration (5 deals), infrastructure completion, success coverage

[Each section backed by charts, specific numbers, action items]
```

### Bad Output (what to avoid)
```
"Q3 Review" with no plan comparison (was it good or bad?)
Metrics without context (revenue $2.4M—is that good?)
No wins/losses section; lack of transparency
Risk register without mitigation status (problems but no solutions)
Initiatives without connection to business impact
Vague Q4 priorities ("Improve customer satisfaction" not actionable)
60+ slides (too long; executives can't digest)
No clear status indicators (Green/Amber/Red); confusing
```

## Common Mistakes

1. **Mistake**: Comparing to last quarter instead of plan.
   → **Fix**: Always compare Actual vs. Plan. "We did $2.4M; planned $2.3M; beat by $100K."

2. **Mistake**: Financial results without business context.
   → **Fix**: Explain drivers: "Revenue beat due to 3 enterprise deals closing ahead of schedule."

3. **Mistake**: Hiding losses or presenting as minor.
   → **Fix**: Transparent: "2 customers churned due to outage; saved 2 others with proactive outreach."

4. **Mistake**: Risk register exists but no mitigation status.
   → **Fix**: Every risk should show: description → impact → mitigation → status → owner.

5. **Mistake**: Q4 priorities are too vague to execute.
   → **Fix**: Specific: "Close 5 enterprise deals ($2M+ revenue); success metric is 5 deals booked."

## Anti-Patterns
- Never present metrics without a plan baseline. (Actual vs. Plan is the story, not raw number.)
- Never hide risk; transparency builds trust. (Board appreciates honesty about challenges.)
- Never make Q4 priorities longer than 5 items. (More than 5 = you're unfocused; choose ruthlessly.)
- Never forget to celebrate wins. (Morale matters; teams need to know they're winning.)
- Never skip the risk/issue register. (Leadership needs to know what keeps you up at night.)
- Never present without a clear status indicator (Green/Amber/Red). (Ambiguity = confusion.)
