# Change Announcement

## Purpose
Change creates anxiety. In the vacuum of clear information, people assume the worst. This skill creates an announcement that minimizes that vacuum: crystal clear on what's changing and why, explicit on what's NOT changing (to reduce speculation), and comprehensive FAQ that pre-empts the questions people will ask. The goal is to move quickly from shock to understanding and action.

## When to Use
- Organizational restructures or team changes
- Strategy shifts (market, product, business model)
- Policy or process changes that affect how people work
- Leadership changes or departures
- Budget cuts, hiring freezes, or growth announcements
- Company pivots, acquisitions, significant partnerships
- **Do NOT use when**: The change isn't finalized yet (communicate progress, not rumors); the announcement would be a surprise to the impacted team (socialize first before announcing); you don't have clear answers to FAQ (wait until you do)

## Instructions

### Step 1: Clarify Your Core Message
Before writing, get clear on the one thing people need to understand.

**Bad core messages** (vague, jargon):
- "We're optimizing our organizational structure"
- "We're pivoting to become more customer-centric"
- "We're rightsizing the company"

**Good core messages** (specific, human):
- "We're merging Sales and Customer Success under one leader to reduce handoff friction and improve customer outcomes"
- "We're pausing feature launches for 6 weeks to focus entirely on fixing performance issues our customers are experiencing"
- "We're closing our Australia office by June 30. All Australia-based employees will have the option to work remotely or relocate"

The core message should be specific enough that someone who only reads the first paragraph understands what's happening.

### Step 2: Build the Announcement Structure
Structure matters. People are anxious and will misread. Guide them through a logical sequence.

**Sequence**:
1. **The change** (what's happening, specific, in plain language)
2. **Why** (the reasoning, business context, why now)
3. **What's changing** (specific impacts, who's affected, what they do differently)
4. **What's NOT changing** (this is crucial; people need to know what's stable)
5. **Timeline** (when does this happen, key dates, what's urgent)
6. **Impact on you** (vary this by audience)
7. **FAQ** (pre-empt questions)
8. **How to get more info** (who to talk to, next meeting, office hours)

### Step 3: Communicate the "What" (Specific, Not Jargon)
Start with the actual change in plain language.

**Format**:
"We are [specific action]. This takes effect [date]."

**Examples**:
- "We are merging our Sales and Customer Success teams into one Customer Growth organization, led by [Name]. This takes effect April 1st."
- "We are pausing all new feature development for 6 weeks to focus on performance improvements. This takes effect immediately."
- "We are closing our Australia office by June 30th. All Australia-based team members will have the opportunity to work remotely from Australia or relocate to another office."
- "We are changing our go-to-market strategy from direct sales to self-serve SaaS. This means we won't be hiring additional sales reps and will instead invest in onboarding and support."

Be specific. Name things. Give dates. Avoid "We are exploring" or "We might be evaluating." That creates uncertainty.

If you're not certain about something, say so: "We haven't yet decided how long employees have to relocate. We'll communicate that by March 15th."

### Step 4: Explain the "Why" (Business Logic, Not Spin)
People accept hard changes if they understand the reasoning.

**Good "why" explanations**:
- **Data-driven**: "Our data shows customers are churning due to feature gaps, not sales issues. Reallocating sales to product will address this."
- **Market-driven**: "Enterprise customers are moving away from on-premise solutions. We're shifting to cloud-first to stay competitive."
- **Performance-driven**: "Our platform is slow, and it's costing us customers. We need to stop building features and fix performance for 6 weeks."
- **Sustainability-driven**: "We're not profitable at our current burn rate. We need to reduce costs by 15% or we'll run out of runway in 8 months."

**What not to do**:
- Don't blame people ("Sales team wasn't hitting targets, so we're reorganizing")
- Don't use spin ("We're restructuring to better serve customers" when you really mean "cutting costs")
- Don't be vague ("Optimize our go-to-market" when you mean "cut sales team and go self-serve")

People can handle hard truths better than they can handle spin. Spin breeds cynicism. Honesty builds trust.

**Examples**:
"**The change**: We're merging Sales and Customer Success.

**Why**: Our analysis shows 30% of customers are churning in the first 90 days. The gap isn't in the sales process; it's in onboarding. Sales hands off to CS, and customers fall through the cracks during transition. By merging teams, we remove the handoff and have one person accountable for customer success end-to-end."

"**The change**: We're pausing feature development for 6 weeks.

**Why**: Customers are telling us our product is slow. Our own metrics show a 40% increase in support tickets related to performance. We can't keep shipping features while the foundation is cracking. We need to fix performance now, or we'll lose more customers."

"**The change**: We're closing the Australia office by June 30.

**Why**: We've had limited product-market fit in Australia. Our Australia revenue is $500K (3% of total), but we have 8 people there at a cost of $1.2M annually. We can't sustain a separate office for this level of business. We're reallocating those resources to strengthen our US and EU presence where we have stronger traction."

### Step 5: Detail What IS Changing (Specific Impacts)
People want to know: what does this mean for me?

**By role or team** (if relevant):
- What are they doing differently starting [date]?
- Do they report to someone new?
- Do their responsibilities expand or shrink?
- Do they move locations or change teams?
- Do compensation or benefits change?

**By location** (if location-based change):
- Are they relocating?
- What's the timeline and assistance?
- What happens if they don't relocate?

**By function** (if org change):
- Do you have new cross-functional dependencies?
- Do meetings change?
- Do tools or systems change?

**Examples**:
"**What's changing for Sales team**:
- You'll now report to [Name], VP of Customer Growth (starting April 1)
- Your title changes to "Customer Growth Lead"
- You'll own the customer from first conversation through month 3 (instead of handing off at close)
- Your goals include both new customer acquisition AND 90-day onboarding success
- Compensation adjusts to reflect new responsibilities (details in separate conversation with [Name])
- Team meeting moves from 2x/week to 3x/week (to align with CS)

**What's changing for Customer Success team**:
- You'll report to [Name], VP of Customer Growth (starting April 1)
- Sales reps will be embedded in your onboarding process (you're no longer fully independent)
- You'll have input on pre-sale conversations (to understand customer needs better)
- Team meetings align with Sales (3x/week, timing TBD)
- One CS person per Sales rep (pairing announced tomorrow)"

### Step 6: Explicitly State What is NOT Changing (Critical)
This is where people stop speculating. They need to know what's stable.

**What typically doesn't change but people wonder about**:
- "We're not laying off people (only closing Australia office affects headcount)"
- "We're not changing compensation (except Australia team, see FAQ)"
- "We're not changing our product roadmap (we're fixing performance within current roadmap)"
- "We're not relocating our HQ (US operations stay in San Francisco)"
- "We're not stopping investment in [area people care about]"
- "Your job is not at risk if you're willing to relocate"
- "We're not pivoting away from [core product]"

**Why this matters**: Without clarity on what's NOT changing, people assume the worst. Someone hears "reorganization" and thinks "layoffs." Someone hears "closing one office" and thinks "company is dying." Explicit "what's NOT changing" cuts through speculation.

**Examples**:
"**What is NOT changing**:
- We are not doing a company-wide layoff. Only the Australia office is closing (8 people).
- We are not changing our compensation structure globally. Australia-based salary adjustments only (see FAQ).
- We are not changing the product roadmap. This reorg enables us to execute faster.
- We are not relocating our HQ or any other offices.
- We are not changing our core mission or business model.
- We are not pausing hiring (Sales and CS teams are open; Product is paused for 6 weeks)."

This calms people. Most people aren't directly impacted by the Australia office closure. When you say "what's not changing," they can relax about the things that matter to them.

### Step 7: Communicate Timeline & Key Dates
Uncertainty creates anxiety. Specific dates create action.

**Key dates to communicate**:
- When does the change take effect? (Implementation date)
- When do 1-on-1s happen to discuss impact? (Manager conversations)
- When is the all-hands or town hall? (Company-wide discussion)
- What's the deadline for decisions (relocate, remote, etc.)? (Decision date)
- What's the final date for transitions or last day in old structure? (End date)
- When will you communicate next? (Next update)

**Example timeline**:
"**Timeline**:
- March 15 (today): This announcement
- March 17: [CEO] hosts all-hands. Questions answered live.
- March 18-20: 1-on-1s with managers. You'll hear specifically how this affects you.
- April 1: Change takes effect. New reporting structure, new team composition.
- April 15: Australia team relocation decision deadline. Let us know if you're relocating, remote, or stepping away.
- May 1: Australia office operations wind down.
- June 30: Australia office closed.

Australia-based team members: we're holding a separate all-hands on March 16 (tomorrow, 4 PM Sydney time) to answer Australia-specific questions."

Dates matter. They show you've thought this through and have a plan.

### Step 8: Tailor Impact Statement by Audience
People care about how this affects THEM. You may communicate to multiple audiences.

**For impacted team** (most detail):
- Exactly how does your job change?
- Do you report to someone new? (Name them)
- Do your goals change?
- Does compensation change? (Be specific or say "coming in separate conversation")
- Do benefits change? (Health insurance, vacation, etc.)
- What questions do you have for your manager?

**For adjacent teams** (how it affects collaboration):
- Are the teams you work with changing structure?
- Do handoffs change?
- Do meeting cadences or dependencies change?
- How does this help or affect your work?

**For the whole company** (big picture):
- Why does this matter to us as a company?
- How does this move us toward our mission?
- What's your role in making this successful?

**Examples**:
"**If you're on the Sales team** (see detailed email coming from [Sales Manager]):
- You'll report to [Name] starting April 1
- Your job changes from 'close deals' to 'close + onboard successfully'
- [More in 1-on-1]

**If you're on the CS team** (see detailed email coming from [CS Manager]):
- You'll report to [Name] starting April 1
- You'll partner with Sales reps on onboarding
- [More in 1-on-1]

**If you're on Product, Engineering, or Finance**:
- Sales and CS teams are now one organization
- Your cross-functional partners might change
- This change helps us fix the revenue leak in onboarding, which unlocks growth for you
- No change to your reporting or operations

**For everyone**:
- This change happens because customers are asking for it. We're aligning our org to what customers need.
- Your role is to help your team understand the change and get support in the transition."

### Step 9: Build Comprehensive FAQ
Pre-empt the questions you know people will ask. This is where you address the anxious, angry, or skeptical.

**FAQ structure**:
- **Question (short, conversational)**
- **Answer (direct, specific, honest)**

**Common FAQ categories**:
- **Job security**: "Will I get laid off?" "If I don't relocate, am I fired?" "Is my job safe?"
- **Reporting**: "Who's my manager now?" "When does this change happen?" "Do I interview for a new role?"
- **Compensation**: "Does my salary change?" "What about bonus?" "What about equity?"
- **Logistics**: "Do I have to relocate?" "What assistance do we get?" "What's the deadline?"
- **Operations**: "When does this take effect?" "Do we have a transition plan?" "How long will this be chaotic?"
- **Decisions**: "How was this decided?" "Was my input considered?" "Why didn't we try X instead?"

**Example FAQ**:

"**FAQ**

**Q: Will there be more layoffs after Australia?**
A: Not planned. The Australia office closure was a specific decision about that market. The rest of the organization is stable. That said, like any company, if business conditions change significantly, we'll revisit. We're not hiding anything. You'll hear from us first.

**Q: I'm on the Sales team. Do I have to report to a new manager?**
A: Yes, starting April 1, Sales reports to [Name], VP of Customer Growth (instead of [Current Manager], VP of Sales). [Current Manager] is moving into a different role in operations. You'll get a 1-on-1 with [Name] next week.

**Q: If I'm in Australia and don't want to relocate, am I getting fired?**
A: No. We're offering three options: (1) relocate with assistance, (2) work remotely from Australia, or (3) step away with a severance package (details TBD, but we're being generous). You choose. Decision deadline is April 15.

**Q: How much does relocation assistance cover?**
A: We're covering: (1) moving costs (household goods, car), (2) 30 days hotel in your new city, (3) help finding housing. We're not covering a full relocation cost of living difference. Details in the next email from HR.

**Q: What if I relocate and then the role doesn't work out?**
A: That's a fair risk. If you relocate and then decide this isn't for you, we'll work with you on transition. We're not going to stick you in a job you hate. But give it 6 months before deciding.

**Q: Does this mean we're failing in Australia?**
A: Not failing; we're being realistic. Australia is a smaller market for us (3% revenue). We can't sustain a separate office for 3% of the business. We're reallocating those resources to where we have stronger traction (US, EU). That's not failure; that's strategy.

**Q: Was there any discussion about keeping Australia?**
A: Yes, leadership debated this for 2 months. Options were: (1) keep as is (not sustainable), (2) downsize to 1-2 people remote (limits our presence), (3) close office (most realistic). We chose #3.

**Q: I'm on Product. How does this affect me?**
A: Minimal day-to-day impact. Sales and CS are now one team, so some cross-functional meetings might be different. But your reporting, team, and roadmap don't change. Talk to your manager if you're concerned about changes to your work.

**Q: Why not just do this with a process change instead of a full reorg?**
A: Because the problem is structural. Sales and CS are incentivized differently (Sales optimizes for closing; CS optimizes for retention). The handoff creates misalignment. You can't fix that with better processes; you need one team with one incentive. That requires a reorg.

**Q: I heard we're cutting cost. Is that why we're closing Australia?**
A: Partially. Australia costs $1.2M/year for $500K revenue. That's not sustainable. But we're not taking those savings and dividing them as bonuses. We're reinvesting in US and EU where we have higher growth potential.

**Q: This seems rushed. Did we think about this enough?**
A: Leadership has been analyzing this for months. The Australia decision was discussed thoroughly. What's new to you today might feel fast, but it's been brewing. That said, fair question: what would you like to understand better? Ask in the all-hands."

Make FAQ honest. If a question has a hard answer ("your job is changing a lot"), don't spin it.

### Step 10: Close with Clear Next Steps
End with clarity on what happens next and how people can get help.

**Closing actions**:
- When is the all-hands / discussion? (Attendance expected, time on calendar)
- When will 1-on-1s happen? (Manager will schedule)
- Who to contact with questions? (Name someone, make them available)
- When's the next update? (So people know updates are coming)
- How people can help make this successful? (Optional but good for engagement)

**Example closing**:
"**Next Steps**

- **All-hands**: March 17, 2 PM PT. [CEO] will present and answer questions. Attendance expected.
- **1-on-1s**: March 18-20. Your manager will schedule. Come with questions.
- **Questions before the all-hands?** Email [Name] at [email]. We'll answer pre-meeting questions live.
- **Australia-specific all-hands**: March 16, 4 PM Sydney. We know this is most relevant to you. We'll deep-dive on timelines, relocation support, and options.
- **Next update**: March 25. We'll communicate relocation decisions and transition details.

**How you can help**:
- Share questions and concerns early (helps us address them)
- Be patient with your team as we transition
- Help new people getting to know the changed structure
- Trust that this is best for the company (and for you)"

Clear, organized, actionable.

**Why** (Business Logic):
[The reasoning. Data, market, or performance-driven reasoning. Why now.]

**What IS Changing** (By Role/Function):

- [Role 2]: [Specific impacts]

- [What people worry about but won't change]

## Quality Gates

1. **Is the change crystal clear?**: Could someone who only reads the first paragraph understand what's happening?
2. **Is the "why" honest?**: Would you tell a friend this reason, or are you spinning?
3. **Is what's NOT changing explicit?**: Did you address the things people worry about?
4. **Are impacts specific, not vague?**: "You'll report to X" not "Your role will evolve"?
5. **Is the FAQ comprehensive?**: Are the hardest questions answered?
6. **Are dates specific?**: Or did you say "soon" and "over time"?
7. **Is the tone compassionate?**: Or does it feel corporate and cold?
8. **Could this pre-empt rumors?**: Will people speculate, or will they understand?

## Examples

### Good: Reorganization Announcement
"**Merging Sales & CS Teams**

Starting April 1, we're merging Sales and Customer Success into one Customer Growth organization led by [Name]. Sales and CS have been handing customers off to each other, and it's creating friction. We're fixing that by making one team accountable for customer success end-to-end.

**What's changing**:
- Sales team: You now own customer success from first meeting through month 3
- CS team: You now partner with Sales reps on onboarding (less independent)
- Reporting: Both teams report to [Name], VP Customer Growth

**What's NOT changing**:
- We're not cutting anyone. Same team, one org.
- Product roadmap stays the same.
- Compensation changes for Sales only (new role, new pay; conversation with [Name])

**Timeline**:
- March 17: All-hands
- March 18-20: 1-on-1s with managers
- April 1: New structure takes effect

**FAQ**:
- Q: Do I have to report to a new manager?
  A: If you're on Sales, yes. If you're on CS, yes. If you're on another team, no.
- Q: Will my compensation change?
  A: Sales only—conversation with [Name]. CS compensation unchanged.

**Questions?** [Manager name] or [Email address]"

### Good: Office Closure Announcement
"**Closing Australia Office by June 30**

We're closing our Australia office as of June 30. Australia is 3% of our revenue and costs $1.2M/year to operate. We can't sustain that financially. We're reallocating those resources to US and EU markets where we have higher traction.

**What's changing**:
- If you're based in Australia: You have three options: (1) relocate with full assistance, (2) work remotely from Australia, (3) step away with severance
- All other employees: No change to your operations

**What's NOT changing**:
- No company-wide layoffs beyond the Australia office
- Product roadmap is unchanged
- We're still hiring in US/EU
- Compensation structure is stable globally (Australia salary adjustments only)

**Timeline**:
- March 15: This announcement
- March 16: Australia-specific all-hands (details, questions)
- April 15: Australia team decisions due (relocate / remote / step away)
- June 30: Office closes

**FAQ**:
- Q: If I'm in Australia and don't relocate, do I get fired?
  A: No. You can work remotely. Or take a severance package if you prefer to step away.
- Q: What relocation assistance do you offer?
  A: Moving costs, 30 days hotel, housing help. Details in follow-up email.

**Australia team**: We'll answer detailed questions in tomorrow's all-hands. We're being generous here and want to support you however you choose."

### Bad: Vague, Spin, Missing What's NOT Changing
"We're optimizing our organizational structure to better align with our strategic vision and create a more customer-centric organization. Some role changes may occur. We'll share more details soon. Thanks for your patience during this transition."

Vague ("optimizing," "customer-centric"). Missing specifics. No timeline. No FAQ. People speculate. Creates the worst rumors.

### Bad: Buries the Change
"We're excited to announce a new chapter in our company's journey. As we scale, we're learning what works and what doesn't. After careful consideration, we've decided to realign a few teams to better serve our customers. More details coming."

Hasn't said what the change IS. Reading between the lines, people assume layoffs or worse.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Burying the change in corporate language**: "Organizational optimization," "strategic realignment," "rightsizing." Just say it: "We're closing the Australia office" or "We're laying off 10% of the team."

2. **Missing "what's NOT changing"**: You announce a change and people assume everything is different. Be explicit about what's stable. Most people aren't directly impacted; tell them that.

3. **Weak FAQ**: You ask "Any questions?" but don't pre-empt the hard ones. FAQ should include: job security, compensation, reporting, timeline, decisions made.

4. **Too much spin**: "This is an opportunity for growth" when you really mean "We're cutting cost" or "We're exiting a market." People smell spin and lose trust.

5. **No clear timeline**: "This will happen over the coming months." People interpret that as "chaos for 6 months." Give specific dates.

6. **Forgetting to say why**: "We're restructuring" without explaining why. People need the reasoning to accept it.

7. **No follow-up**: You announce the change. Then silence. People fill the vacuum with rumors. Follow up with 1-on-1s, FAQs, all-hands discussion.

## Anti-Patterns

1. **Announcing before leadership agreement**: You announce a change, someone in leadership didn't agree, they contradict you. Lose credibility. Make sure leadership is aligned before announcing.

2. **Surprising the affected team**: They hear about the change from company all-hands, not from their manager. They feel blindsided. Brief affected teams 24 hours before announcing.

3. **Making it personal**: "This person is terrible, so we're replacing them" shows on the affected person. Keep it business, not personal.

4. **Hiding the hard impact**: "No one's job is at risk" when actually 20 people are getting fired. People find out from email and feel betrayed. Be honest about who's impacted and when.

5. **No process to appeal or discuss**: You announce a decision that affects people and there's no way for them to give input or express concerns. Always have a channel (office hours, Q&A, 1-on-1s) to discuss.
